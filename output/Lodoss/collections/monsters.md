# Monster Collection 怪物集（评注版）

> 来源：Record of Lodoss War Companion I（1989, Group SNE）PDF p.77–82
> 译本：英文同人翻译版

Monsters that attack characters may seem merely hateful when they're just numbers. However, if you know their appearance, personality, and habits, you might come to feel a fondness for them. Even if you think there's no help for it if you get eaten despite feeling fond of them, knowing your enemy might allow you to exploit their weaknesses and defeat them easily.

> **本章性质**：这里收录每只怪物的「编辑评注」——外观、习性、战术心得。完整的数据块（LP/MP/IV/AV/Damage 等）见 `rules/06-monsters.md`。

## Beasts 野兽

### Wolf 狼

They hunt in packs for prey, making them troublesome for people walking outside at night or camping outdoors. They often attack livestock, so they're despised in farming villages. Originally, they're creatures that should be part of nature, and aside from winter, when food is scarce, they don't come down to human settlements much. However, occasionally they're kept by goblins and act alongside them. They're weak against magic, so finish them off all at once with area-effect spells.

### Giant Bat 巨蝙蝠

Many people dislike bats because of their creepy appearance, but in reality they are not particularly harmful. However, in the fantasy world, there are vicious ones that live up to their looks. That's this Giant Bat. With its wings spread, it's just under a meter in size, clinging to animals' bodies and biting with sharp fangs to suck blood. It possesses the bat's characteristic ultrasonic radar, making it a troublesome creature that flies around freely even in darkness.

### Snake 蛇

Whether in the East or the West, snakes are despised. Here in Lodoss, there is no exception. These aren't the kind of monsters that appear in legends, with body lengths of only about a meter. However, their fangs carry powerful venom, and if you take them lightly, you'll pay for it.

### Spider 蜘蛛

A giant spider. It looks like an extra-large orb weaver or garden spider, but its behavior is more akin to a hunting spider. It sprays silk to immobilize its prey's legs, paralyzes them with poisonous fangs, and bites to suck their bodily fluids. *Apparently this is the national symbol of Alania, which shows pretty poor taste.*

### Giant Ant 巨蚁

Just regular ants scaled up. Their shells are tough, and they're the poster child for "unrewarding" monsters — nothing good comes from beating them. And they come in numbers too. This is definitely a situation where you want to use some strategy to drive them away. Fortunately, they're not smart. Of course, being animals (oops, insects) that live by instinct, psychological traps are completely ineffective. You need something physical to work.

### Bear 熊

A huge forest bear. Omnivorous, it appears near settlements when food is scarce. Indifferent to humans, it raids campers' provisions. Won't attack unless its territory is invaded — except mothers with cubs, who attack anyone. Once it tastes human flesh, it targets humans exclusively. *You might get a request to hunt a man-eating bear.*

### Rat 巨鼠

A giant rat. Since it's being introduced as a monster, it's naturally far tougher than an ordinary rat, but still, a rat is a rat — one alone doesn't amount to much of a fighting force. Like actual rats, these giant rats also live in filthy places in swarms. Usually cowardly and quick to flee when danger approaches, but once cornered, they'll fiercely counterattack, biting adventurers just as they would cats.

### Slime 史莱姆

A mysterious gelatinous creature. There are various types, but they're all sticky and slimy. They're nasty creatures that secrete acid and assimilate whatever they touch. Since they move slowly, they often cling to ceilings and drop down to attack prey that passes below.

## Humanoids 人形

### Goblin 哥布林

Fantasy's quintessential cannon fodder. Originally they came from the Fairy Realm, same as elves and dwarves, but the whole race sold their souls to the dark gods and happily became Youma. Usually, they just charge at you without any finesse, but there are Goblin Shamans among them who use shaman magic, so if you carelessly attack, you might get hit with a terrible counterattack.

### Kobold 狗头人

The ultimate punching bag in RPGs. If the numbers are even, any random farmer could beat them, but they always come in groups, so naturally, it becomes a job for adventurers. That said, if you lose to these guys, you have no business going on adventures. They especially have no resistance to magic, so if you hit them with area-effect magic, they're as good as wiped out.

### Minotaur 牛头人

A giant with the head of a bull. The one living in the labyrinth of the Valis delta is famous (have you defeated it yet?). A carnivorous and ferocious race that sometimes appears near human settlements and causes trouble. There's even a theory that they particularly favor human flesh. They are technically intelligent beings, but their heads are quite dim. There are no females in their species, so they use females of other species to reproduce. Combining pleasure with necessity, they sometimes demand sacrifices from nearby villages. If you defeat one, you'll certainly be thanked, but they're quite strong.

## Spirits & Fae 精灵与妖精

### Will-o'-Wisp 鬼火

In the real world, they're ghostly lights floating over swamps at night; in Lodoss, they're spirits of light. They're summoned by shaman magic. Deedlit used these frequently as well. Their combat power is negligible, but when they touch something, they release powerful energy. The shock from that is nothing to scoff at.

### Sylph 风精

A wind spirit favored by Deedlit. While it doesn't have much attack power on its own, it displays a variety of tricks such as deflecting incoming arrows and silencing sounds. Spirits are originally part of nature and shouldn't attack adventurers who are on the side of justice, but since they cannot distinguish between good and evil, they may be used as minions by enemy shamans, or thoughtless people may anger them, forcing you to fight them against your will. Since spirits have no physical form, they cannot be harmed by ordinary weapons. If you don't have a magic weapon, don't forget to cast the **Enchant Weapon** spell.

### Shade 暗影

A dark spirit that emerges from the shadows. And a spirit of terror as well. It wraps everything around it in darkness, and its true form is never seen. Like Will-o'-Wisp, it emits energy. Wisps and Shades are natural enemies — when they touch each other, they both vanish without a trace.

### Gremlin 小妖精

A malicious fairy. Characterized by leathery wings. They're not much of an opponent, but they cast shaman magic that affects the mind. There's also a theory that they bring misfortune.

### Dryad 树精

A spirit that dwells in forest trees, taking the form of a beautiful maiden. Famous for possessing powerful enchantment abilities, it is said that when she sees a handsome man, she seduces him and drags him into the tree. Though normally, a Dryad's mischief doesn't cause any real harm. *Occasionally, there are those who can't forget the days spent with a Dryad and become socially maladjusted...*

However, she shows no mercy to those who harm the forest. Though physically weak, she uses magic to drive away intruders. Even if you happen to defeat a Dryad, you'll be treated as the villain, so your only option is to honestly apologize and retreat. *Women really are hard to deal with.*

## Constructs 构造体

### Wood Golem 木魔像

A wooden doll brought to life by magic. A golem is a type of artificial human created by magic, and since they have no heart, magic that affects the mind doesn't work on them. They obey their master's commands absolutely, so during the ancient kingdom period, they were widely used as treasure guardians or sorcerer bodyguards, but nowadays, the method of making them has been lost. Normal golems can't be harmed except by magical weapons, but wood golems are the **budget version** among golems, with flaws such as being able to be defeated by ordinary weapons. Well, they're mid-level enemies.

### Flesh Golem 肉魔像

A golem made by stitching together human bodies (well, any humanoid will do). You could say it's the fantasy version of Frankenstein's monster. It's got everything going for it — nonmagical weapons don't work on it, and since it was originally a living thing, it naturally heals from injuries. But because the manufacturing process is so specialized, you rarely see them. Rumor has it they're guarding pyramids (those who played the computer version know what I'm talking about).

### Dragon Tooth Warrior 龙牙战士

At first glance, a heavily armed skeleton. But don't be fooled by appearances. This is a powerful warrior born from an enchanted dragon's fang. Though it has no head, its skill far surpasses that of an ordinary swordsman, and spells that affect the mind have no effect whatsoever — quite formidable indeed. High-ranking mages often use them as guards. Karla used them too, didn't she?

They're not undead monsters, but are created like golems, so **Turn Undead** spells won't work on them. Be careful.

## Undead 不死生物

### Skeleton 骷髅

A punching bag of the monster world alongside kobolds. A type of undead monster created by ancient magic, it can be easily made if you have a complete skeleton. Weak in strength and weak in mind. In particular, these guys have the critical weakness of being crushed by a Priest's magic. They only become a threat when they appear in numbers. If you lose to these guys, it's a bit embarrassing as an adventurer.

### Zombie 僵尸

Like skeletons, these are animated corpses given temporary life through magic. They're also cannon fodder, but are tougher than skeletons thanks to having flesh. However, they share the skeleton's weakness to **Turn Undead** spells. They move more slowly than skeletons and are dimmer, but they're strong, so they're used for labor as well as combat. The downside is that they don't keep well since they're organic matter.

### Ghoul 食尸鬼

Also called corpse-eaters, these are monsters that devour dead bodies and often appear in graveyards. They're part of the undead monster family, but unlike zombies and skeletons, they're a higher class of being that moves by its own will. If the former two are golems using negative life force, these are more like people possessing negative life force. They have a constitution that only allows them to eat the flesh of their own kind, so they search graveyards for corpses. However, they don't only eat corpses — they also attack living humans. Perhaps fresh meat is better after all. *Spark is surely possessed by one of these.*

### Wight 伐特

An undead with lead-colored skin wrapped in yellow light throughout its body. Having no true life, it survives by absorbing the life force of other living beings. **Level drain is frightening**, so if you spot one, have a priest turn it.

### Wraith 怨灵

A wraith is a mass of resentment that has gained negative life force and remained in this world. Like wights, they sustain themselves by devouring other life.

## Werecreatures 兽人

### Werewolf 狼人

A monster that transforms between human and wolf form every time the full moon comes, due to some curse. When people think of werewolves these days, many consider them something cool because of novels and movies, but in Lodoss, they're terrible monsters with a wolf's heart implanted with human madness, and most transform regardless of their own will. Only **silver or magical weapons** work against these creatures, so be very careful.

### Werebear 熊人

This time it's not a wolf but a bearman. This one also rampages on nights of the full moon. Since it transforms into a large and strong species of bear, it's a real handful. Just as a bear is stronger than a wolf one-on-one, this is a tougher opponent than a werewolf. Again, only **silver or magical weapons** will work, so be careful.

### Wererat 鼠人

A monster that transforms into a giant rat night after night to commit evil deeds. Unlike other werecreatures, it's said to retain its intelligence even after transforming, making it quite cunning. They usually make their home in city sewers and can even summon giant rats to use as minions. They're not particularly strong opponents, but again, only **silver or magical weapons** work against them, and since they're clever, they'll set various traps, making them quite a nuisance. Don't let your guard down.

## Dark Servants 暗黑仆从

### Dark Priest 暗黑神官

Dark Priests are evil clerics who serve the dark gods like Phalaris. Dark gods aren't necessarily evil, but apparently, any dark god's cleric who isn't evil gets kicked down by their rivals and can't advance in the ranks. They're formidable enemies who use priest magic. You'll have to trust in divine protection and fight. *There's really no other option.*

### Dark Elf 暗黑精灵

Elves who have sold their souls to the dark gods — the evil kind of elf. They're terrible opponents — they unleash shaman magic and dark spells left and right, shower you with arrows, and they're resistant to your magic. They were quite formidable enemies in the computer version, but in the replay sessions, Spark and the others gave them a pretty hard time.

### Dark Magician 暗黑魔术师

Wizards who practice evil black magic. The famous Master Wagnard holds this position. Since they've succumbed to desire and delved into forbidden magic, they have terrible personalities and usually bring along a ton of bodyguards, making them difficult to defeat. However, while their magic is powerful, they're weak in melee combat, so they become much weaker when their magic is sealed. If you cast a **Silence** spell on them, they shouldn't be much of a problem.

### Dark Lord 暗黑骑士

Evil elite warriors who have pledged their souls to the dark gods. They're the mortal enemies of Spark and Parn. You'd think evil knights would be disgraces to knighthood, but in reality, many of them are cool like Ashram. **The attractive villain archetype probably originates from around here.**
