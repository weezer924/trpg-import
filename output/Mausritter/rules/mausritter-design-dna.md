## Mechanical lineage

Games and blogs Isaac Williams drew from:

| Ancestor | What Mausritter inherits |
| -------- | ------------------------ |
| **Into the Odd** (Chris McDowall) | Most of core rules — auto-hit attacks, HP as avoidance pool, saves as roll-under stat, critical damage on STR rolls |
| **Goblin Punch** (Arnold Kemp) | Magic system — dice-investment casting, [DICE] / [SUM] spell effects, 3× usage dots |
| **Knave** (Ben Milton) | Characters defined by **inventories** rather than classes; slot-based encumbrance |
| **Mothership** (Tuesday Knight Games) | Densely-packed page layouts, usable-at-the-table procedural text |
| **Moonhop** (David Schirduan) | Permission to "just make your game" — embrace hacking and kit-bashing |
| **Last Gasp Grimoire** (Coins & Scrolls adjacent) | Arts-and-crafts inventory visuals — cards, tokens, slot cards |
| **Coins and Scrolls** (Skerples) | Re-introduction to old-school role-playing — orthodox OSR procedural thinking |

## Tonal / setting lineage

Theme, setting, and tone drawn from:

| Source | What it contributes |
| ------ | ------------------- |
| **Brambly Hedge** (book series, Jill Barklem) | Cozy, detailed mouse villages in trees and burrows; seasonal rituals; craft-and-comfort aesthetic |
| **Church Mice** (book series, Graham Oakley) | Humble churchgoing mice navigating a human world; humor and small-scale drama |
| **The Tales** (Beatrix Potter) | Anthropomorphic small animals, simple dangerous stakes, moral consequence |
| **The Rescuers** (Margery Sharp) | Small brave mice organizing for adventure; secret societies of mice |
| **Arrietty** (Studio Ghibli) | Scale — the sense of a huge world where a sewing needle is a sword and a cat is a terror |
| **A Distant Mirror** (Barbara Tuchman) | Medieval detail for the mouse kingdoms — feudal politics, noblemouse caste, plague, warband warfare |
| **Mouse Guard** (comic, David Petersen) | Brave mouse knights on patrol; seasonal duties; predators as existential threats |
| **Mice and Mystics** (boardgame, Jerry Hawthorne) | Cooperative mouse adventure party dynamics |

## Notes for AI GM

When you need to make a ruling or fill a gap, triangulate against the lineage:

- **A combat edge case?** → check what **Into the Odd** would do. Attacks auto-hit; damage is visible; HP is the "don't get seriously hurt" buffer, not the meat layer.
- **A magic homebrew?** → check **Goblin Punch**'s spell-as-creature framing. Spells are entities, not verbs. They want specific things (to be burned, buried, given away). The 4-5-6 usage mark is a bargain with the spirit, not a tax.
- **A dungeon page layout / adventure site?** → check **Mothership**'s "one-page dense" style. Every page must be usable mid-play, not read cover-to-cover.
- **A settlement or domestic scene?** → **Brambly Hedge** vibes. Detailed cozy clutter — flour mill, herb garden, honey storage, bunting, mouse-scale furniture. Warmth broken by the occasional reminder that a cat lord is nearby.
- **A seasonal or political beat?** → **A Distant Mirror** + **Mouse Guard**. Noblemice tithe from settlements; seasonal duties (midsummer feast, harvest, winter bonfire); warband warfare is slow, logistics-bound.
- **A predator encounter?** → **Arrietty** scale first. Describe the predator by its shadow, its breath, the tremor of its weight on the floorboards, before anything else.
- **A PC death?** → **Mice and Mystics** + OSR tradition. Don't eulogize. Roll a new mouse. The next one might be braver, or dumber, or both.

**Never** import mechanics from D&D/Pathfinder lineage (XP-for-monsters, hit dice scaling, class abilities). Mausritter is the Into-the-Odd branch of the OSR tree, not the TSR branch.
