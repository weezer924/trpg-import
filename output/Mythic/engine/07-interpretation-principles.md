# Interpretation Principles

> Distilled heuristics for turning Mythic's mechanical outputs into coherent narrative. Load alongside engine/ at session start. Reference when interpretation feels stale, forced, or stuck.

## How To Use

Principles are numbered P-01, P-02, … Use them as a checklist when a roll result feels ambiguous or forced. Each principle cites its source in `rules/` or `examples/` so you can audit its derivation. Apply them as a flexible toolkit, not a rigid procedure.

---

## P-01 Accept the answer; reverse-engineer the cause

When Mythic produces a Fate Answer, treat it as truth and find the in-fiction reason. Never re-roll for a "better" outcome. The roll is the new Context.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §To Answer Your Question; examples/01-henny-in-z-land.md turn 4 (Exceptional No on locked door → not just unlocked, but house is intact and stocked).

## P-02 Choose Odds from in-fiction Context, not from narrative preference

Odds reflect what the situation suggests, not what you want to happen. Count the reasons toward Yes vs No that the PC could plausibly observe; that's where the Odds sit.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Choosing Odds; examples/01-henny-in-z-land.md turn 1 (Very Unlikely chosen because deep in forest, not because Player wanted "no zombies").

## P-03 50/50 is the legitimate fallback

If you genuinely don't know what the Odds should be, pick 50/50 and roll. Don't stall trying to compute "real" Odds.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Choosing Odds + sidebar (p.33); examples/01-henny-in-z-land.md turn 4 (locked door — "no idea" → 50/50).

## P-04 Exceptional Yes intensifies the Yes

Don't just say "Yes." Push the answer to the next logical level — louder, bigger, more fully.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Exceptional Yes; examples/04-the-big-example.md scene 2 (Exceptional Yes on "Is this a warlord?" → cosmic dimension-ruler, not just warlord).

## P-05 Exceptional No flips to opposite, or intensifies the next-most-expected

Two paths: opposite of the Yes (door not just unlocked but partially open), OR the next-most-expected outcome made stronger. Pick whichever fits Context.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Exceptional No; examples/03-guardian-of-chosen-one.md scene 4 (Exceptional No on "spot a cave" → opposite, sign of life — column of smoke).

## P-06 Run with the first interpretation that fits

When a word pair, Focus, or answer lands cleanly, commit. Don't grind for the perfect interpretation when a workable one is available.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Trust Yourself sidebar; examples/02-wutwo-labs.md event 3 (Fight + Outside snapped together, no further work needed).

## P-07 Stretch is allowed if the words are still recognizable

When Meaning words don't fit literally, abstract them. The result must still echo the words; it doesn't have to be their dictionary definition.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §You Need More Information; examples/02-wutwo-labs.md event 2 (Frantic + Ambush → slow-motion frantic ambush — explicitly noted as a stretch that's fine).

## P-08 Treat Focus + Meaning as one synthesis, not separate constraints

Don't tick the Focus box, then tick the Meaning box. Combine all three (Context + Focus + Meaning) into a single interpretation.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Generating Random Events + §Interpreting The Event; examples/02-wutwo-labs.md event 1 (NPC Negative + spiders + Betray + Weapon → spiders fighting each other, callback to weapons program).

## P-09 Use Current Context as Focus when something's already undefined

If the Random Event lands while an NPC's behavior, an unseen room, or a sound from elsewhere is unresolved, use Current Context to let the Event answer that pending question.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Current Context; examples/02-wutwo-labs.md event 2 (moss-man's behavior was undefined — Current Context Focus settled it).

## P-10 Empty list rolls fall back to Current Context

When the rolled Focus calls for a Thread or NPC and the relevant List is empty, treat Current Context as the Focus instead.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Empty List sidebar; examples/04-the-big-example.md scene 1 (First Scene generation: NPC Action with empty Characters List → went to Meaning Tables directly).

## P-11 Blank-line list rolls are a choose-or-reroll opportunity

When the row roll lands on an empty line in a populated section, choose the most fitting element OR reroll. The choice is when you nudge the narrative deliberately.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §What's The Number For sidebar; examples/04-the-big-example.md scene 2 (blank line on Characters → Player chose Captured Alien because it was the most loaded with potential).

## P-12 Apply the Yes/No first, then layer the Random Event

A Fate Question that triggers a Random Event still produces an answer. Resolve the Yes/No in fiction, then add the Event on top of that resolution.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Random Events; examples/01-henny-in-z-land.md turn 2 (Yes on "find anything useful" + Random Event → both apply: useful find AND it's at an abandoned camp).

## P-13 Cap your Fate Question chains at two per detail

If the answer doesn't give you enough on the first ask, ask a second clarifying Question. Don't ask a third — commit to interpretation from there.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Give Me More!; rules/04-scenes.md §The Many Questions Pitfall.

## P-14 Phrase Questions action-oriented with Yes = the more interesting outcome

"Are there zombies outside?" not "Is it all clear?" The Chaos Factor's bias toward Yes-as-action makes this matter for tone.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Action Oriented sidebar.

## P-15 Don't ask Questions outside PC scope

Stay within what the PC could plausibly know or sense. Avoid Questions that exist only because *you* know something the Character doesn't.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Guided By Expectations sidebar; rules/05-variations.md §Resolving Character Vs Player Knowledge.

## P-16 Tension-building Questions over narrative-killing ones

Ask hard Questions whose answers reshape the story. Avoid Questions whose No would simply end a plotline with no payoff.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §Tension-Building Questions + §Narrative-Killing Questions sidebar.

## P-17 Defer to chosen-RPG mechanics for tasks the RPG already handles

Combat hits, skill rolls, social skills — let the chosen RPG resolve them. Use Fate Questions to fill gaps the RPG doesn't cover.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Using Fate Questions To Replace RPG Rules; examples/04-the-big-example.md scene 2 (Persuasion Task Roll instead of Fate Question for "Do they believe Ogberd?").

## P-18 When replacing an RPG rule, treat CF as 5 and Exceptional as regular

For task-resolution Questions standing in for a chosen RPG rule, ignore the current CF (use 5) and treat Exceptional results as regular Yes/No unless the rule has degrees of success.
> Source: rules/02-fate-questions.md §Chaos, Events, And Exceptional Answers.

## P-19 Try connectors when a word pair won't sing

Insert *of*, *-ly*, *and*, *but* between the words to change their relationship. One reading usually clicks.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §Sidebar: Meaning Word Connectors.

## P-20 Mining for Meaning beats giving up

If the first roll won't interpret, roll again on a different Meaning Table for an extra word pair. Layered prompts often resolve what one couldn't.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Mining For Meaning; examples/04-the-big-example.md scene 1 (multiple Names rolls combined into "Ninfee Wryrock", "Tallelrun", "Lennin Pertar", "Orta Leet").

## P-21 The "I Dunno" Rule is a release valve, not a default

If after clarifying Question + connectors + extra Meaning roll the Event still won't interpret, drop it and continue. Use sparingly — distinguish "stuck" from "haven't thought about it yet."
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §The "I Dunno" Rule; examples/02-wutwo-labs.md sidebar (introduced as the explicit fallback).

## P-22 Doubled-down Element rolls intensify rather than just repeat

Rolling the same word twice on an Elements table means push the concept further: not just "Open" but radically open.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Doubling Down sidebar.

## P-23 Every detail you record becomes Context for later

A Yes here, a Meaning roll there — they all stack into the Context that shapes future Odds and interpretations. Treat each detail as load-bearing for the rest of the session.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Context; examples/04-the-big-example.md scene 1 sidebar (Rapid working downtown, leaving without notice — small details, big later impact).

## P-24 First Scene is exempt from CF testing

The First Scene happens as conceived. The d10-vs-CF test starts with Scene 2.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §The First Scene; examples/03-guardian-of-chosen-one.md Scene 1 (no test, established baseline).

## P-25 d10 vs CF: odd within → Altered, even within → Interrupt

Don't pick by mood; the parity of the roll decides. Altered = same Scene, one shift. Interrupt = generated as a Random Event.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §Testing The Expected Scene; examples/03-guardian-of-chosen-one.md (3 = Altered; 4 = Interrupt; 8 = Expected).

## P-26 An Altered Scene uses the next-most-plausible expectation, not random invention

When the Expected Scene is denied, pick the *next* thing you'd expect — not something out of left field. Other Altered methods (Tweak / Fate Question / Meaning / Scene Adjustment) are alternatives only when the next expectation isn't obvious.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §The Next Expectation; examples/03-guardian-of-chosen-one.md Scene 3 (Altered: heading to village → instead, orc warband catches up at camp).

## P-27 Interrupt Scenes are generated as Random Events but still tied to Context

Roll Focus + Meaning, but interpret with the prior Scene's situation in mind. The Interrupt is a derailment, not a teleport to an unrelated story.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §Interrupt Scenes + §Making Connections; examples/04-the-big-example.md Scene 2 (Interrupt about Captured Alien who was already on the List from Scene 1 — Scene 2 picks up the loose end).

## P-28 CF transition reflects PC control, not Scene success/failure

Did the PC drive the Scene, or react to it? Surviving an ambush by an unplanned magical reveal is "out of control" even if no one died.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §Adjust The Chaos Factor; examples/03-guardian-of-chosen-one.md Scene 3 (CF +1 because Ninfee's display was unplanned, even though they survived).

## P-29 Threads and Characters cap at three instances

Once an element appears 3 times on a List, no more entries. The cap is the only mechanism that prevents one element from monopolizing random rolls.
> Source: rules/04-scenes.md §Add List Elements; examples/03-guardian-of-chosen-one.md (Deliver, Chosen One, Avoid orcs all hit cap at different scenes).

## P-30 Interpretation is more art than science — trust the gut

When in doubt: pick the reading that excites you most. Mythic's prompts are starting points, not constraints.
> Source: rules/03-random-events.md §Trust Yourself sidebar; rules/04-scenes.md §Sidebar: A Literal Problem.
