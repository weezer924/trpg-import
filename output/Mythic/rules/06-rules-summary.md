## Asking Fate Questions

Use Fate Questions to learn new details about your adventure.

- **FOLLOW YOUR EXPECTATIONS**: Fill in details about your adventure following your expectations.
- **TEST EXPECTATIONS**: If you think an expectation should be tested because it is important or uncertain, then ask a Fate Question.
- **FATE CHART OR FATE CHECK**: Test the expectation using a Yes/No Question resolved either using the Fate Chart or a Fate Check.
- **RANDOM EVENT**: If you rolled a double-digit number (11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, or 99) and the single-digit value (1-9) is equal to or less than the current Chaos Factor, then a Random Event happens in addition to the answer.
- **INTERPRET THE RESULTS**.

---

## Fate Chart

- **FORM A QUESTION**: Ask a Yes/No question.
- **ASSIGN ODDS**: Give the Odds of the Question being a Yes: 50/50, Likely, Very Likely, Nearly Certain, Certain, Unlikely, Very Unlikely, Nearly Impossible, or Impossible.
- **CHECK THE FATE CHART**: Look up your Odds in the column corresponding to the current Chaos Factor.
- **ROLL 1D100**:
  - If the roll is **equal to or less than the large number** found on the Fate Chart, the answer is **Yes**.
  - If it's **equal to or less than the smaller number to the left**, it is an **Exceptional Yes**.
  - If it's **greater than the large number**, the answer is **No**.
  - If it's **equal to or greater than the smaller number to the right**, it is an **Exceptional No**.
- **RANDOM EVENT**: Same double-digit rule as above.
- **INTERPRET THE RESULTS**.

(Full Fate Chart in `../tables/fate-chart.md`.)

## Fate Check

- **FORM A QUESTION**: Ask a Yes/No question.
- **ASSIGN ODDS**: As above.
- **GET MODIFIERS**: Determine modifiers for Odds and Chaos Factor; add them together.
- **ROLL 2D10**: Add 2d10 together and apply modifiers.
  - **18-20**: Exceptional Yes.
  - **11 or more**: Yes.
  - **10 or less**: No.
  - **2-4**: Exceptional No.
- **RANDOM EVENT**: If you rolled a double-digit number (11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, or 99) and the single-digit value (1-9) is equal to or less than the current Chaos Factor, a Random Event happens in addition to the answer.
- **INTERPRET THE RESULTS**.

(Modifiers table in `../tables/fate-check-modifiers.md`.)

## Fate Questions As RPG Rules

Use Mythic Fate Questions to replace rules in your chosen RPG.

- **ASK FATE QUESTION**: Pose the rule as a Mythic Yes/No Fate Question. Resolve as normal.
- **CHAOS FACTOR**: Treat as **5**, regardless of the actual current value.
- **EXCEPTIONAL RESULTS**: Treat Exceptional Yes/No as a regular Yes/No if the rule being replaced doesn't use degrees of success or failure.
- **RANDOM EVENTS**: Ignore Random Events unless they make sense to include with the replaced rule.

## Generating Random Events

Random Events can happen when rolling a Fate Question or when you generate an Interrupt Scene.

- **CONSIDER CONTEXT**: The Random Event is happening in relation to the Context of your adventure, which helps guide your interpretation.
- **DETERMINE EVENT FOCUS**: Roll 1d100 on the Random Event Focus Table to discover what the event is about. Alternatively, you can choose the Event Focus instead of rolling.
- **DETERMINE EVENT MEANING**: Choose a Meaning Table that fits the event best and roll for two inspirational words. Use them to help interpret.
- **INTERPRET**: Combine adventure Context, Event Focus, and Meaning words into an interpretation.

(Random Event Focus Table in `../tables/random-event-focus.md`.)

---

## Rolling On Lists

Random Events sometimes require you to roll on the Threads List or Characters List.

- **DETERMINE HOW MANY SECTIONS ARE ACTIVE**: A section with at least one element is active. The active section furthest down the List determines the first die: nothing, d4, d6, d8, or d10.
- **ROLL**: If necessary, roll the section die. Then roll 1d10 to determine the element in that section.
- **CHOOSE**: If you roll an empty line, you may either choose any element from anywhere on the List or roll again.

## First Scene

Choose a method to develop the first Scene:

- **INSPIRED IDEA**: Come up with an idea rather than determining randomly.
- **RANDOM EVENT**: Generate a Random Event to form the start of the Scene.
- **MEANING TABLES**: Choose Meaning Tables and roll as many word pairs as needed for inspiration.
- **4W**: Roll on Meaning Tables in a structured way for the Who, What, Where, and Why:
  - **Who**: Characters Elements Meaning Table.
  - **What**: Actions Meaning Tables.
  - **Where**: Locations Elements Meaning Table.
  - **Why**: Actions Meaning Tables.

## Testing Scenes

- **EXPECTED SCENE**: Decide how you think the next Scene will begin.
- **ROLL 1D10**: Test the Expected Scene.
  - Roll Chaos Factor or less, **odd** (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) → **Altered Scene**.
  - Roll Chaos Factor or less, **even** (2, 4, 6, 8) → **Interrupt Scene**.
  - Roll greater than Chaos Factor → Expected Scene plays as imagined.

---

## Altered Scenes

Generate an Altered Scene by modifying your Expected Scene. Use one strategy:

- **THE NEXT EXPECTATION**: Default. The Scene is what you would *next* most expect.
- **A TWEAK**: Change one element in the Expected Scene.
- **FATE QUESTION**: Ask a Fate Question to choose how the Scene changes.
- **MEANING TABLES**: Roll a word pair for inspiration.
- **SCENE ADJUSTMENT TABLE**: Roll 1d10 on the Scene Adjustment Table (`../tables/scene-adjustment.md`).

---

## Interrupt Scenes

- **RANDOM EVENT**: Generate a Random Event and use it as the basis for the start of the Scene.

---

## Begin And End Scenes

Choose a method for deciding when a Scene begins and ends:

- **INTEREST**: Default. A Scene starts with something interesting and ends when that interest is concluded.
- **TIME OR LOCATION**: Scenes end when there is a change in time or location.
- **NARRATIVE SHIFT**: A Scene ends when there is a shift in the narrative; the next Scene picks up with that change.
- **MOOD**: Scenes end when you feel they have run out of steam.
- **AUTOMATIC INTERRUPT**: Choose to have a Scene begin as an Interrupt without testing against the Chaos Factor.

## Generating NPC Behavior

- **EXPECTATIONS**: If you have a clear idea what the NPC will do or say, and the action isn't crucial to the adventure, then follow your expectations.
- **NO IDEA**: If you have no expectation, roll a word pair on a Meaning Table for inspiration.
- **EXPECTATION AND CRUCIAL**: If you have an idea AND the action is important, frame it as a Fate Question.

(NPC Behavior Table in `../tables/npc-behavior.md`.)

## End Of Scene Bookkeeping

- **EDIT LIST ELEMENTS**: Add new Threads/NPCs that were important in the Scene to the Lists. For Threads/Characters already on the List, add another entry if they were important again, **up to a maximum of three on the List**. Remove Threads/Characters no longer relevant.
- **ADJUST THE CHAOS FACTOR**:
  - PC was mostly **in control** of the Scene → **-1** to Chaos Factor (minimum 1).
  - PC was mostly **not in control** → **+1** to Chaos Factor (maximum 9).

## Determine NPC Statistics

- **EXPECTED STATISTIC VALUE**: Decide what you think the value should be.
- **ASK A FATE QUESTION**: "Does the NPC have a statistic of this value?" Assign Odds and resolve.
- **INTERPRET**:
  - **Yes**: The value you expected.
  - **Exceptional Yes**: About 25% higher.
  - **No**: About 25% lower.
  - **Exceptional No**: About 50% lower.
  - **Random Event**: Generate a Random Event as a special condition associated with this statistic.

(NPC Statistics Table in `../tables/npc-statistics.md`.)

## Thread Progress Track

Use a Thread Progress Track to keep an adventure focused on a particular Thread.

- **FOCUS THREAD**: Choose a Thread to be the Focus of the Track.
- **CHOOSE A TRACK**: 10, 15, or 20 points (longer = harder to resolve).
- **PROGRESS**: Any Progress toward resolving the Focus Thread in a Scene awards **2 Progress Points**.
- **FLASHPOINT**: A dramatic, important event involving the Focus Thread awards **2 Progress Points** and marks the "Did A Flashpoint Happen?" box for that phase.
- **TRACK FLASHPOINT**: If you reach enough Points to trigger a Flashpoint and one hasn't already occurred in that phase, a Flashpoint event happens. Treat as a Random Event with automatic Event Focus of Current Context.
- **DISCOVERY CHECK**: When forward momentum has stalled and you're out of ideas, ask the Fate Question "Is something discovered?" Assign Odds **no less than 50/50**.
  - **Yes**: Roll on the Thread Discovery Check Table.
  - **No**: Nothing useful is found.
  - **Exceptional Yes**: Roll twice on the Thread Discovery Check Table.
  - **Exceptional No**: Nothing found, AND no more Discovery Checks for the rest of this Scene.
- **PLOT ARMOR**: The Focus Thread cannot be resolved through normal Mythic play as long as the Track is still in progress. Any results that would complete or end the Thread must be reinterpreted to keep it active.
- **CONCLUSION**: When the Track is completed, the Conclusion is triggered. Generate a Random Event with automatic Event Focus of Current Context, with Context revolving around an Event that can resolve and end the Focus Thread. You can choose to delay the Conclusion until the next Scene.

(Track sheets and Discovery tables in `../tables/thread-progress-track.md`.)

## Keyed Scenes

- **KEYED SCENE TRIGGER**: Decide what triggers the Keyed Scene Event.
- **KEYED SCENE EVENT**: Decide what happens when the Trigger occurs. The Event happens at the **beginning of the next Scene**.

## Using Mythic With Prepared Adventures

- **SCALING**: Decide on a scaling factor (Diminisher Value) for your PC vs. the adventure. E.g., 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5.
- **APPLY DIMINISHER**: Apply the Diminisher Value to all challenges—reduce encounter counts, NPC stats, and other values.
- **READ INTRO**: Read the introductory portions of the prepared adventure.
- **PREPARE LISTS**: Add relevant Threads and NPCs from the intro to your Lists.
- **ADVENTURE FEATURES**: Create a third List, Adventure Features, including anything special about this prepared adventure.
- **FIRST SCENE**: Create a first Scene based on how the prepared adventure suggests starting.
- **PLAY SCENE BY SCENE**: Play through normally, using the prepared adventure's content as Context.
- **TESTING EXPECTED SCENES**: Test as normal. Instead of Altered/Interrupt Scenes, **rolling Chaos Factor or less keeps the Expected Scene but starts it with a Random Event**.
- **ENCOUNTERS AND LOCATIONS**: When reading new sections, read as little as possible before deciding PC actions.
- **ASK FATE QUESTIONS**: Use Fate Questions to decide details and outcomes when there is doubt.
- **RANDOM EVENTS**: Use the **Prepared Adventure Event Focus Table** instead of the regular table.

(Adventure Features sheet and Prepared Adventure Focus Table in `../tables/adventure-features.md`.)

## Peril Points

Use Peril Points to change the narrative.

- **DECIDE QUANTITY**: Choose how many Peril Points your PC gets. **Default: 2**.
- **DECIDE IF THEY REPLENISH**: Reset to maximum at the start of each session, OR a one-time resource that does not replenish.
- **EXPEND PERIL POINTS**: Use a Peril Point to avert an outcome that would otherwise end the adventure or an important narrative.

---

## Mythic With The Adventure Crafter

- **FIRST SCENE**: Generate a Turning Point or two for inspiration.
- **INTERRUPT SCENES**: Replace Random Events with Turning Points.
- **LISTS**: Use Mythic's List system instead of *The Adventure Crafter's*. References to Plotlines = Threads. **When rolling on a List for a Plot Point, roll on the full List**, not just sections that have elements.
- **CHOOSE …**: When rolling an empty line on a List for a Plot Point, you have the additional choice of **Add A Thread** or **Add A Character**. If you Choose to select a Character on the List, you can include the PC as an option even though they aren't on the List.
