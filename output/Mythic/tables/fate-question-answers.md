# Fate Question Answers

> Source: *Mythic GME 2e* p.25, p.195 (collected sheets).
> When: Translating a Fate Chart / Fate Check roll into narrative meaning.
> Input: One of four answer types (Yes / Exceptional Yes / No / Exceptional No), plus optional Random Event flag.
> Output: How to read the answer when interpreting it.

| Answer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Yes** | The answer is Yes, confirming your expectation. |
| **Exceptional Yes** | The answer is Yes, confirming your expectation **and beyond** — take the Yes to the next logical level (intensity, magnitude, scope). |
| **No** | The answer is No. Go with the **next most expected outcome** (with simple Yes/No questions, this is usually the opposite of Yes). |
| **Exceptional No** | The answer is the **opposite of a Yes** — or, if that would already be the same as a regular No, then **the opposite of a Yes intensified**. |

## Worked Examples (from p.25)

| Question | Yes | Exceptional Yes | No | Exceptional No |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Is the door locked?" | Yes, the door is locked. | Yes, and it's a darn good lock. | No, it's unlocked. | No — in fact, the door is partially open. |
| "Does the black hole begin to suck us in?" | Yes, the black hole is sucking you in. | Yes, and the stressors are causing damage all across the ship. | No, you're fine for now. | No, you're actually moving away from it. |
| "Is the gladiator armed with a sword?" | Yes, he's armed with the kind of sword you'd expect. | Yes, a huge greatsword. | No, he's armed with a spear. | No, he's not armed at all. |

## Interpretation Notes

- **Don't over-Question.** A string of narrow follow-ups can stall the game. Ask a few "big picture" Questions and run with expectations.
- **Mythic answers prompt; you interpret.** The same Yes can mean different things depending on Context.
- **Exceptional results are signals to amplify**, not just confirm. Push the narrative past the safe interpretation.
