## Running Adventures

*(PDF p.138–150)*

### The Referee's Role

*(PDF p.138)*

**Facilitator of Fun:** Prepare adventures and maps. Improvise for unexpected player actions. Adapt procedures to keep game moving. Maintain balance of risk and reward.

**Neutral Judge:** Not competitive with players. Rules applied equally to PCs, monsters, and NPCs.

**Arbiter of Rules:**
- **Rulings:** Rules don't cover everything; referee must apply judgement.
- **Resolving actions:** Use ability checks, saving throws, or judge likelihood (percentage or X-in-6).
- **Disagreements:** Brief discussion, referee is final arbiter.
- **Randomness:** Judicious use of die rolls and random tables.

### Handling PCs

*(PDF p.139)*

**Rate of Advancement:**
- Standard: At least one PC at 2nd level after 3–4 sessions. Adjust treasure/monster difficulty as needed.
- Fast: Large treasure hoards (with appropriately challenging monsters).
- Slow: Reduced advancement rate and danger.

**Character Knowledge:** Players should not act on information their PCs wouldn't know.

**Grudges:** Natural part of play between characters of opposite alignments. Referee should ensure they don't dominate play.

**Maintaining Challenge:**
- *Magical research:* Careful attention to balance. Permanent, unlimited, no-save, or level-scaling magic can become imbalanced.
- *Special abilities:* Be careful when allowing powers beyond normal class abilities.
- *Excess wealth:* Find ways to take excess money through player choices (e.g. taxes vs. outlaw status).

### Running the Game

*(PDF p.140)*

**Describing the Unknown:**
- Describe what PCs see, hear, smell — no extra information.
- Monsters described, not named.
- Surprise attacks: describe the attack itself first, full description in later rounds.
- Monster/NPC game stats (hp, level) never revealed to players.
- Magic items described, not named. Powers discovered through experimentation.

**Describing Explored Areas:** Players draw maps from referee descriptions. May use map squares. Known areas can be pre-drawn by referee.

**Using Miniatures:** Gridded surface with erasable pens. Typical scale: 1" = 5' (or 5 yards outdoors).

### Monsters and NPCs

*(PDF p.141)*

**Intelligence and Tactics:** Intelligent monsters may use traps/stealth, guards/pets, magic items, learn from past encounters, adapt PC tactics, plan counter-manoeuvres.

**Teamwork:** Melee, missile, and magic users combine. Leaders may have special abilities, max hp, or extra HD.

**Motivation:** Intelligent monsters have their own interests. They should not always cooperate with PCs. May have allies who come to their aid or avenge them.

### Adventure Scenarios

*(PDF p.142–143)*

1. **Banishing Ancient Evil** — Destroy a dormant evil recently awakened.
2. **Clearing Ruins** — Scout and clear a settlement/stronghold for settlers.
3. **Contacting a Lost Civilisation** — Discover a long-lost race in a fantastic location.
4. **Escaping from Captivity** — Begin as prisoners; regain freedom.
5. **Exploring Unknown Territory** — Hired to explore/map an uncharted region.
6. **Performing a Quest** — Sent by deity/king to recover holy/magical items.
7. **Rescuing Captives** — Rescue kidnapped NPCs for reward or personal connection.
8. **Scouting an Enemy Outpost** — Infiltrate, gather intelligence, possibly destroy.
9. **Seeking a Magical Doorway** — Locate/close a portal for magical travel.
10. **Visiting a Sacred Site** — Journey to shrine/temple to consult oracle, remove curse, or find relic.

### Designing a Dungeon

*(PDF p.144–145)*

1. **Choose setting:** Crypt/tomb, natural caverns, settlement, mine, temple, tower/fortification.
2. **Choose monsters:** Based on scenario and setting.
3. **Map the dungeon:** Graph paper, 10'/square. Number each area.
4. **Stock the dungeon:** Note monsters, traps, treasures, special features.

**Dungeon Levels:** Deeper = greater risk and reward. 1 HD monsters on level 1, 2 HD on level 2, etc.

**Random Room Stocking:**

| d6 | Contents | Treasure Chance |
|:--:|----------|:--------------:|
| 1–2 | Empty | 1-in-6 |
| 3–4 | Monster | 3-in-6 |
| 5 | Special | None |
| 6 | Trap | 2-in-6 |

**Example Room Traps:** Falling block (1d10, save vs petrify), gas (save vs poison or die), mist (harmless), pit (falling damage), scything blade (1d8), slide to lower level.

**Example Treasure Traps:** Darts (1d6 × 1d4), flash of light (blind 1d8 turns, save vs spells), hidden monster, illusion monster, spray (doubles wandering monster chance 1d6 hours), poison needle (save vs poison or die).

**Example Specials:** Alarms, animating objects, falling blocks, illusions, shifting architecture, strange waters, teleports, trapdoors, voices.

**Treasure in Empty/Trapped Rooms:**
- Level 1: 1d6 × 100sp; 50%: 1d6 × 10gp; 5%: 1d6 gems; 2%: 1d6 jewellery; 2%: 1 magic item.
- Level 2–3: 1d12 × 100sp; 50%: 1d6 × 100gp; 10%: 1d6 gems; 5%: 1d6 jewellery; 8%: 1 magic item.
- Level 4–5: 1d6 × 1,000sp; 1d6 × 200gp; 20%: 1d6 gems; 10%: 1d6 jewellery; 10%: 1 magic item.
- Level 6–7: 1d6 × 2,000sp; 1d6 × 500gp; 30%: 1d6 gems; 15%: 1d6 jewellery; 15%: 1 magic item.
- Level 8–9: 1d6 × 5,000sp; 1d6 × 1,000gp; 40%: 1d12 gems; 20%: 1d12 jewellery; 20%: 1 magic item.

### Designing a Wilderness

*(PDF p.146–147)*

1. **Choose setting:** Basic geography, climate, milieu (technology, magic, races). Start small for new campaigns.
2. **Map the region:** Graph/hex paper. Large scale (24 mi/hex) first, then smaller (6 mi/hex). Mark mountains, rivers, seas, lakes, forests, swamps.
3. **Locate human realms:** Note rulers (lord, king, merchants, etc.).
4. **Locate non-human realms:** Demihumans, monstrous races, significant non-intelligent monsters.
5. **Place base town:** Near a river or road, centre of map.
6. **Place dungeons:** About a day's journey from base town.
7. **Create regional encounter tables:** Standard or custom.

### Designing a Base Town

*(PDF p.147)*

1. **Size:** Village (50–999), Small Town (1,000–4,999), Large Town (5,000–14,999), City (15,000+).
2. **Note services:** Black market, guard, healing, lodgings, rumours.
3. **Detail ruler:** Sheriff, mayor, council, merchant confederacy, or high-level NPC.
4. **Detail other NPCs:** Associated with services or quest-givers.
5. **Create rumours:** Hooks for exploration and dungeon discovery.

### Awarding XP

*(PDF p.148)*

**Recovered Treasure:** Primary source of XP (usually ¾+ of total). 1 XP per 1gp value. Magical treasure does not grant XP.

**Defeated Monsters:** XP by HD:

| Monster HD | Base XP | Bonus XP/Ability |
|:----------:|--------:|-----------------:|
| Less than 1 | 5 | 1 |
| 1 | 10 | 3 |
| 1+ | 15 | 4 |
| 2 | 20 | 5 |
| 2+ | 25 | 10 |
| 3 | 35 | 15 |
| 3+ | 50 | 25 |
| 4 | 75 | 50 |
| 4+ | 125 | 75 |
| 5 | 175 | 125 |
| 5+ | 225 | 175 |
| 6 | 275 | 225 |
| 6+ | 350 | 300 |
| 7–7+ | 450 | 400 |
| 8–8+ | 650 | 550 |
| 9–10+ | 900 | 700 |
| 11–12+ | 1,100 | 800 |
| 13–16+ | 1,350 | 950 |
| 17–20+ | 2,000 | 1,150 |
| 21–21+ | 2,500 | 2,000 |

For each HD above 21: +250 to both Base and Bonus. Extraordinary peril: treat as 1 HD higher.

**Division:** Total XP divided evenly among all survivors (including retainers), regardless of treasure split.

**Bonuses/Penalties:** Referee may optionally grant XP bonuses for good play or penalties for poor contribution.

## Encounter Tables

*(PDF p.132–141)*

This section provides encounter tables by dungeon level and wilderness terrain type, using the monsters found in this book. Special tables may also be created to reflect the different balance of monsters inhabiting a specific dungeon or wilderness region.

### Rolling a Dungeon Encounter

Roll 1d4 and 1d10 and look up the results on the column in the table for the dungeon level being explored. The result indicates the monster encountered, with the number appearing in parentheses.

- **Number Appearing:** The values listed in the tables for some monsters do not match the values listed in the monster description. If desired, the referee may use the number appearing value listed in the monster description, for consistency.

### Rolling a Wilderness Encounter

1. **Roll 1d8:** Look up the result on the column in the Wilderness Encounter By Terrain table for the terrain being explored. The result indicates which sub-table to consult.
2. **Roll 1d20:** Look up the result in the sub-table indicated.

- **Lost World Encounters:** In Lost World or prehistoric settings, any Animal result may be rolled on the Prehistoric Animal table, in sub-table 2.
- **Aerial Encounters:** Always use the Flyer table, in sub-table 1.
- **NPC Adventurers:** Encounters with NPC adventuring parties are listed in the tables as "Basic Adventurers", "Expert Adventurers", "High-Level Cleric", "High-Level Fighter", or "High-Level Magic-User". Guidelines for generating NPC adventurers can be found under Adventuring Parties.

## NPC Encounters

### Adventuring Parties

This procedure generates parties of NPC adventurers. As the procedure is fairly involved, referees may wish to pre-generate some NPC parties for use in random encounters.

**General Details** (applies to all NPC party types):

- **Spells:** If spell casters are present, choose or roll their memorized spells.
- **Equipment:** Normal adventuring gear.
- **Treasure:** Treasure types U+V, shared among the group.
- **Marching order:** Decided by referee.

If the classes presented in the Advanced Fantasy Player's Tome are not in use, the referee should replace the listed classes by equivalents in use in the campaign.

#### Basic Adventurers

- **Composition:** 1d4+4 characters of random class and level (see table above).
- **Alignment:** Either roll the alignment of each NPC or roll once for the party.

#### Expert Adventurers

- **Composition:** 1d6+3 characters of random class and level (see table above).
- **Alignment:** Either roll the alignment of each NPC or roll once for the party.
- **Mounts:** 75% chance of being mounted, in the wilderness.
- **Magic items:** Per individual: there is a chance of the NPC having a magic item from each suitable magic item sub-table. The chance per sub-table is 5% per level of the NPC. Rolled items that cannot be used by the NPC should be ignored (no re-roll).

#### High-Level Cleric

A high-level cleric and party. (Mounts and magic items as per Expert Adventurers.)

- **Composition:** Leader (cleric of level 1d6+6), 1d4 clerics (level 1d4+1), 1d3 fighters (level 1d6).
- **Alignment:** Roll for the whole party.
- **Alternatives:** The party may alternatively be led by a high-level bard, druid, or paladin.

#### High-Level Fighter

A high-level fighter and a group of retainers, often on their way to or from war. (Mounts and magic items as per Expert Adventurers.)

- **Composition:** Leader (fighter of level 1d4+6), 2d4 retainers (level 1d4+2, any class).
- **Alignment:** Roll for the whole party.
- **Alternatives:** The party may alternatively be led by a high-level barbarian, knight, paladin, or ranger.

#### High-Level Magic-User

A high-level magic-user, accompanied by their apprentices and a group of hired guards, often on a quest for arcane lore. (Mounts and magic items as per Expert Adventurers.)

- **Composition:** Leader (magic-user of level 1d4+6), 1d4 apprentices (magic-users of level 1d3), 1d4 mercenaries (fighters of level 1d4+1).
- **Alignment:** Roll the leader's alignment. The apprentices have the same alignment as the leader, but the mercenaries may be of different alignment.
- **Alternatives:** The party may alternatively be led by a high-level illusionist with apprentices of the same class.

### Strongholds

When PCs wander into the vicinity of the wilderness stronghold of a high-level NPC, a warm reception is not always guaranteed. These guidelines may be used if the referee does not have specific notes on a stronghold's ruler and guard patrols.

#### Ruler

The referee should decide what class of NPC claims ownership of the stronghold and the surrounding lands:

- **Arcane spell caster:** Of level 1d4+10. (e.g. illusionist, magic-user.)
- **Divine spell caster:** Of level 1d8+6. (e.g. cleric, paladin.)
- **Martial character:** Of level 1d6+8. (e.g. fighter, knight.)
- **Demihuman Rulers:** Demihuman strongholds are unusual cases, and should be detailed in advance by the referee. Typically, they will attempt to avoid all contact with travellers.

#### Patrols

Strangers in the lands around a stronghold will typically be spotted by mercenary groups hired to patrol the lands. The type of troops depends on the ruler's class:

- **Arcane spell caster:** 2d6 heavy footmen. Equipped with chainmail + shield (AC 4 [15]) and swords. Morale 8.
- **Divine spell caster:** 2d6 medium horsemen. Equipped with chainmail (AC 5 [14]) and lances. Morale 9.
- **Martial character:** 2d6 heavy horsemen. Equipped with plate mail (AC 3 [16]), lances, and swords. Morale 9.
- **Garrison:** Patrols such as those described are only a small part of the ruler's garrison. Further forces may include magical monsters or humans mounted on flying creatures.

#### Reaction to Travellers

The reaction of the ruler to travellers in their domain depends on the class of the ruler and can be determined by rolling 1d6 and consulting this table:

| d6 | Arcane | Divine | Martial |
|---:|--------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Chase | Chase | Chase |
| 2 | Ignore | Chase | Chase |
| 3 | Ignore | Ignore | Chase |
| 4 | Ignore | Ignore | Ignore |
| 5 | Ignore | Invite | Ignore |
| 6 | Invite | Invite | Invite |

- **Chase:** The patrol is instructed to chase intruders out of the domain. Alternatively, they may demand a toll from travellers passing through. The exact price demanded depends on the ruler's character, the apparent wealth of the PCs, etc. If the PCs refuse to pay the toll, the patrol may attack them, drive them away, or attempt to take them prisoner.
- **Ignore:** The patrol leaves the PCs to go about their business.
- **Invite:** The patrol brings a message from the domain's ruler, inviting the PCs to stay in the stronghold. The ruler's exact motive for doing so will depend on their personality—it is not necessarily benign.
