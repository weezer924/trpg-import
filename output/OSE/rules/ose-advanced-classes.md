## Character Classes

*(PDF p.24–77)*

22 character classes (7 classic + 15 advanced). Demihumans (Dwarf, Elf, Halfling, and advanced demihuman races) use the race-as-class system—race and class are one and the same. Classes marked (Advanced) are from the Advanced Fantasy Player's Tome.

### Acrobat

*(Advanced) (PDF p.30–31)*

Acrobats are trained in skills of balance, gymnastics, and stealth. They often work in conjunction with thieves and may belong to a Thieves' Guild.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** DEX
**Hit Dice:** 1d4
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, no shields
**Weapons:** Missile weapons, dagger, sword, short sword, pole arm, spear, staff
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Adjust Ability Scores:** In step 3 of character creation, acrobats may not lower STR.

**Encumbrance:** Evasion, falling, jumping, and tightrope walking cannot be performed if encumbrance reduces the acrobat's movement rate to less than 90' (30').

**Acrobat Skills:**

- **Climb Sheer Surfaces (CS):** Roll per 100' climbed. Failure = fall at halfway point, suffering falling damage.
- **Falling (FA):** When able to tumble, acrobats suffer no damage from the first 10' of any fall. Damage from greater heights is reduced by the listed percentage (rounding fractions down).
- **Hide in Shadows (HS):** Must be motionless—attacking or moving while hiding is not possible.
- **Move Silently (MS):** Attempt to sneak past enemies unnoticed.
- **Tightrope Walking (TW):** Walk along tightropes, narrow beams, and ledges at up to half normal movement rate. Roll per 60'. Failure = fall, suffering falling damage. Wind may reduce success by up to 20%. A balance pole increases success by 10%.

**Rolling Skill Checks:** All skills except falling are rolled on d% (success if ≤ listed percentage).

**Player Knowledge:** The referee should roll HS and MS on the player's behalf (the acrobat always believes the attempt succeeded).

**Combat:** Acrobats cannot wear armour bulkier than leather and cannot use shields. They can use all missile weapons. Melee weapons are restricted to light blades and staff weapons.

**Evasion:** When retreating from melee, an acrobat's tumbling ability negates the opponent's usual +2 bonus to hit.

**Jumping:** With a 20' run-up, an acrobat can jump across a 10' wide pit or chasm (20' with a pole). With a pole, can jump over a 10' high wall or onto a 10' high ledge. Suitable poles: 10' poles, pole arms, spears, staves.

**Tumbling Attack:** Using falling or jumping, an acrobat can make a tumbling melee attack dealing double damage. Against an unaware opponent, also gains +4 to hit.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** Can establish a training school, attracting 2d6 apprentices of 1st level. If any are arrested or killed, replacements cannot be found. A successful acrobat burglar might use these followers to start a Thieves' Guild.

### Assassin

*(Advanced) (PDF p.32–33)*

Assassins are adventurers who specialise in the arts of infiltration and killing by stealth. They sometimes form guilds whereby their illicit services may be hired.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** DEX
**Hit Dice:** 1d4
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Alignment:** Assassins may not be lawful.

**Adjust Ability Scores:** In step 3 of character creation, assassins may not lower STR.

**Assassin Skills:**

- **Assassination (AS):** When attacking an unaware humanoid from behind, +4 bonus to hit. If the attack succeeds, the victim must save versus death with a penalty dependent on the assassin's level (see table). If the save fails, the victim is instantly killed; otherwise normal damage is dealt. Can assassinate humans/demihumans of any level and humanoid monsters of up to 4+1 HD. Non-living creatures are immune.
- **Climb Sheer Surfaces (CS):** Roll per 100' climbed. Failure = fall at halfway point, suffering falling damage.
- **Hear Noise (HN):** In a quiet environment, attempt to listen at a door or hear approaching sounds.
- **Hide in Shadows (HS):** Must be motionless—attacking or moving while hiding is not possible.
- **Move Silently (MS):** Attempt to sneak past enemies unnoticed.

**Rolling Skill Checks:** HN is rolled on 1d6 (success if within listed range). Assassination is not rolled as a check. Other skills are rolled on d% (success if ≤ listed percentage).

**Player Knowledge:** The referee should roll HN, HS, and MS on the player's behalf (the assassin doesn't immediately know if the attempt succeeded).

**Combat:** Assassins cannot wear armour bulkier than leather but may use shields. They can use all types of weapons.

**Disguise:** Assassins are masters of disguise—able to create disguises that pass even close scrutiny. Everyone the assassin meets has a 2% chance of spotting the disguise (rolled once per day of meeting). Posing as another class, race, or sex increases chance of detection by 2% per change. A disguise may alter height (up to 3" shorter or 5" taller) or weight (slightly thinner, much bulkier).

**Hirelings:** Assassins of 1st–3rd level may not employ retainers, mercenaries, or specialists. From 4th level, may hire other assassins of lower level. From 8th level, may hire thieves. From 12th level, any type of character.

**Poison:** Victims of a poisoning by an assassin suffer a –2 penalty to the saving throw.

**After Reaching 12th Level:** May attempt to take over an existing guild of assassins or thieves by killing the old guild-master (by any means—poisoning, assassination, duel, etc.). If successful, can maintain a guild of 7d4 members. 75% of existing members will leave, but new members (typically 1st level assassins) will arrive over time.

### Barbarian

*(Advanced) (PDF p.34–35)*

Barbarians are tribal warriors from wild lands. They are formidable fighters with many useful survival skills but have a deep mistrust of the arcane.

**Requirements:** Minimum DEX 9
**Prime Requisite:** CON and STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d8
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, chainmail, shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** A barbarian with at least 13 in one prime requisite gains +5% XP. If both STR and CON are 16 or higher, +10% bonus.

**Literacy:** A 1st level barbarian cannot read or write, irrespective of INT score.

**Agile Fighting:** Upon reaching 4th level, a barbarian gains a +1 AC bonus. This increases to +2 at 6th level, +3 at 8th level, and +4 at 10th level.

**Barbarian Skills:** In the wilderness, barbarians can use the following skills:

- **Climb Sheer Surfaces (CS):** Only applies to natural surfaces (tree trunks, cliff faces). Roll per 100' climbed. Failure = fall at halfway point, suffering falling damage.
- **Hide in Undergrowth (HD):** Must be motionless—attacking or moving while hiding is not possible.
- **Move Silently (MS):** Attempt to sneak past enemies.

**Rolling Skill Checks:** All skills are rolled on d% (success if ≤ listed percentage).

**Player Knowledge:** The referee should roll HD and MS on the player's behalf (the barbarian always believes the attempt succeeded).

**Combat:** Barbarians can use all types of weapons and can use leather armour, chainmail, and shields.

**Cure Poison:** In the wilderness, a barbarian can gather herbs to concoct an antidote to natural poisons. Takes one turn per character to be cured. Each subject may make a second save versus poison to end the effects.

**Fear of Magic:** Barbarians are mistrustful of magic and will refuse to knowingly use or come under the influence of spells or magic items. Barbarians will accept divine magic associated with their tribal religion.

**Foraging and Hunting:** A party with a barbarian succeeds at foraging with a 2-in-6 chance and finds prey when hunting with a 5-in-6 chance.

**Strike Invulnerable Monsters:** A barbarian of 4th level or higher can hit monsters that can normally only be harmed by magic.

**After Reaching 8th Level:** Can call upon their native people to form a great horde of warriors, numbering 250 per level above 7th. The warriors are 1st level fighters with basic combat gear and may be mounted if customary in their culture. A horde can only be mustered for a specific purpose (e.g. attacking an enemy city, rescuing hostages). Once the purpose is achieved or abandoned, the horde disbands. Mustering takes one week; the horde remains for one week per level of the barbarian.

### Bard

*(Advanced) (PDF p.36–37)*

Bards are members of a sect of minstrels and warrior poets associated with the druids. Like druids, bards worship the force of nature and the myriad deities that personify it. Their strengths lie in their deep knowledge of myth and legend, the magic they wield on behalf of their gods, and the enchanting power of their music.

**Requirements:** Minimum DEX 9, minimum INT 9
**Prime Requisite:** CHA
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, chainmail, no shields
**Weapons:** Missile weapons, one-handed melee weapons
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Anti-Charm:** While the bard plays music and sings, allies within 30' are immune to song-based magical effects and the beguiling powers of sylvan creatures or fairies. Allies already under such effects may make another saving throw with a +4 bonus.

**Combat:** Bards can use leather armour or chainmail but not plate mail or shields. They can use all missile weapons and all one-handed melee weapons.

**Divine Magic:** See Magic for full details on divine magic.

- **Deity disfavour:** Bards must be faithful to the tenets of their alignment and religion. Falling from favour may incur penalties.
- **Spell casting:** From 2nd level, bards may pray to receive spells. Bards use the druid spell list.
- **Using magic items:** Bards can use magic scrolls of spells on their spell list and any items that only druids can use.

**Enchantment:** By playing music and singing, the bard can fascinate subjects in a 30' radius. Does not function in combat.

- **Number of subjects:** Up to 2HD of creatures per level of the bard are affected. May target a specific individual or a group (affected individuals determined randomly).
- **Types of subjects:** At 1st level, persons. At 4th level, also animals. At 7th level, also monsters.
- **Effect:** Each subject must save versus spells or be fascinated:
  - *Rapt:* Fascinated subjects' attention is fully bent on the bard's performance, as long as it continues.
  - *Follow:* The bard may walk while playing. Fascinated subjects will follow.
  - *Interruptions:* If the performance is interrupted (e.g. by loud noise or violence), the fascination ends immediately.

**Charming Fascinated Subjects:** If the bard performs for at least one turn and the performance ends without interruption, fascinated subjects may be placed under a deeper charm. Each subject makes another save versus spells (+2 bonus) or is charmed for one turn per level of the bard:

- *Friendship:* Charmed subjects regard the bard as a trusted friend and ally and will come to the bard's defence.
- *Commands:* If they share a language, charmed subjects will obey the bard's commands.
- *Alignment:* Commands contradicting the creature's nature or alignment may be ignored.
- *Suicidal commands:* Never obeyed.

**Languages:** Bards learn new languages as they advance. At every even-numbered level above 3rd (4th, 6th, 8th, etc.), the player may choose an additional language. Bards may learn the secret tongue of druids.

**Lore:** From 2nd level, a bard has a 2-in-6 chance of knowing lore pertaining to monsters, magic items, or heroes of folktale or legend. This ability may be used to identify the nature and powers of magic items.

**After Reaching 11th Level:** Can establish a manor. 2d6 apprentice bards of 1st level will come to study under the character.

### Cleric

*(PDF p.24–25)*

Clerics are adventurers who have sworn to serve a deity. They are trained for battle and channel the power of their deity.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** WIS
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Any blunt weapons (club, mace, sling, staff, war hammer)
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Combat:** Clerics can use all types of armour. Strict holy doctrine prevents use of weapons with a sharp, cutting edge or stabbing point.

**Divine Magic:** (See Magic, p68 for full details on divine magic.)
- **Holy symbol:** A cleric must carry a holy symbol.
- **Deity disfavour:** Clerics must be faithful to the tenets of their alignment, clergy, and religion. Falling from favour may incur penalties.
- **Magical research:** A cleric of any level may spend time and money on magical research. At 9th level, they can create magic items.
- **Spell casting:** From 2nd level, clerics may pray to receive spells. The spell list is on p72.
- **Using magic items:** Clerics can use magic scrolls of spells on their list and items usable only by divine spell casters (e.g. some magic staves).

**Turning the Undead:** Clerics can invoke the power of their deity to repel undead. Roll 2d6 and consult the Turning Table. On success, roll 2d6 for number of HD affected.

- **Turned undead:** Leave the area if possible and will not harm or contact the cleric.
- **Destroyed undead (D result):** Instantly and permanently annihilated.
- **Excess:** Rolled HD insufficient to affect a monster are wasted.
- **Minimum effect:** At least one undead monster is always affected on success.
- **Mixed groups:** Lowest HD undead are affected first.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** May establish or build a stronghold at half normal price (divine intervention). Attracts 5d6 × 10 fighters of level 1–2 as followers who never check morale.

### Drow

*(Advanced) (PDF p.40–41)*

Drow (also known as dark elves) are slender, fey demihumans with pointed ears, skin as black as the night sky, and hair of silver or white. They have extremely long lifespans, being nigh immortal. Drow dwell exclusively underground, carving great cities of stone and crystal. They are related to the elves of the surface world and share their love of nature and magic. Drow typically weigh about 120 pounds and are from 5 to 5½ feet tall. They are talented fighters and gain powerful magic through the worship of their strange subterranean deities. They have a strong resistance to magic, as reflected in their saving throws.

**Requirements:** Minimum INT 9
**Prime Requisite:** STR and WIS
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 10
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Deepcommon, Elvish, Gnomish, the secret language of spiders

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** A drow with at least 13 STR and WIS gains +5% XP. A drow with at least 13 STR and at least 16 WIS gains +10% bonus.

**Combat:** Drow can use all types of weapons and armour.

**Detect Secret Doors:** 2-in-6 chance when actively searching.

**Divine Magic:** See Magic for full details on divine magic.

- **Holy symbol:** A drow must carry a holy symbol.
- **Deity disfavour:** Drow must be faithful to the tenets of their alignment, clergy, and religion. Falling from favour may incur penalties.
- **Magical research:** A drow of any level may spend time and money on magical research. At 9th level, they can create magic items.
- **Spell casting:** A drow may pray to receive spells. Drow cast from the cleric spell list. At 1st level, a drow may only pray for the *light (darkness)* spell. From 2nd level, any spell on the cleric list. From 3rd level, drow are also able to pray for the magic-user *web* spell.
- **Using magic items:** Drow can use magic scrolls of spells on their spell list and items usable only by divine spell casters (e.g. some staves).

**Immunity to Ghoul Paralysis:** Completely unaffected by ghoul paralysis.

**Infravision:** 90'.

**Light Sensitivity:** When in bright light (daylight, *continual light*), drow suffer a –2 penalty to attack rolls and a –1 penalty to Armour Class.

**Listening at Doors:** 2-in-6 chance of hearing noises.

**Spider Affinity:** Drow live alongside many species of spiders, including giant spiders. They can speak the secret language of spiders and gain a +1 bonus to reaction rolls when encountering spiders.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** May establish a subterranean stronghold or temple. If in favour with their deity, the complex may be bought or built at half normal price. Will attract followers (5d6 × 10 non-spell casting drow of 1st or 2nd level). These troops are completely devoted (never checking morale). The referee decides proportions of 1st/2nd level and archers/infantry.

### Druid

*(Advanced) (PDF p.42–43)*

Druids are priests of nature, protecting wild lands from the encroachment of "civilised" Law and the corrupting touch of Chaos. They worship the force of nature itself, personified in the form of various nature deities.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** WIS
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, wooden shields
**Weapons:** Club, dagger, sling, spear, staff
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Alignment:** Druids must be neutral in alignment. They regard the ways of the natural world as the ideal state; Law and Chaos are both equally against nature.

**Charm Immunity:** Druids of 7th level and above are immune to the charms of fairies and sylvan creatures (e.g. dryads, nixies).

**Combat:** Druids may not use metal armour or shields (wooden shields may be purchased at normal price). Weapons are limited to clubs, daggers, slings, spears, and staves.

**Divine Magic:** See Magic for full details on divine magic.

- **Holy symbol:** A druid must carry a holy symbol: a sprig of mistletoe which the character must harvest.
- **Deity disfavour:** Druids must be faithful to the tenets of their alignment and religion. Falling from favour may incur penalties.
- **Magical research:** A druid of any level may spend time and money on magical research. At 9th level, they can create magic items.
- **Spell casting:** A druid may pray to receive spells from nature. The druid spell list is on p.131 of the Player's Tome.
- **Using magic items:** Druids can use magic scrolls of spells on their spell list and items usable only by divine spell casters (e.g. some staves). Druids may not use magical books or tomes.

**Energy Resistance:** Druids gain a +2 bonus to saving throws against electricity (lightning) and fire.

**Identification:** Druids can identify all plants and animals and can discern pure water.

**Languages:** Druids speak a secret tongue known only to their sect. At each level above 2nd, a druid also learns a language used by creatures of sylvan forests (e.g. dryads, green dragons, pixies, treants).

**Pass Without Trace:** From 3rd level, a druid can pass through natural environments without leaving any tracks. Also able to move through overgrown areas at normal speed without impediment.

**Path-Finding:** A party with a druid has only a 1-in-6 chance of getting lost in woodlands.

**Shape Change:** At 7th level, a druid gains the power to change into the form of a reptile, bird, and mammal (once per day each). The animal may be of any size up to around twice the bulk of the druid's normal form. If a druid has lost hit points, they regain 1d4 hit points per level upon changing into an animal. All equipment carried is absorbed into the animal form and reappears when the druid changes back.

**Reaching 12th Level and Above:** There can only be nine druids of 12th level. When a druid has enough XP to reach 12th level, they must challenge an existing 12th level druid. The character only advances if the other druid is defeated. Such challenges may take any agreed form, including combat (which need not be fatal). Likewise, there can only be three druids of 13th level and a single druid (the arch-druid) of 14th level. Druids who attain 12th level or above may build a stronghold integrated into a natural setting.

### Duergar

*(Advanced) (PDF p.46–47)*

Duergars (grey dwarves) are scrawny, bearded demihumans with grey skin and hair and gnarled faces. They are around 4' tall, about 120 pounds, and have lifespans of up to 500 years. Duergars dwell in strongholds and cities deep underground. They are renowned for their greed for precious metals and stones and for their xenophobia toward other races. Duergars have a naturally strong constitution and are highly resistant to magic.

**Requirements:** Minimum CON 9, minimum INT 9
**Prime Requisite:** STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 10
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Small or normal sized (cannot use longbows or two-handed swords)
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Deepcommon, Dwarvish, Gnomish, Goblin, Kobold

**Combat:** Duergars can use all types of armour. Due to short height, they can only use small or normal sized weapons.

**Detect Construction Tricks:** 2-in-6 chance of detecting new construction, sliding walls, or sloping passages when searching.

**Detect Room Traps:** 2-in-6 chance of detecting non-magical room traps when searching.

**Infravision:** 90'.

**Light Sensitivity:** When in bright light (daylight, *continual light*), duergars suffer a –2 penalty to attack rolls and a –1 penalty to AC.

**Listening at Doors:** 2-in-6 chance of hearing noises.

**Mental Powers:** Once per day per level, a duergar may activate one of the following mental powers (requires spending a round concentrating—no moving, attacking, or other actions; can be disrupted in combat like spell casting; must declare before initiative is rolled):

- **Enlargement:** The duergar's body, clothing, and armour (but not other equipment) double in size for 1d4 rounds. While enlarged, melee attacks inflict double damage.
- **Invisibility:** The duergar disappears from the sight of one or more creatures (selected when activated) for one turn. Up to 1HD of creatures per level are affected. In combat, affected creatures may attack the duergar with a –4 penalty after the first round.
- **Shrinking:** The duergar and all equipment shrinks to 6" tall for up to one turn per level. While shrunk, can slip through narrow spaces and, when motionless, has a 90% chance of going unnoticed. Cannot harm creatures larger than 1' tall.
- **Heat:** A creature or object within sight is subjected to scorching heat. Paper/cloth ignite, liquids boil, metals glow. Lasts one round per level. Applied to flesh or metal in contact with a creature (e.g. armour), inflicts 1d4 damage per round.

**Stealth:** Underground, duergars have a 3-in-6 chance of moving silently.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** May create an underground stronghold that attracts duergars from far and wide. Duergars of the character's clan will be attracted to live under their roof. Clans may collaborate in times of war or disaster. May only hire duergar mercenaries; specialists and retainers of any race may be hired.

### Gnome

*(Advanced) (PDF p.54–55)*

Gnomes are a race of short demihumans with long noses and beards. They are cousins of the dwarves and the two races are on friendly terms. Gnomes prefer to dwell in underground complexes in forests or foothills. They love mining, precious stones, and machinery—from miniature marvels of clockwork to great construction engines. Gnomes are also famed for their penchant for pointy, red, felt hats. Gnomes are typically around 3½' tall and weigh around 100 pounds.

**Requirements:** Minimum CON 9
**Prime Requisite:** DEX and INT
**Hit Dice:** 1d4
**Maximum Level:** 8
**Armour:** Leather, shields
**Weapons:** Any appropriate to size (cannot use longbows or two-handed swords)
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Gnomish, Dwarvish, Kobold, the secret language of burrowing mammals

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** A gnome with at least 13 DEX and INT gains +5% XP. A gnome with at least 13 DEX and at least 16 INT gains +10% bonus.

- **Magical research:** A gnome of any level may spend time and money on magical research. At 8th level, they can create magic items.
- **Spell casting:** Gnomes carry spell books. A 1st level gnome has one spell in their book, selected by the referee (who may allow the player to choose). Gnomes use the illusionist spell list.
- **Using magic items:** Gnomes can use magic scrolls of spells on their list and items usable only by arcane spell casters (e.g. magic wands).

**Combat:** Gnomes can use leather armour and shields. Armour must be tailored to gnomes' small size. They can use any weapon appropriate to their stature (as determined by the referee).

**Defensive Bonus:** +2 bonus to AC when attacked by large opponents (greater than human-sized).

**Detect Construction Tricks:** 2-in-6 chance of detecting new construction, sliding walls, or sloping passages when searching.

**Infravision:** 90'.

**Listening at Doors:** 2-in-6 chance of hearing noises.

**Speak with Burrowing Mammals:** Gnomes often keep burrowing mammals (badgers, moles, etc.) as pets. They know the secret language of such creatures.

**After Reaching 8th Level:** May create an underground stronghold that attracts gnomes from far and wide. All burrowing mammals within 5 miles of the stronghold will become friends with the gnomes (warn of intruders, carry messages, etc.). In exchange, the gnome must protect the animals from harm. ### Half-Elf

*(Advanced) (PDF p.56–57)*

Half-elves are the rare offspring of elves and humans. Physically, they tend to combine the best features of their parents, having the innate beauty of elves and the robust physique of humans. They are human-like in stature but always have a feature that marks their elven heritage (e.g. pointed ears or unusually bright eyes). Half-elves are skilled fighters and dabble with magic, though they lack their elvish parents' mastery of the arcane.

**Requirements:** Minimum CHA 9, minimum CON 9
**Prime Requisite:** INT and STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 12
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Elvish

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** A half-elf with at least 13 INT and STR gains +5% XP. A half-elf with 16+ in one prime requisite and 13+ in the other gains +10% bonus.

**Arcane Magic:** See Magic for full details on arcane magic.

- **Spell casting:** From 2nd level, half-elves gain the ability to cast arcane spells, owning a spell book. A 2nd level half-elf has one spell in their book, selected by the referee (who may allow the player to choose). Half-elves use the magic-user spell list.
- **Using magic items:** Half-elves can use magic scrolls of spells on their list and items usable only by arcane spell casters (e.g. magic wands).

**Combat:** Half-elves can use all types of weapons and armour.

**Detect Secret Doors:** 2-in-6 chance when actively searching.

**Infravision:** 60'.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** A half-elf may strive to become a leader among humans or among elves, constructing a stronghold either in the name of a human liege or in the deep forests of the elves.

- *Human Stronghold:* May build a castle or stronghold and control surrounding lands. May be granted a title such as Baron or Baroness.
- *Elven Stronghold:* May construct a stronghold in the depths of the forest, blending with natural environment. Construction costs as much as stone due to exquisite craftsmanship. Forest animals within 5 miles become friends (warn of intruders, carry messages). In exchange, the half-elf must protect the animals. May only hire elven mercenaries; specialists and retainers of any race may be hired.

Halflings are small, rotund demihumans with furry feet and curly hair, weighing about 60 pounds and around 3' tall. ### Half-Orc

*(Advanced) (PDF p.62–63)*

Half-orcs are the rare offspring of orcs and humans. They are human-like in stature and appearance, but usually have at least one feature that marks their orcish heritage (e.g. fangs or a pig-like snout). Due to the common animosity between orcs and humans, half-orcs are typically outcasts from both their parent cultures, living on the fringes of society and making a living by whatever means they can. Half-orc adventurers are capable combatants and have some skill as thieves.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** DEX and STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 8
**Armour:** Leather, chainmail, shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Orcish

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** A half-orc with at least 13 DEX and STR gains +5% XP. A half-orc with at least 16 DEX and STR gains +10% bonus.

**Back-Stab:** When attacking an unaware opponent from behind, +4 bonus to hit and double damage dealt.

**Combat:** Half-orcs can use all types of weapons and can use leather armour, chainmail, and shields.

**Infravision:** 60'.

**Retainers:** Half-orcs often have difficulty gaining trust. Retainers in a half-orc's employ have their loyalty score reduced by one (does not apply to half-orc retainers).

**Thieving Skills:**

- **Move Silently (MS):** Attempt to sneak past enemies unnoticed.
- **Pick Pockets (PP):** If the victim is above 5th level, –5% per level above 5th. A roll of more than twice the required percentage means the theft is noticed.

**Rolling Skill Checks:** All skills are rolled on d% (success if ≤ listed percentage).

**Player Knowledge:** The referee should roll HS and MS on the player's behalf (the half-orc always believes the attempt succeeded).

**After Reaching 8th Level:** Can establish a bandit stronghold, attracting 2d6 apprentices—1st level fighters, thieves, or half-orcs. If any are arrested or killed, replacements cannot be found. A successful half-orc might use these followers to start a Thieves' Guild.

### Knight

*(Advanced) (PDF p.66–67)*

Knights are warriors who serve a noble house or knightly order, carrying out their liege's command and upholding the honour of the liege at all costs. They are masters of heavily armoured, mounted combat, preferring the lance above all other weapons. Knights are often members of the noble classes, but a person of lowlier origin may be initiated as a knight as a reward for noble deeds.

**Requirements:** Minimum CON 9, minimum DEX 9
**Prime Requisite:** STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d8
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Chainmail, plate mail, shields
**Weapons:** Melee weapons only
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Alignment:** A knight must have the same alignment as their liege.

**Knighthood:** Knights of 1st and 2nd level are known as "squires" and are not yet regarded as true knights. Upon reaching 3rd level, the character is knighted by their liege and gains the right to bear a coat of arms (typically emblazoned upon the knight's shield).

**Chivalric Code:** Knights are bound by a stringent code of honour. A knight does not earn XP for deeds at odds with this code. The fundamentals: preference of death over dishonour; service of the knight's liege as the greatest honour; glory in single combat; defence to the death of any in the knight's charge; honouring of superiors; respect of equals; demanding obedience from inferiors; scorning the ignoble.

- *Dishonour:* A knight who brings dishonour upon themselves or their liege (including changing alignment) may have their knighthood revoked, becoming a fighter of equivalent level. Knighthood may be regained by performing a special quest.

**Combat:** Knights can use all melee weapons but cannot use missile weapons (regarded as dishonourable). They can wear any metal armour but scorn non-metal armour.

- *Prowess of arms:* Knights always favour the most impressive and impervious-looking armour available.
- *Single combat:* In battle, a knight must attack the most powerful or worthy foe in single combat.

**Flying Mounts:** A knight of 5th level or higher can train fantastic, flying monsters as mounts (one month training). May train flying monsters of HD at most equal to the knight's level. Use at referee's discretion.

**Horsemanship:**

- *Assessing steeds:* Knights can assess the worth of any steed, determining whether an animal has low, average, or high hit points for its type.
- *Urging great speed:* From 5th level, can urge their steed to increase its movement rate by 30' (10') for up to 6 turns, once per day.

**Hospitality:** A knight of 3rd level or higher can expect hospitality and aid from nobles and other knights of the same alignment or social affiliation. A knight is expected to extend such hospitality in kind.

**Mounted Combat:** Knights gain a +1 bonus to attack rolls when mounted.

**Strength of Will:**

- *Fear:* Knights are immune to all supernatural fear effects. From 3rd level, companions within 10' gain +2 to saves against fear, and retainers/mercenaries within 10' gain +2 to loyalty/morale.
- *Beguilement:* +4 to saves against hold spells, charms, mind control, hypnotism, suggestion. +2 to saves against illusion. Gain a saving throw (not normally allowed) against sleep spells.

**Stronghold:** Any time a knight of 3rd level or higher wishes (with sufficient money), they can build a castle or stronghold. Permission of the knight's liege is always required.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** May be granted a title such as Baron or Baroness. The land under the knight's control is then known as a Barony.

### Paladin

*(Advanced) (PDF p.70–71)*

Paladins are warriors sworn by sacred oath to the service of a Lawful holy order.

**Requirements:** Minimum CHA 9
**Prime Requisite:** STR and WIS
**Hit Dice:** 1d8
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Alignment:** A paladin must be lawful. If the character's alignment ever changes (for any reason), they lose all class abilities and become a fighter of the same level. The referee may allow a quest of atonement to regain paladin status.

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** A paladin with at least 13 in one prime requisite gains +5% XP. If both STR and WIS are 16 or higher, +10% bonus.

**Combat:** Paladins can use all types of weapons and armour.

**Divine Magic:** See Magic for full details on divine magic.

- **Holy symbol:** A paladin must carry a holy symbol.
- **Deity disfavour:** Paladins must be faithful to the tenets of their alignment and religion. Falling from favour may incur penalties.
- **Spell casting:** From 9th level, a paladin may pray to receive spells. Paladins use the cleric spell list.

**Holy Resistance:** Paladins are immune to all disease.

**Laying on Hands:** Once per day, a paladin can heal 2 hit points per level by laying hands on wounded characters. Additionally, a paladin of 5th level or higher may cure disease once per week by laying on hands.

**Turning the Undead:** Paladins of 3rd level and higher can invoke their deity's power to repel undead. Roll 2d6 and consult the turning table. On success, roll 2d6 for HD affected.

- *Turned undead:* Leave the area if possible and will not harm or contact the paladin.
- *Destroyed undead (D result):* Instantly and permanently annihilated.
- *Excess:* Rolled HD insufficient to affect a monster are wasted.
- *Minimum effect:* At least one undead monster is always affected on success.
- *Mixed groups:* Lowest HD undead affected first.

**Vow of Humility:** A paladin may keep only one suit of magical armour, one magical shield, and one magical melee weapon. Must also donate 10% of all income to a Lawful religious institution.

**Warhorse:** A paladin of 4th level or higher may summon a holy charger—a warhorse with AC 5 [14], 5+5 HD, and movement rate 180' (60'). If the horse dies, another may not be summoned for 10 years.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** With permission of their holy order, may build a castle or stronghold. May be granted a title such as Baron or Baroness.

### Ranger

*(Advanced) (PDF p.72–73)*

Rangers are members of a secret society which protects their native lands from invasion and the influence of Chaos. They are skilled warriors adapted to life in the wilds. At higher levels, their connection with nature grants them the ability to cast spells.

**Requirements:** Minimum CON 9, minimum WIS 9
**Prime Requisite:** STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d8
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, chainmail, shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Alignment:** Rangers may only be lawful or neutral. If a ranger ever changes alignment to chaotic, they lose all special class abilities and become a fighter of the same level. May regain ranger status by performing a special quest.

**Awareness:** Rangers are only surprised on a roll of 1. This may mean a ranger can act in the surprise round while companions are surprised.

**Combat:** Rangers can use all types of weapons and can use leather armour, chainmail, and shields. Because of their need for stealth, they cannot use plate mail.

**Divine Magic:** See Magic for full details on divine magic.

- **Spell casting:** From 8th level, due to a deep connection with nature, a ranger gains the ability to cast spells. Rangers use the druid spell list.

**Foraging and Hunting:** A party with a ranger succeeds at foraging with a 2-in-6 chance and finds prey when hunting with a 5-in-6 chance.

**Limited Possessions:** A ranger may only keep wealth and possessions that can be kept on their person or mount. Excess must be donated to worthy causes (not other PCs!).

**Pursuit:** When the ranger's party pursues an opposing group in the wilderness, the chance of evasion is reduced by 10%.

**Stealth:** In the wilderness, a ranger has a 3-in-6 chance of hiding and a 3-in-6 chance of moving silently.

**Tracking:** Rangers can identify and follow tracks. Success improves with level (see table). Modifiers: soft/hard ground (+20% to –50%); size of group (+2% per creature beyond the first); mingling tracks (–50%); age of tracks (–5% per 12 hours); rainfall (–25% per hour); efforts to hide tracks (–25%).

**After Reaching 10th Level:** 2d12 beings will join the ranger as followers. The nature of these followers is up to the referee—may include adventurers, animals, fantastic mounts, sylvan creatures, or special monsters. If any die, they are not replaced.

### Svirfneblin

*(Advanced) (PDF p.74–75)*

Short, thickset demihumans with long noses and gnarled, hairless, grey skin. Svirfneblins (also known as deep gnomes) are subterranean cousins of the gnomes who live close to the surface. Svirfneblins are skilled tunnellers and makers of mechanical contraptions and cunning secret doors. They love gems above all else and excavate their communities around veins of precious stones. **Prime Requisite:** STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 8
**Armour:** Any appropriate to size, including shields
**Weapons:** Any appropriate to size (cannot use longbows or two-handed swords)
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Deepcommon, Gnomish, Dwarvish, Kobold, the language of earth elementals

**Blend into Stone:** Svirfneblins can go unnoticed in an environment of natural or carved stone, so long as they remain silent and motionless. Chance of success: 4-in-6 in gloomy conditions, 2-in-6 in well-lit conditions.

**Combat:** Svirfneblins can use all types of armour (tailored to their small size) and any weapon appropriate to their stature.

**Defensive Bonus:** +2 bonus to AC when attacked by large opponents (greater than human-sized).

**Detect Construction Tricks:** 2-in-6 chance of detecting new construction, sliding walls, or sloping passages when searching.

**Illusion Resistance:** +2 bonus to all saving throws against illusions.

**Infravision:** 90'.

**Speak with Earth Elementals:** Svirfneblins can speak the grinding, growling tongue of natives of the elemental plane of earth.

**Stone Murmurs:** Svirfneblins can understand the imperceptible grumblings of stone. If a svirfneblin stands quietly for one turn with their ear pressed against a stone surface, they have a 2-in-6 chance of divining one of the following (player's choice):

- The presence of secret doors in the stone, within 10'.
- The presence of gems or precious metals, up to 30' beyond the surface.
- The presence of living creatures, up to 30' beyond the surface.
- The presence of bodies of water or open spaces, up to 60' beyond the surface.

**Using Magic Items:** Svirfneblins can use all magic items (except spell scrolls) which deal with summoning or controlling earth elementals, including items normally only usable by spell casters.

**After Reaching 8th Level:** May construct a subterranean stronghold attracting svirfneblins from far and wide. 1d3 16 HD earth elementals will live in the rocks around the stronghold and be friendly with the svirfneblins (warning of intruders, carrying messages, protecting in times of grave danger). In exchange, the svirfneblin ruler must always defend earth elementals within the territory. May hire retainers or specialists of any race, but only soldiers of svirfneblin stock.

### Thief

*(PDF p.36–37)*

Thieves are adventurers who live by their skills of deception and stealth. Their range of unique skills makes them very handy companions in adventures. However, thieves are not always to be trusted.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** DEX
**Hit Dice:** 1d4
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, no shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Adjust Ability Scores:** In step 3 of character creation, thieves may not lower STR.

**Back-Stab:** When attacking an unaware opponent from behind, +4 bonus to hit and double damage dealt.

**Combat:** Thieves cannot wear armour heavier than leather and cannot use shields. They can use any weapon.

**Read Languages:** From 4th level, 80% chance to read non-magical text in any language (including dead languages and basic codes). On failure, cannot try that text again until gaining a level.

**Scroll Use:** From 10th level, can cast arcane spells from scrolls. 10% chance of error: spell does not function as expected and creates an unusual or deleterious effect.

**Thief Skills:**

- **Climb Sheer Surfaces (CS):** Roll per 100' climbed. Failure = fall at halfway point, taking falling damage.
- **Find or Remove Treasure Traps (TR):** One roll to find, another to remove. May only attempt once per trap.
- **Hear Noise (HN):** In quiet environments, attempt to listen at a door or hear approaching sounds.
- **Hide in Shadows (HS):** Must be motionless—attacking or moving while hiding is not possible.
- **Move Silently (MS):** Attempt to sneak past enemies unnoticed.
- **Open Locks (OL):** Requires thieves' tools. One attempt per lock; on failure, cannot retry until gaining a level.
- **Pick Pockets (PP):** If victim is above 5th level, –5% per level above 5th. Always at least 1% chance of failure. Roll of more than twice the required percentage means the theft is noticed.

**Rolling Skill Checks:** Hear Noise is rolled on 1d6 (success if within listed range). All other skills are rolled on d% (success if ≤ listed percentage).

**Player Knowledge:** The referee should roll Hear Noise, Hide in Shadows, and Move Silently on the player's behalf (the thief doesn't immediately know if the attempt succeeded).

**After Reaching 9th Level:** Can establish a thief den, attracting 2d6 apprentices of 1st level. If any are arrested or killed, they cannot be replaced. A successful thief might use these followers to start a Thieves' Guild.

## Character Races

*(PDF p.80–89)*

When using separate race and class (the Advanced method), players first choose a race, then select a class from those available to that race. Each demihuman race lists the classes available and the maximum level for each. Humans can select any non-demihuman class with unlimited level advancement.

**Class and Level Restrictions:** Not all races are equally suited to every class. Restrictions represent a race's suitability and serve as a balancing factor, offsetting extra racial abilities with strict limits on class choice and level advancement.

**Lifting Class and Level Restrictions (Optional Rule):** Many groups do not enforce these restrictions. If lifted, it is recommended to grant humans the optional racial abilities below to compensate for the loss of their main advantage (unlimited advancement in any class).
