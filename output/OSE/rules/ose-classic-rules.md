## Introduction

*(PDF p.6–11)*

### About This Game

**What is Old-School Essentials?**

- A role-playing game of fantastic adventure, where players work together to overcome fearsome monsters, sinister plots, and deadly traps in search of wealth, power, and glory.
- An old-school role-playing game, styled after the beloved games of the 1970s and 1980s.
- A relatively rules-light game, where the rules are kept simple in order to let imagination and fast-paced action take the spotlight.
- A streamlined rules set, with the books carefully structured for maximum usability during play.
- A modular game that can be easily expanded for play in many different fantastic genres and game worlds.

**New to Role-Playing Games?** The easiest way to learn is to dive in and play. The next best way is to watch other people playing via live streams and YouTube channels.

**New to Old-School Games?** Newer RPGs (post-2000) tend to work from different design principles. Recommended reading for old-school play style:

- *Principia Apocrypha*, by Ben Milton, Steven Lumpkin, and David Perry.
- *Quick Primer for Old School Gaming*, by Matthew Finch.

**Ascending Armour Class:** Players used to an AC system where higher scores are better are advised to use the optional rule for Ascending Armour Class (p13).

**A Note on Rulings:** In an old-school game such as Old-School Essentials, the rules are not intended to cover all possible eventualities. The referee must be ready to apply judgement to resolve any unexpected situations which arise.

### Classic Fantasy Gaming

*(PDF p.7)*

This book presents all the rules required to run Old-School Essentials games in classic fantasy settings.

**Peril and Adventure:** Players take on the role of adventurers drawn to confront danger in search of wealth, ancient secrets, and wonder. Danger and reward are most commonly found in wilderness and dungeons.

- **Wilderness:** Forbidden forests, swamps, wild mountain ranges, uncharted seas. Any outdoor space where peril and adventure can be met.
- **Dungeons:** Forsaken ruins, primal caverns, subterranean cities. Any indoor or subterranean space where peril and adventure can be met.

**The Fantastic:** The rules assume a setting where PCs come into contact with the fantastic, otherworldly, weird, and wonderful.

**Treasure:** Hoards of gold, fabled magical objects, artefacts crafted by the gods. The promise of attaining treasures—for their material value or fantastic powers—is the lure that pulls adventurers into perilous realms.

**Monsters:** Terrible creatures, fearsome mythical beasts, chimeric experiments, bands of monstrous beast-men, beings from otherworldly dimensions. Such creatures lurk in wilderness and dungeons, guarding wondrous treasures.

**Magic:** Dark sorcery, divine rituals, summoned monsters, sites of eldritch power, objects bound with occult energies. Magic is a powerful tool that may be wielded by player characters, but also by their enemies.

**Sentient Species:** Fairies, dwarves, bestial humanoids, mutants twisted by magic. While humans are typically the most widespread species, other intelligent species exist. Intelligent species available as player characters are termed **demihumans**.

### Terminology

*(PDF p.8)*

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Referee** | Also known as the Game Master. Designs the game world, runs sessions, arbitrates rules, determines reactions of NPCs and monsters. |
| **Players** | Every other participant. Each player usually runs a single character. |
| **Player Character (PC)** | The imagined character played by a player. |
| **Party** | The group of PCs who go on adventures together. |
| **Adventure** | A series of one or more game sessions exploring a specific location or engaging with a particular plot. |
| **Campaign** | A series of adventures, usually involving a consistent set of PCs and a consistent game world. |
| **Non-Player Character (NPC)** | Any other character encountered by the PCs. All NPCs are played by the referee. |
| **Monster** | Any creature encountered by the party during an adventure. All monsters are played by the referee. |

### Dice Rolling Notation

| Notation | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| d4 | A four-sided die |
| d6 | A normal six-sided die |
| d8 | An eight-sided die |
| d10 | A ten-sided die |
| d12 | A twelve-sided die |
| d20 | A twenty-sided die |

**Multi-Dice Rolls:** The number of dice is noted before the "d". E.g. "3d6" = roll three six-sided dice and sum the results.

**Modifiers:** Flat modifiers applied after summing all dice. E.g. "3d6+2" = roll 3d6, sum, then add 2.

**"Natural" Rolls:** The number rolled on the die before any modifiers are applied.

**Percentile Rolls (d100):** Mimicked using two d10s—one as "tens", one as "ones". Two 0s = 100.

**X-in-6 Rolls:** Roll 1d6; if the result is less than or equal to the specified chance, the check succeeds.

### Compatibility

*(PDF p.11)*

**The Basic/Expert Rules:** Old-School Essentials is 100% compatible with the 1981 Basic/Expert edition (B/X). Any B/X material can be used directly.

**Other Basic/Expert Games:** Material for any game designed to be compatible with B/X is easy to use with OSE.

**Other Editions:** Material published for all 20th century editions of the world's most popular fantasy RPG is also largely compatible, though some adaptation may be required.

**Heritage:** This book represents a 100% faithful restatement of the B/X rules. Errors in the original have been corrected, and areas of ambiguity or contradiction have been clarified.

## Player Characters

*(PDF p.14–21)*

### Game Statistics

Player characters are described by the following game statistics:

- **Ability Scores:** 6 scores (STR, INT, WIS, DEX, CON, CHA), each rated 3–18.
- **Class:** An adventuring profession defining main abilities. See [Character Classes](#character-classes).
- **Race:** Human unless a demihuman class is selected.
- **Level:** Experience level, starting at 1st.
- **Experience Points (XP):** Tracks advancement; awarded by the referee after successful adventures.
- **Prime Requisite:** The ability score(s) most important to the class. Affects XP gain rate.
- **Alignment:** Law, Neutrality, or Chaos.
- **Hit Points (hp):** Ability to avoid dying. At 0 hp, the character is dead. Rest or healing can restore lost hp, never above maximum.
- **Hit Dice (HD):** Number and type of dice determining maximum hp, based on class and level.
- **Armour Class (AC):** Ability to avoid damage. Lower is better (descending). Ascending AC in square brackets. Unarmoured AC: 9 [10].
- **THAC0:** Attack Roll "to Hit AC 0". Lower is better. Attack bonus in square brackets. E.g. THAC0 15 [+4].
- **Saving Throw Values:** Five categories: Death/Poison (D), Wands (W), Paralysis/Petrification (P), Breath Attacks (B), Spells/Rods/Staves (S).
- **Movement Rate:** Base movement rate with encounter rate in parentheses. Default: 120' (40'). Overland: base ÷ 5 = miles/day. Exploration: base rate in feet per turn (10 minutes).
- **Class Abilities:** Special abilities including armour/weapon proficiencies and languages.

**Ascending AC (Optional Rule):** Higher AC is better. Uses attack bonus instead of THAC0 and attack matrix.

### Creating a Character

*(PDF p.16–17)*

1. **Roll Ability Scores:** Roll 3d6 for each: STR, INT, WIS, DEX, CON, CHA. Sub-par characters (8 or less in every score, or extremely low in one) may be discarded at referee's discretion.
2. **Choose a Class:** Select from available classes, noting minimum ability score requirements. Class determines race (human unless demihuman class chosen).
3. **Adjust Ability Scores:** May raise prime requisite(s) by lowering other non-prime requisite scores. For every 2 points lowered, 1 point may be added to a prime requisite. Only STR, INT, and WIS may be lowered. No score may be lowered below 9. Some classes have additional constraints.
4. **Note Ability Score Modifiers:** See tables below.
5. **Note Attack Values:** THAC0 from class level progression chart. 1st level PCs have THAC0 19 [0].
6. **Note Saving Throws and Class Abilities:** Record class abilities and saving throws. If spellcaster, determine starting spells.
7. **Roll Hit Points:** Roll the die type for chosen class, apply CON modifier. Minimum 1 hp regardless of modifier. *Re-Rolling 1s and 2s (Optional Rule):* Referee may allow re-rolling 1 or 2 results (before CON modifier).
8. **Choose Alignment:** Lawful, Neutral, or Chaotic.
9. **Note Known Languages:** Class determines native languages (always Common + alignment language). High INT grants additional languages.
10. **Buy Equipment:** Start with 3d6 × 10 gold pieces. Consult equipment lists. Class may restrict some equipment.
11. **Note Armour Class:** Determined by armour worn + DEX modifier. Unarmoured: AC 9 [10].
12. **Note Level and XP:** Begin at 1st level with 0 XP.
13. **Name Character.**

### Ability Scores

*(PDF p.18–19)*

**Strength (STR)** — Brawn, muscle, and physical power.
- **Melee:** Applied to attack and damage rolls with melee weapons.
- **Open Doors:** Chance of success forcing open a stuck door.

**Intelligence (INT)** — Learning, memory, and reasoning.
- **Spoken Languages:** Number of languages the character can speak.
- **Literacy:** Ability to read and write native languages.

**Wisdom (WIS)** — Willpower, common sense, perception, and intuition.
- **Magic Saves:** Applied to saving throws versus magical effects (not breath attacks, but may apply to any other category).

**Dexterity (DEX)** — Agility, reflexes, speed, and balance.
- **AC:** Modifies AC (bonus lowers AC, penalty raises it).
- **Missile:** Applied to attack rolls (not damage) with ranged weapons.
- **Initiative:** Modifies initiative roll (if using optional individual initiative).

**Constitution (CON)** — Health, stamina, and endurance.
- **Hit Points:** Applied when rolling hp at each level. Minimum 1 hp per Hit Die regardless of modifier.

**Charisma (CHA)** — Force of personality, persuasiveness, personal magnetism, physical attractiveness, ability to lead.
- **NPC Reactions:** Applied when hiring retainers and interacting with monsters.
- **Max # of Retainers:** Maximum retainers at any one time.
- **Retainer Loyalty:** Determines retainers' loyalty.

### Alignment

*(PDF p.20)*

All beings adhere to one of three alignments:

- **Law:** Lawful beings believe in truth and justice. They follow laws, believe all things must adhere to order, and will choose the good of a larger group over the individual.
- **Neutrality:** Neutral beings believe in balance between Law and Chaos. They tend to do what serves themselves, and generally will not put others' needs ahead of their own.
- **Chaos:** Chaotic beings are in direct opposition to Law. They tend to act in "evil" ways, are utterly selfish, believe in chance, and hold that there is no innate order to life.

**Revealing Alignment:** The player must inform the referee but does not have to tell other players.

**Role-Playing Alignment:** Players should adhere to their chosen alignment. The referee may reassign alignment if behaviour deviates too much, and deviation may be penalised.

### Languages

*(PDF p.21)*

Native languages are determined by class. These always include **Common** and the character's **alignment language**. High INT grants additional languages.

**The Common Tongue:** Widespread among intelligent species. All PC races and many monsters speak Common. The referee may rule that different cultures have different languages.

**Alignment Languages:** All intelligent beings know a secret, unwritten language of gestures, signs, and code words associated with their alignment. Beings of the same alignment can communicate this way. Others will recognise an alignment language is being used but won't understand. Changing alignment means forgetting the former language and learning the new one.

**Other Languages:** Many demihuman and monster species have their own language. PCs with high INT may choose from the following:

## Character Classes

*(PDF p.24–37)*

Seven classic character classes. Demihumans (Dwarf, Elf, Halfling) use the race-as-class system—race and class are one and the same.

### Cleric

*(PDF p.24–25)*

Clerics are adventurers who have sworn to serve a deity. They are trained for battle and channel the power of their deity.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** WIS
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Any blunt weapons (club, mace, sling, staff, war hammer)
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Combat:** Clerics can use all types of armour. Strict holy doctrine prevents use of weapons with a sharp, cutting edge or stabbing point.

**Divine Magic:** (See Magic, p68 for full details on divine magic.)
- **Holy symbol:** A cleric must carry a holy symbol.
- **Deity disfavour:** Clerics must be faithful to the tenets of their alignment, clergy, and religion. Falling from favour may incur penalties.
- **Magical research:** A cleric of any level may spend time and money on magical research. At 9th level, they can create magic items.
- **Spell casting:** From 2nd level, clerics may pray to receive spells. The spell list is on p72.
- **Using magic items:** Clerics can use magic scrolls of spells on their list and items usable only by divine spell casters (e.g. some magic staves).

**Turning the Undead:** Clerics can invoke the power of their deity to repel undead. Roll 2d6 and consult the Turning Table. On success, roll 2d6 for number of HD affected.

- **Turned undead:** Leave the area if possible and will not harm or contact the cleric.
- **Destroyed undead (D result):** Instantly and permanently annihilated.
- **Excess:** Rolled HD insufficient to affect a monster are wasted.
- **Minimum effect:** At least one undead monster is always affected on success.
- **Mixed groups:** Lowest HD undead are affected first.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** May establish or build a stronghold at half normal price (divine intervention). Attracts 5d6 × 10 fighters of level 1–2 as followers who never check morale.

### Dwarf

*(PDF p.26–27)*

Dwarves are stout, bearded demihumans, about 4' tall and weighing about 150 pounds. They typically live underground and love fine craftsmanship, gold, hearty food, and strong drink. They have skin, hair, and eye colours in earth tones. Known for stubbornness and practicality, dwarves are hardy and have strong resistance to magic, as reflected in their saving throws.

**Requirements:** Minimum CON 9
**Prime Requisite:** STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d8
**Maximum Level:** 12
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Small or normal sized (cannot use longbows or two-handed swords)
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Dwarvish, Gnomish, Goblin, Kobold

**Combat:** Dwarves can use all types of armour. Due to short height, they can only use small or normal sized weapons.

**Detect Construction Tricks:** 2-in-6 chance of detecting new construction, sliding walls, or sloping passages when searching.

**Detect Room Traps:** 2-in-6 chance of detecting non-magical room traps when searching.

**Infravision:** 60'.

**Listening at Doors:** 2-in-6 chance of hearing noises.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** May create an underground stronghold that attracts dwarves from far and wide. Dwarves of the character's clan will be attracted to live under their roof. Clans may collaborate in times of war or disaster. May only hire dwarven mercenaries; specialists and retainers of any race may be hired.

### Elf

*(PDF p.28–29)*

Elves are slender, fey demihumans with pointed ears, typically weighing about 120 pounds and between 5 and 5½ feet tall. They seldom live in human settlements, preferring to feast and make merry in the woods. Masters of both sword and spell, elves are fascinated by spells and beautifully constructed magic items.

**Requirements:** Minimum INT 9
**Prime Requisite:** INT and STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 10
**Armour:** Any, including shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Elvish, Gnoll, Hobgoblin, Orcish

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** An elf with at least 13 INT and STR gains +5% XP. An elf with INT 16+ and STR 13+ gains +10% XP.

**Arcane Magic:** (See Magic, p68 for full details on arcane magic.)
- **Magical research:** An elf of any level may spend time and money on magical research. At 9th level, they can create magic items.
- **Spell casting:** Elves carry spell books. The level progression table shows spells in book and spells memorisable. A 1st level elf has one spell in their book. The spell list is the same as magic-users (p73).
- **Using magic items:** Elves can use magic scrolls of spells on their list and items usable only by arcane spell casters (e.g. magic wands).

**Combat:** Elves can use all types of weapons and armour.

**Detect Secret Doors:** 2-in-6 chance when actively searching.

**Immunity to Ghoul Paralysis:** Completely unaffected by ghoul paralysis.

**Infravision:** 60'.

**Listening at Doors:** 2-in-6 chance of hearing noises.

**After Reaching 9th Level:** May construct a stronghold in the depths of the forest, blending with the natural environment (treetops, beside a valley, behind a waterfall). Construction costs as much as stone work due to exquisite craftsmanship. Forest animals within 5 miles become friends (warn of intruders, carry messages) in exchange for protection. May only hire elven mercenaries; specialists and retainers of any race may be hired.

### Halfling

*(PDF p.32–33)*

Halflings are small, rotund demihumans with furry feet and curly hair, weighing about 60 pounds and around 3' tall. Friendly and welcoming, they love the comforts of home and are not known for their bravery. Halflings who gain treasure through adventuring often use their wealth in pursuit of a quiet, comfortable life.

**Requirements:** Minimum CON 9, minimum DEX 9
**Prime Requisite:** DEX and STR
**Hit Dice:** 1d6
**Maximum Level:** 8
**Armour:** Any appropriate to size, including shields
**Weapons:** Any appropriate to size (cannot use longbows or two-handed swords)
**Languages:** Alignment, Common, Halfling

**Prime Requisite XP Bonus:** A halfling with at least 13 in one prime requisite gains +5% XP. If both DEX and STR are 13 or higher, +10% bonus.

**Combat:** Halflings can use all types of armour (tailored to size) and any weapon appropriate to their stature.

**Defensive Bonus:** +2 bonus to AC when attacked by large opponents (greater than human-sized).

**Hiding:**
- In woods or undergrowth: 90% chance of success.
- In dungeons: 2-in-6 chance to hide in shadows or behind cover. Must be motionless.

**Initiative Bonus (Optional Rule):** +1 to initiative rolls (if using optional individual initiative).

**Listening at Doors:** 2-in-6 chance of hearing noises.

**Missile Attack Bonus:** +1 bonus to attack rolls with all missile weapons.

**Stronghold:** Any time a halfling wishes (and has sufficient money), they may build a stronghold forming the basis of a new community (called a Shire). The leader is called the Sheriff.

### Thief

*(PDF p.36–37)*

Thieves are adventurers who live by their skills of deception and stealth. Their range of unique skills makes them very handy companions in adventures. However, thieves are not always to be trusted.

**Requirements:** None
**Prime Requisite:** DEX
**Hit Dice:** 1d4
**Maximum Level:** 14
**Armour:** Leather, no shields
**Weapons:** Any
**Languages:** Alignment, Common

**Adjust Ability Scores:** In step 3 of character creation, thieves may not lower STR.

**Back-Stab:** When attacking an unaware opponent from behind, +4 bonus to hit and double damage dealt.

**Combat:** Thieves cannot wear armour heavier than leather and cannot use shields. They can use any weapon.

**Read Languages:** From 4th level, 80% chance to read non-magical text in any language (including dead languages and basic codes). On failure, cannot try that text again until gaining a level.

**Scroll Use:** From 10th level, can cast arcane spells from scrolls. 10% chance of error: spell does not function as expected and creates an unusual or deleterious effect.

**Thief Skills:**

- **Climb Sheer Surfaces (CS):** Roll per 100' climbed. Failure = fall at halfway point, taking falling damage.
- **Find or Remove Treasure Traps (TR):** One roll to find, another to remove. May only attempt once per trap.
- **Hear Noise (HN):** In quiet environments, attempt to listen at a door or hear approaching sounds.
- **Hide in Shadows (HS):** Must be motionless—attacking or moving while hiding is not possible.
- **Move Silently (MS):** Attempt to sneak past enemies unnoticed.
- **Open Locks (OL):** Requires thieves' tools. One attempt per lock; on failure, cannot retry until gaining a level.
- **Pick Pockets (PP):** If victim is above 5th level, –5% per level above 5th. Always at least 1% chance of failure. Roll of more than twice the required percentage means the theft is noticed.

**Rolling Skill Checks:** Hear Noise is rolled on 1d6 (success if within listed range). All other skills are rolled on d% (success if ≤ listed percentage).

**Player Knowledge:** The referee should roll Hear Noise, Hide in Shadows, and Move Silently on the player's behalf (the thief doesn't immediately know if the attempt succeeded).

**After Reaching 9th Level:** Can establish a thief den, attracting 2d6 apprentices of 1st level. If any are arrested or killed, they cannot be replaced. A successful thief might use these followers to start a Thieves' Guild.

## Advancement

*(PDF p.38–40)*

### Experience

All characters who make it through an adventure alive receive experience points (XP), awarded by the referee. XP is gained from two sources: **treasure recovered** and **monsters defeated**.

**Prime Requisite Modifiers:** Characters receive an XP bonus or penalty based on their prime requisite score (see [Prime Requisite Modifiers](#ability-score-modifier-tables)).

### Levelling Up

When a character gains enough XP to reach the next experience level, consult the class description and note improvements in saving throws, attack probabilities, spells per day, and other class abilities. If Hit Dice increase, roll a new Hit Die of the specified type and add to maximum hp.

**Maximum XP in One Session:** Characters cannot advance more than one level in one session. Excess XP is lost, leaving the character at 1 XP below the total for the next level.

### Level Titles

In some campaigns, characters gain titles as they advance. These may be granted by guilds, colleges, or temples. Titles vary by campaign world; the following may be used as inspiration:

- **Cleric:** Acolyte, Adept, Priest(ess), Vicar, Curate, Elder, Bishop, Lama, Matriarch (Patriarch).
- **Dwarf:** Dwarven Veteran, Dwarven Warrior, Dwarven Swordmaster, Dwarven Hero, Dwarven Swashbuckler, Dwarven Myrmidon, Dwarven Champion, Dwarven Superhero, Dwarven Lord (Lady).
- **Elf:** Medium/Veteran, Seer/Warrior, Conjurer/Swordmaster, Magician/Hero, Enchanter(Enchantress)/Swashbuckler, Warlock(Witch)/Myrmidon, Sorcerer(Sorceress)/Champion, Necromancer/Superhero, Wizard/Lord (Lady).
- **Fighter:** Veteran, Warrior, Swordmaster, Hero, Swashbuckler, Myrmidon, Champion, Superhero, Lord (Lady).
- **Halfling:** Halfling Veteran, Halfling Warrior, Halfling Swordmaster, Halfling Hero, Halfling Swashbuckler, Halfling Myrmidon, Halfling Champion, Sheriff.
- **Magic-User:** Medium, Seer, Conjurer, Magician, Enchanter (Enchantress), Warlock (Witch), Sorcerer (Sorceress), Necromancer, Wizard.
- **Thief:** Apprentice, Footpad, Robber, Burglar, Cutpurse, Sharper, Pilferer, Thief, Master Thief.

### High-Level Play (Optional Rule)

*(PDF p.39)*

Human character classes have a maximum level of 14. If the referee allows continued play beyond this, human characters may advance to a maximum of 36th level:

- **Clerics:** +100,000 XP per level beyond 14th, +1 hp per level. Higher-level spells become available.
- **Fighters:** +120,000 XP per level beyond 14th, +2 hp per level. At 20th level: 2 attacks/round; 25th level: 3 attacks/round; 30th level: 4 attacks/round.
- **Magic-Users:** +150,000 XP per level beyond 14th, +1 hp per level. Higher-level spells become available.
- **Thieves:** +120,000 XP per level beyond 14th, +2 hp per level. Additional skills at high levels (climbing upside down/horizontally, voice mimicry, ventriloquism, other deception skills).

## Vehicles and Mounts

*(PDF p.48–57)*

### Rules for Vehicles

**Game Statistics:**

- **Hull Points (hp):** Structural integrity. At 0 hp, the vehicle is destroyed.
- **Armour Class (AC):** Ability to resist damage.
- **Movement Rate:** Base movement rate with encounter rate in parentheses (encounter = 1/3 base).
- **Cargo Capacity:** Maximum load in coins.
- **Required Crew:** Number of people or animals required for normal operation.
- **Passengers/Mercenaries:** Some vessels have extra space for passengers or mercenaries beyond crew.

**Seaworthiness:** Seaworthy vessels can operate on the high seas. Unseaworthy vessels are restricted to rivers, lakes, or coastal waters.

**Damaging Vehicles:**
- **Normal attacks:** Do not inflict hull damage unless noted.
- **Magical attacks:** 1 hull damage per 5 hp of normal damage.
- **Giant monsters:** 1 hull damage per 5 hp of normal damage.
- **Mounted weaponry:** Inflicts hull damage directly.

**Effects of Hull Damage:**
- For every 10% reduction from max hp, movement rate reduces by an equal percentage.
- **Destruction (0 hp):** Vehicle loses structural integrity in 1d10 rounds (e.g. sinks). Mounted weaponry no longer functional.

**Repairs:**
- *In a workshop:* Full repair by experienced technicians.
- *In the field:* Crew can repair up to half of damage sustained; remaining requires a workshop.
- *Time:* 5 crew-members per 1 hull point per turn. Crew repairing cannot take other actions.

**Boarding:**
- *Forceful boarding:* 2-in-6 chance of manoeuvring into position. Vehicles clamped with grappling hooks.
- *Mutual boarding intent:* Automatic success.
- *Boarding characters:* –2 penalty to attack rolls and AC for one round.

**Rowed Water Vessels:**
- Some rowed vessels have increased encounter movement rate (great effort by oarsmen, unsustainable long-term).
- For every 10% reduction in available rowing crew, rowing speed reduces by equal percentage.

## Hired Help

*(PDF p.58–65)*

### Retainers

Retainers are NPCs hired by characters to accompany them on adventures.

**Limit per PC:** Determined by CHA score (see [Charisma Modifiers](#ability-score-modifier-tables)).

**Duties:** Retainers are not mindless slaves. They will share the party's risks but will not willingly act as battle fodder. Abused retainers will warn others, making future hiring difficult.

**Class and Level:** Any class (including normal humans of level 0), but must be equal or lower level to the hiring PC.

#### Recruitment

Potential retainers may be found in drinking establishments or by posting help wanted notices. The referee plays applicant NPCs during negotiation.

#### Wages and Upkeep

Pay rates depend on:
- **Skill level:** More experienced retainers demand higher pay.
- **Competition:** Many applicants = lower rates; few applicants = higher rates.

**Standard Rate:** Guaranteed fee (per day or per adventure) plus a share of treasure (at least a half share). Example: 1gp/day + half share of treasure.

**Upkeep:** Hiring PC pays for food, lodgings, and any new gear, weapons, or mounts.

**Shares of Treasure:** Fractional shares calculated by dividing treasure by total shares. Example: 5 PCs (full shares) + 1 retainer (half share) find 2,750gp → 2,750 ÷ 5.5 = 500gp per PC, 250gp for retainer.

#### Applicant Reactions

Roll 2d6 modified by:
- CHA reaction modifier
- Generosity: +1/+2 for generous offers, –1/–2 for poor offers
- Reputation: –1/–2 if hiring PC has a bad reputation

| 2d6 | Result |
|:---:|--------|
| 2 or less | Ill will (–1 to further hiring rolls in same area) |
| 3–5 | Offer refused |
| 6–8 | Roll again |
| 9–11 | Offer accepted |
| 12 or more | Offer accepted, +1 loyalty |

#### Experience

Retainers acquire XP like PCs and can advance in level.

- **XP penalty:** Retainers receive –50% XP (they follow instructions rather than solve problems).
- **Normal humans:** When a level 0 retainer gains XP, they must choose an adventuring class.

#### Loyalty

Loyalty rating determined by hiring PC's CHA. May be adjusted by referee:
- **Bonuses:** For PCs who are particularly good to the retainer (e.g. extra treasure).
- **Penalties:** For cruel or deceitful treatment.

**Loyalty Checks:** Roll 2d6 ≤ loyalty rating = success.
- **Peril:** When exposed to particularly dangerous situations. Failure → retainer flees.
- **After an adventure:** Failure → retainer will not work for the PC again.

## Strongholds

*(PDF p.66–69)*

### Construction

1. **Permission:** May need to secure permission from existing authority (not required in uncharted wilderness).
2. **Clear land:** All monsters in a 6-mile area (one hex on a typical wilderness map) must be killed or driven off.
3. **Design:** Player creates a plan and calculates costs.
4. **Review:** Referee approves the plans.
5. **Hire engineers:** 1 engineer per 100,000gp cost.
6. **Construction:** One day of game time per 500gp of total price.
7. **Prepare for settlers:** Clear a wider area of dangers to found a dominion.

**Building in Towns:**
- Permission: Local rulers may allow building but unlikely to permit castles/fortifications.
- Stone buildings: Cost reduced by 60%.
- Wooden buildings: Cost reduced by 80%.

### Domain Management

**Maintaining Cleared Lands:** Mercenary patrols cover up to 18 miles radius around a stronghold (6 miles in inhospitable terrain: swamp, mountains, thick jungle). Larger areas require additional garrisons.

**Settlers:** PC must ensure safety of surrounding lands and fund construction of buildings (accommodation, commercial, transport). Advertising may be needed. Referee determines costs and settlers attracted.

**Taxation:** 10gp per settler per year.

## Magic

*(PDF p.70–76)*

### Spells

Some character classes can memorize and cast magical spells. A spell consists of a pattern of magical energy memorized in a character's mind. When cast, the spell is erased until memorized again.

- **Arcane and divine magic:** Spells are either granted by a deity (divine) or learned by esoteric study (arcane).
- **Spell list:** Class determines which spells a character can cast.
- **Spells in memory:** Level determines how many spells can be memorized at one time.
- **Reversible spells:** Some spells are reversible, as noted in descriptions.

### Memorizing Spells

- **Rest and time:** After an uninterrupted night's sleep. One hour to memorize all spells.
- **Duplicate spells:** A character may memorize the same spell more than once, if able to memorize more than one spell of that level.

### Casting Spells

- **Once only:** Knowledge erased from mind until memorized again.
- **Freedom:** Must be able to speak and move hands. Cannot cast if gagged, bound, or in magical silence.
- **Line of sight:** Target must be visible to the caster (unless noted otherwise).

### Spell Effects

- **Selecting targets:** If not specified, the referee decides how targets are chosen.
- **Concentration:** Some spells require concentration. Any other action or being attacked ends concentration (unless stated otherwise).
- **Cumulative effects:** Multiple spells cannot increase the same ability (e.g. attack rolls, AC, damage, saves). Different abilities can be combined. Spells can combine with magic items.

### Arcane Magic

- **Memorizing:** From spell books; limited to spells in the book, which must be at hand.
- **Reversing:** Normal or reversed form chosen when memorized. Both forms may be memorized if the character can memorize more than one spell of that level.

### Divine Magic

- **Memorizing:** Through prayer. May choose any spell in the class list of appropriate level.
- **Reversing:** Reversed version cast by speaking words/performing gestures backwards when cast.
- **Deity disfavour:** Falling from favour may result in penalties (–1 attack, fewer spells, perilous quest). Regaining favour requires a great deed (donating items/gold, building a temple, converting people, vanquishing enemies).
- **Spells and deity alignment:**
  - **Lawful:** Only use reversed spells in dire circumstances.
  - **Chaotic:** Usually use reversed spells; normal versions only to benefit allies.
  - **Neutral:** Favour either normal or reversed spells depending on the deity (no deity favours both).

### Spell Books

- **Number of spells:** Exactly the number the character can memorize (per class and level).
- **Beginning spells:** Start with as many spells as can be memorized. Referee may choose or allow player to select.
- **Adding spells:** When memorizable spells increase (e.g. level up):
  - *Mentoring:* Consult an arcane guild or mentor (~1 week). Spells determined by referee.
  - *Research:* Via Magical Research.
- **Lost spell books:** Can be rewritten at 1 week and 1,000gp per spell level. Requires complete concentration.
- **Captured spell books:** Only readable by the owner without magic.

### Magical Research

*(PDF p.73)*

Spell casters can research new spells, magic items, and other magical effects. Requires time, money, and sometimes rare ingredients. Minimum 15% chance of failure.

**Creating New Spells:**
- Player describes spell and effects; referee determines if possible and spell level.
- Character must be able to cast spells of the resulting level.
- Cost: 2 weeks + 1,000gp per spell level.

**Creating Magic Items:**
- Player describes item and effects; referee determines feasibility and required materials.
- **Divine casters:** May only create items they can use.
- **Arcane casters:** May create any item except those only usable by divine casters.
- **Materials:** Often require rare components (gems, rare animal/monster ingredients).
- **Cost (duplicating spell effects):** 1 week + 500gp per level of mimicked spell. Multi-use items multiply cost by number of uses.
- **Other items:** 10,000–100,000gp, 1 month to 1 year. Examples: 20 arrows +1 (10,000gp, 1 month), plate mail +1 (10,000gp, 6 months), crystal ball (30,000gp, 6 months), ring of x-ray vision (100,000gp, 1 year).

**Other Magical Research:** Sanctifying holy sites, creating traps/constructs/portals, etc. May require specific spells, rare ingredients, periodic renewal.

## Adventuring

*(PDF p.112–135)*

### Time, Weight, and Movement

*(PDF p.112–113)*

**Tracking Time:** The referee tracks game time (distinct from real time). In dungeons, turns are tracked; in wilderness, days.

**Resources:** Referee tracks consumption of food, water, light sources, spell durations, etc.

**Tracking Movement:** Referee tracks party location on a prepared map (usually secret from players). Movement rate determines travel time.

**Group Movement Rate:** Determined by the slowest member.

#### Measurements

| Unit | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Turn** | 10 minutes of game time. 6 turns per hour. Used in dungeon exploration. |
| **Round** | 10 seconds of game time. 60 rounds per turn. Used in encounters and combat. |

**Distance:** Imperial units — inches ("), feet ('), yards, miles. 1 foot = 12 inches; 1 yard = 3 feet; 1 mile = 1,760 yards = 5,280 feet.

**Weight:** Measured in coins. 10 coins = 1 pound.

### Encumbrance (Optional Rule)

Tracks weight and bulk of gear and treasure. Maximum load: 1,600 coins (cannot move above this).

#### Treasure Encumbrance

| Treasure | Weight (Coins) |
|----------|---------------:|
| Coin (any type) | 1 |
| Gem | 1 |
| Jewellery (1 piece) | 10 |
| Potion | 10 |
| Rod | 20 |
| Scroll | 1 |
| Staff | 40 |
| Wand | 10 |

**Option 1 — Basic Encumbrance:** Only treasure weight tracked. Movement rate by armour worn:

| Armour Worn | Without Treasure | Carrying Treasure |
|-------------|:----------------:|:-----------------:|
| Unarmoured | 120' (40') | 90' (30') |
| Light armour | 90' (30') | 60' (20') |
| Heavy armour | 60' (20') | 30' (10') |

**Option 2 — Detailed Encumbrance:** Track treasure + armour + weapons weight. Misc. gear = 80 coins.

| Encumbrance | Movement Rate |
|-------------|:-------------:|
| Up to 400 coins | 120' (40') |
| Up to 600 coins | 90' (30') |
| Up to 800 coins | 60' (20') |
| Up to 1,600 coins | 30' (10') |

### Ability Checks

*(PDF p.114)*

Roll 1d20 ≤ ability score = success. Modifiers: –4 (easy) to +4 (very difficult). Natural 1 = success; natural 20 = failure.

### Damage, Healing, and Death

- **Death:** 0 hp or less = dead.
- **Destruction of items:** Killed by destructive spell/attack = equipment destroyed.
- **Magic item survival:** Save using character's values. Combat bonus items may add bonus to save.
- **Natural healing:** 1d3 hp per full day of complete rest. Interrupted rest = no healing.
- **Magical healing:** Instantaneous. Can combine with natural healing.

### Saving Throws

*(PDF p.115)*

Five categories:
- **Death or Poison (D):** Death ray or poison.
- **Wands (W):** Magical wand effects.
- **Paralysis or Petrification (P):** Paralysis or petrification effects.
- **Breath Attacks (B):** Dragon or monster breath.
- **Spells, Rods, or Staves (S):** Baneful spells or rod/staff effects.

**Rolling:** 1d20 ≥ saving throw value = success.
- **Damaging effects:** Success = half damage.
- **Other effects:** Success = entirely avoided/negated.
- **Poison:** Failed save usually fatal. Damage from poisonous attacks is unaffected by save result.

### Hazards and Challenges

*(PDF p.116–117)*

- **Climbing:** Referee may require DEX ability check. Sheer surfaces normally impossible without special equipment or class abilities.
- **Darkness:** Light sources enable 30' radius normal vision.
  - *Infravision:* Sees heat energy (living things bright, cold objects black). Cannot read. 60' range for monsters (unless noted). Disrupted by visible light or large heat sources.
  - *Light and surprise:* Carrying light in darkness usually prevents surprise.
- **Falling:** 1d6 damage per 10' fallen.
- **Losing direction:** Follows trails/roads/landmarks = safe. Untracked regions = risk. Chance depends on terrain (see Wilderness Adventuring).
- **Starvation:** Full day without food/water → referee may penalise attacks, movement, require more rest, or deduct hp.
- **Swimming:** Half normal movement rate. All characters assumed to know how to swim. Drowning risk judged by referee based on circumstances (rough water, armour, heavy loads).
- **Wandering monsters:** Checked periodically. Usually 1-in-6 chance. Noise/light may increase; quiet hiding may decrease.

### Dungeon Adventuring

*(PDF p.118–119)*

**Doors:**
- *Secret doors:* Search 10'×10' area. 1-in-6 chance of finding (some classes have better chance).
- *Stuck doors:* Force open based on STR. Failed attempt eliminates surprise.
- *Locked doors:* Picked by lock-pick proficient characters or opened by magic.
- *Swinging shut:* Doors tend to close after passing. Use iron spikes to wedge open.
- *Monsters and doors:* Dungeon-dwelling monsters can usually open doors (even stuck ones) unless blocked, magically closed, or spiked.
- *Listening at doors:* 1-in-6 chance. Referee rolls in secret. One attempt per character per door.

**Movement:**
- *Exploring unknown areas:* Base movement rate in feet per turn (very slow, accounting for exploration, mapping, stealth).
- *Familiar areas:* Referee may allow faster movement (e.g. 3× base rate per turn).

**Sequence of Play Per Turn:**
1. Wandering monsters: Referee checks.
2. Actions: Party decides (move, search, listen, enter rooms).
3. Description: Referee describes; if monsters encountered, see Encounters.
4. End of turn: Update time records (light sources, spell durations, rest needs).

**Resting:** Must rest 1 turn every hour. Penalty for not resting: –1 to attack and damage until rested.

**Traps:**
- *Treasure traps:* Small traps on items (e.g. poison needle).
- *Room traps:* Affect anyone entering an area (e.g. pit).
- *Triggering:* 2-in-6 chance of springing when triggering action occurs. Damage is automatic (no attack roll). Monsters may bypass.
- *Searching for room traps:* 1-in-6 chance (some classes better). Search 10'×10' area.
- *Searching for treasure traps:* Only by characters with the class ability. Chance noted in class description.

**Searching:** Takes 1 turn. Referee rolls in secret. One attempt per area/item per character.

**Wandering Monsters:** Check every 2 turns. 1-in-6 chance. Encountered 2d6 × 10 feet away.

### Wilderness Adventuring

*(PDF p.120–121)*

**Distance:** Ranges and movement rates measured in yards (tripled). Spell area effects still in feet.

**Flying:** Double overland distance per day. Terrain does not affect. Flying mounts: 3 HD = half-human rider, 6 HD = human, 12 HD = horse, 24 HD = elephant.

**Foraging:** Alongside normal movement. 1-in-6 chance/day of finding food for 1d6 beings.

**Hunting:** Sole activity for the day. 1-in-6 chance of encountering suitable animals (in addition to normal random encounters).

**Sequence of Play Per Day:**
1. Decide course.
2. Losing direction: Referee determines.
3. Wandering monsters: Referee checks.
4. Description: Referee describes terrain; encounters per Encounters procedure.
5. End of day: Update records (rations, spells, rest).

**Losing Direction:** Chance per terrain:
- Clear, grasslands: 1-in-6
- Barren lands, hills, mountains, woods: 2-in-6
- Desert, jungle, swamp: 3-in-6

**Overland Travel:** Miles/day = base movement rate ÷ 5. Terrain modifiers:
- Broken lands, desert, forest, hills: 33% slower.
- Jungle, mountains, swamp: 50% slower.
- Maintained roads: 50% faster.

**Forced March:** +50% distance. Must rest a full day after.

**Resting:** 1 day per 6 days of travel. Penalty: –1 to hit and damage until rested.

**Surprise:** If surprised by 3+ monsters, they may have encircled the party.

**Visibility:** Usually 3 miles in open terrain (may be reduced or increased).

**Wandering Monsters:** Check once per day (up to 3–4). Distance: 4d6 × 10 yards (1d4 × 10 if surprised). Chance by terrain:
- City, clear, grasslands, settled: 1-in-6
- Aerial, barren, desert, forest, hills: 2-in-6
- Jungle, mountains, swamp: 3-in-6

### Waterborne Adventuring

*(PDF p.122–123)*

**Distance:** Ranges/movement in yards (tripled). Spell areas in feet.

**Losing Direction:**
- With navigator: 2-in-6
- Without navigator: 100% on open seas; 2-in-6 within sight of land

**Surprise:** Aquatic monsters usually not surprised by ships (unless thick fog etc.).

**Travel:** Miles/day = base movement rate ÷ 5.
- *River travel:* Currents modify distance by 1d6+6 miles/day (downstream increase, upstream decrease).
- *Sailing:* Affected by wind conditions.

**Visibility:** Land spotted at 24 miles on clear day. Ships at 300 yards (40 yards in dense fog).

**Sequence of Play Per Day:**
1. Decide course.
2. Losing direction.
3. Weather: Wind conditions.
4. Wandering monsters.
5. Description and encounters.
6. End of day.

**Wandering Monsters:** Check once/day (up to 3–4). 2-in-6 on oceans/rivers, 3-in-6 in swamps. Distance: 4d6 × 10 yards (1d4 × 10 if surprised). May occur on water or land (if docked).

**Wind Conditions (2d6):**
- 2: No wind; sailing impossible.
- 3–11: Normal sailing possible.
- 12: Gale or storm; sailing speed tripled.

#### Variable Wind Conditions (Optional Rule)

| 2d6 | Wind | Effect |
|:---:|------|--------|
| 2 | No wind | Sailing impossible. Oar movement at 1/3 rate. |
| 3 | Faint breeze | Sailing rate 1/3 normal. |
| 4 | Gentle breeze | Sailing rate 1/2 normal. |
| 5 | Moderate breeze | Sailing rate 2/3 normal. |
| 6–8 | Fresh breeze | Normal sailing rate. |
| 9 | Strong breeze | Sailing rate +1/3. |
| 10 | High wind | Sailing rate +1/2. |
| 11 | Near gale | Sailing rate doubled. See Near Gales. |
| 12 | Gale or storm | Sailing rate tripled. See Gales and Storms. |

**Near Gales:** Seaworthy vessels 10% chance of taking on water; unseaworthy 20%. Taking on water reduces speed by 1/3 until repaired at dock.

**Gales and Storms:**
- *Seaworthy:* Travel at 3× speed in random direction (1d6). 75% chance of wrecking if land encountered.
- *Unseaworthy:* 80% chance of sinking. May attempt to beach if in sight of land (automatic on clear shore; 2-in-6 chance of safe harbour otherwise).

### Encounters

*(PDF p.124–125)*

**Surprise:** Each unaware side rolls 1d6. Result of 1–2 = surprised. Both surprised = momentary confusion. One side surprised = opponent gets 1 free round.

**Encounter Distance:**
- Dungeon: 2d6 × 10 feet.
- Wilderness: 4d6 × 10 yards (1d4 × 10 if surprised).
- Waterborne: 4d6 × 10 yards (1d4 × 10 if surprised).

**Initiative:** Each side rolls 1d6 at start of each round. Highest acts first. Ties = re-roll or simultaneous.

**Encounter Sequence:**
1. Surprise.
2. Encounter distance.
3. Initiative (non-surprised sides).
4. Actions.
5. Conclusion (1 turn elapsed).

**Monster Reaction Roll (2d6, modified by CHA):**

| 2d6 | Result |
|:---:|--------|
| 2 or less | Hostile, attacks |
| 3–5 | Unfriendly, may attack |
| 6–8 | Neutral, uncertain |
| 9–11 | Indifferent, uninterested |
| 12 or more | Friendly, helpful |

**Common Actions:** Combat, Evasion, Parley.

**Encounter Movement Rate:** 1/3 base movement rate per round (feet in dungeon, yards in wilderness). Maximum 60 rounds (1 turn).

### Evasion and Pursuit

*(PDF p.126–127)*

#### In the Dungeon

**Evasion:**
- Fleeing side faster → automatic success (unless forced to stop).
- Fleeing side not faster → pursuit occurs.

**Pursuit (Dungeon):**
- Time in rounds, running at full speed (base movement rate in feet per round).
- Monsters usually stop pursuit when PCs leave line of sight.
- Dropping treasure: 3-in-6 chance treasure-loving monsters stop to collect.
- Dropping food: 3-in-6 chance hungry/unintelligent monsters stop.
- Obstacles (burning oil etc.) may slow/stop pursuit.
- Exhaustion after 30 rounds: –2 to attacks, damage, AC. Lasts until 3 full turns of rest.
- Mapping not possible while running.

#### In the Wilderness

**Evasion:** Percentile roll based on relative group size. Surprise affects evasion:
- No surprise → use Wilderness Evasion Base Chance table.
- Surprised side → generally cannot evade (referee may allow small chance).
- Surprising side → automatic evasion.

**Wilderness Evasion Base Chance:**

| Fleeing Group | 1 Pursuer | 2–3 Pursuers | 4+ Pursuers |
|--------------:|:---------:|:------------:|:-----------:|
| 1–4 | 50% | 70% | 90% |
| 5–12 | 35% (1–3) | 50% (4–8) | 70% (9+) |
| 13–24 | 25% (1–6) | 35% (7–16) | 50% (17+) |
| 25+ | 10% (1–10) | 25% (11–30) | 35% (31+) |

**Evasion Modifiers:** Pursuing side 2× faster: –25%. Fleeing side 2× faster: +25%. Environment may modify (e.g. dense woods +25%). Minimum 5% chance.

**Pursuit (Wilderness):** Day by day:
1. Fleeing side moves in random direction (no mapping).
2. If pursuing side faster: 50% chance of catching up. If caught, pursuit ends.
3. Fleeing side decides to continue or stop. If continuing, another evasion attempt. Failure returns to step 1.

#### Waterborne

**Evasion:** Based on movement rate difference:

| Fleeing Side's MV vs Pursuer | Evasion Chance |
|:-----------------------------|:--------------:|
| Faster than pursuer | 80% |
| 0'–30'/round slower | 50% |
| 31'–60'/round slower | 40% |
| 61'–90'/round slower | 35% |
| 91'–120'/round slower | 25% |
| 121'+/round slower | 10% |

Success = pursuer cannot catch up until next day (only if random encounter indicates). Failure = pursuit.

**Pursuit (Waterborne):** Time in rounds. Start at normal encounter distance. Gap closes by speed difference each round (minimum 30'/round).

## Combat

*(PDF p.130–135)*

### Combat Sequence Per Round

1. **Declare spells and melee movement.**
2. **Initiative:** Each side rolls 1d6.
3. **Winning side acts:**
   a. Monster morale
   b. Movement
   c. Missile attacks
   d. Spell casting
   e. Melee attacks
4. **Other sides act** in initiative order.

### Initiative

- Roll 1d6 per side at start of each round.
- Highest goes first. Ties: re-roll or simultaneous.
- **Slow weapons:** Two-handed melee and some missile weapons → always act last.
- **Individual Initiative (Optional):** Roll per individual, modified by DEX. Referee may assign initiative modifiers to fast/slow monsters.

### Movement in Combat

- **Outside melee:** Up to encounter movement rate per round. Maximum 60 rounds.
- **In melee:**
  - *Fighting withdrawal:* Move backwards at up to half encounter rate. Clear path required.
  - *Retreat:* Turn and flee at full encounter rate. Cannot attack this round. Opponent gets +2 to attacks and ignores retreating combatant's shield AC bonus.

### Missile Attacks

Possible when opponents are more than 5' apart.

**Range Modifiers:**
- Short range: +1
- Medium range: No modifier
- Long range: –1
- Beyond long range: Not possible

**Targets Behind Cover:** Complete = cannot hit. Partial = –1 to –4 penalty.

### Spell Casting in Combat

- Must speak and move hands (no casting if gagged, bound, or in silence).
- Sole action for the round. No movement.
- Target must be visible (unless noted).
- **Disruption:** If caster loses initiative and is successfully attacked or fails a save before their turn, the spell fails and is lost from memory.

### Melee Attacks

Possible when opponents are 5' or less apart.

### Attack Rolls

1. Roll 1d20.
2. Apply modifiers (STR for melee; DEX, range, cover for missile).
3. Look up result in attack matrix row for attacker's THAC0. Column = AC hit.
4. If hit AC ≤ opponent's AC: hit! Roll damage.

**Ascending AC (Optional):** 1d20 + modifiers + attack bonus. If total ≥ opponent's AAC: hit.

**Natural 1 and 20:** Natural 20 always hits. Natural 1 always misses.

### Rolling for Damage

- **PC attacks:** 1d6 (modified by STR for melee). *Variable Weapon Damage (Optional):* Use weapon's listed damage.
- **Monster attacks:** As listed in description.
- **Minimum damage:** A hit always deals at least 1 damage.
- **Death:** 0 hp or less = killed.

### Other Combat Issues

- **Attacking from behind:** AC bonuses from shields negated.
- **Blindness:** Cannot attack.
- **Bombing:** Flying creatures drop objects. Max altitude 300'. Hit on 16+ (1d20). Area and damage depend on bomb size.
- **Invulnerabilities (Optional):** Monsters immune to normal weapons may also be harmed by other invulnerable monsters or monsters with 5+ HD.
- **Paralysed opponents:** Automatically hit in melee (roll damage only).
- **Spacing:** Referee judges how many attackers can reach one opponent. 10' passage: typically 2–3 side by side.
- **Subduing (Optional):** Announce non-lethal intent. Bludgeoning only (or flat of blade). Track subdual damage separately. At 0 hp from subdual: intelligent creature surrenders.
- **Unarmed attacks:** As melee. 1d2 damage (modified by STR).
- **Unstable surfaces:** Missile attacks –4. Spells cannot be cast. Magic items normal. Magical flight generally not unstable.
- **Water combat:** Attack and damage penalised. Missile weapons usually don't work underwater.

### Morale (Optional Rule)

*(PDF p.133)*

Monsters have a morale rating (2–12). Score of 2 = never fights. Score of 12 = fights to death.

**Morale Checks:** Roll 2d6 > morale score = monster surrenders/flees. ≤ morale = continues fighting. Two successful checks in one encounter = fights to death (no more checks).

**When to Check:**
- First death on side.
- Half the side incapacitated.

**Adjustments:** –2 to +2 based on circumstances (losing/winning). Never applied to morale 2 or 12.

**Mercenary Morale:** Same system. Rating by troop type:

| Troop Type | Morale |
|------------|:------:|
| Untrained, militia | 6 |
| Barbarian horde | 7 |
| Trained warriors | 8 |
| Mounted | +1 |
| Elite troops | +1 |
| Fanatics, berserkers | +2 |

**Retainer Morale:** Do not check morale in combat. Loyalty check in extreme peril instead.

## Running Adventures

*(PDF p.138–150)*

### The Referee's Role

*(PDF p.138)*

**Facilitator of Fun:** Prepare adventures and maps. Improvise for unexpected player actions. Adapt procedures to keep game moving. Maintain balance of risk and reward.

**Neutral Judge:** Not competitive with players. Rules applied equally to PCs, monsters, and NPCs.

**Arbiter of Rules:**
- **Rulings:** Rules don't cover everything; referee must apply judgement.
- **Resolving actions:** Use ability checks, saving throws, or judge likelihood (percentage or X-in-6).
- **Disagreements:** Brief discussion, referee is final arbiter.
- **Randomness:** Judicious use of die rolls and random tables.

### Handling PCs

*(PDF p.139)*

**Rate of Advancement:**
- Standard: At least one PC at 2nd level after 3–4 sessions. Adjust treasure/monster difficulty as needed.
- Fast: Large treasure hoards (with appropriately challenging monsters).
- Slow: Reduced advancement rate and danger.

**Character Knowledge:** Players should not act on information their PCs wouldn't know.

**Grudges:** Natural part of play between characters of opposite alignments. Referee should ensure they don't dominate play.

**Maintaining Challenge:**
- *Magical research:* Careful attention to balance. Permanent, unlimited, no-save, or level-scaling magic can become imbalanced.
- *Special abilities:* Be careful when allowing powers beyond normal class abilities.
- *Excess wealth:* Find ways to take excess money through player choices (e.g. taxes vs. outlaw status).

### Running the Game

*(PDF p.140)*

**Describing the Unknown:**
- Describe what PCs see, hear, smell — no extra information.
- Monsters described, not named.
- Surprise attacks: describe the attack itself first, full description in later rounds.
- Monster/NPC game stats (hp, level) never revealed to players.
- Magic items described, not named. Powers discovered through experimentation.

**Describing Explored Areas:** Players draw maps from referee descriptions. May use map squares. Known areas can be pre-drawn by referee.

**Using Miniatures:** Gridded surface with erasable pens. Typical scale: 1" = 5' (or 5 yards outdoors).

### Monsters and NPCs

*(PDF p.141)*

**Intelligence and Tactics:** Intelligent monsters may use traps/stealth, guards/pets, magic items, learn from past encounters, adapt PC tactics, plan counter-manoeuvres.

**Teamwork:** Melee, missile, and magic users combine. Leaders may have special abilities, max hp, or extra HD.

**Motivation:** Intelligent monsters have their own interests. They should not always cooperate with PCs. May have allies who come to their aid or avenge them.

### Adventure Scenarios

*(PDF p.142–143)*

1. **Banishing Ancient Evil** — Destroy a dormant evil recently awakened.
2. **Clearing Ruins** — Scout and clear a settlement/stronghold for settlers.
3. **Contacting a Lost Civilisation** — Discover a long-lost race in a fantastic location.
4. **Escaping from Captivity** — Begin as prisoners; regain freedom.
5. **Exploring Unknown Territory** — Hired to explore/map an uncharted region.
6. **Performing a Quest** — Sent by deity/king to recover holy/magical items.
7. **Rescuing Captives** — Rescue kidnapped NPCs for reward or personal connection.
8. **Scouting an Enemy Outpost** — Infiltrate, gather intelligence, possibly destroy.
9. **Seeking a Magical Doorway** — Locate/close a portal for magical travel.
10. **Visiting a Sacred Site** — Journey to shrine/temple to consult oracle, remove curse, or find relic.

### Designing a Dungeon

*(PDF p.144–145)*

1. **Choose setting:** Crypt/tomb, natural caverns, settlement, mine, temple, tower/fortification.
2. **Choose monsters:** Based on scenario and setting.
3. **Map the dungeon:** Graph paper, 10'/square. Number each area.
4. **Stock the dungeon:** Note monsters, traps, treasures, special features.

**Dungeon Levels:** Deeper = greater risk and reward. 1 HD monsters on level 1, 2 HD on level 2, etc.

**Random Room Stocking:**

| d6 | Contents | Treasure Chance |
|:--:|----------|:--------------:|
| 1–2 | Empty | 1-in-6 |
| 3–4 | Monster | 3-in-6 |
| 5 | Special | None |
| 6 | Trap | 2-in-6 |

**Example Room Traps:** Falling block (1d10, save vs petrify), gas (save vs poison or die), mist (harmless), pit (falling damage), scything blade (1d8), slide to lower level.

**Example Treasure Traps:** Darts (1d6 × 1d4), flash of light (blind 1d8 turns, save vs spells), hidden monster, illusion monster, spray (doubles wandering monster chance 1d6 hours), poison needle (save vs poison or die).

**Example Specials:** Alarms, animating objects, falling blocks, illusions, shifting architecture, strange waters, teleports, trapdoors, voices.

**Treasure in Empty/Trapped Rooms:**
- Level 1: 1d6 × 100sp; 50%: 1d6 × 10gp; 5%: 1d6 gems; 2%: 1d6 jewellery; 2%: 1 magic item.
- Level 2–3: 1d12 × 100sp; 50%: 1d6 × 100gp; 10%: 1d6 gems; 5%: 1d6 jewellery; 8%: 1 magic item.
- Level 4–5: 1d6 × 1,000sp; 1d6 × 200gp; 20%: 1d6 gems; 10%: 1d6 jewellery; 10%: 1 magic item.
- Level 6–7: 1d6 × 2,000sp; 1d6 × 500gp; 30%: 1d6 gems; 15%: 1d6 jewellery; 15%: 1 magic item.
- Level 8–9: 1d6 × 5,000sp; 1d6 × 1,000gp; 40%: 1d12 gems; 20%: 1d12 jewellery; 20%: 1 magic item.

### Designing a Wilderness

*(PDF p.146–147)*

1. **Choose setting:** Basic geography, climate, milieu (technology, magic, races). Start small for new campaigns.
2. **Map the region:** Graph/hex paper. Large scale (24 mi/hex) first, then smaller (6 mi/hex). Mark mountains, rivers, seas, lakes, forests, swamps.
3. **Locate human realms:** Note rulers (lord, king, merchants, etc.).
4. **Locate non-human realms:** Demihumans, monstrous races, significant non-intelligent monsters.
5. **Place base town:** Near a river or road, centre of map.
6. **Place dungeons:** About a day's journey from base town.
7. **Create regional encounter tables:** Standard or custom.

### Designing a Base Town

*(PDF p.147)*

1. **Size:** Village (50–999), Small Town (1,000–4,999), Large Town (5,000–14,999), City (15,000+).
2. **Note services:** Black market, guard, healing, lodgings, rumours.
3. **Detail ruler:** Sheriff, mayor, council, merchant confederacy, or high-level NPC.
4. **Detail other NPCs:** Associated with services or quest-givers.
5. **Create rumours:** Hooks for exploration and dungeon discovery.

### Awarding XP

*(PDF p.148)*

**Recovered Treasure:** Primary source of XP (usually ¾+ of total). 1 XP per 1gp value. Magical treasure does not grant XP.

**Bonuses/Penalties:** Referee may optionally grant XP bonuses for good play or penalties for poor contribution.
