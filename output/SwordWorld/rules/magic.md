# Chapter 5: MAGIC

As the phrase sword and sorcery suggests, magic is one of the most appealing aspects of fantasy, along with weapon combat. This chapter explains how to handle magic in Sword World RPG.

## 5.1 Basic Rules of Magic

### 5.1.1 Types of Magic

In Sword World, there is a power called magic that can produce various mysterious effects by chanting words called runes and performing certain procedures at the same time.
There are five types of runes in the world of Sword World. This difference in runes is at the same time a difference in the type of magic. The five runes are high ancient, silent spirit, holy prayer, demon scream, and dragon roar. Each has a different source of magical power and a completely different procedure (although holy prayer and demon scream are essentially the same power, the only difference being the nature of the magic).
In addition to these runes, there are two others that, although weak, can still manifest magical power. They are called common runes and spellsongs, respectively.

### 5.1.2 Rune Masters and Rune Master Skills

In Sword World, all those who use magic utilizing runes are called rune masters. Rune masters are divided into five types according to the type of magic (runes) they use. Namely,

Sorcerer uses ancient magic
Shaman uses spirit magic
Priest uses holy magic
Dark priest uses dark magic

and dragon priest uses dragon roar magic.

Each corresponds to the skill for which it is named. Those with the sorcerer skill can use ancient magic and are called sorcerers.
Some rune masters are fluent in multiple runes. However, this also has some limitations.
One, is that some races may not be able to learn some or all of the runes. These are as explained earlier under 1.2: Races.
The other, is that you cannot have more than one each of holy, dark, and dragon roar magic at the same time. These types of magic are obtained through belief in gods, wicked gods, and dragons, respectively. A character cannot hold more than one of these beliefs at the same time.

### 5.1.3 Learning Magic

Magic is not omnipotent. It is only possible to achieve a defined and limited set of effects by weaving certain words (in runes), called spells. There are several types of spells, each with different effects. Depending on the strength of their effects, spells are divided into levels from 1-10. 1st level spells are very rudimentary, while 10th level spells are very powerful.
However, working with higher level (i.e., more advanced) spells requires a high rune master skill. A rune master can use all magic up to their skill level, but cannot use magic of a higher level than their current skill.
For those who have acquired multiple rune master skills, consider the level of spells you can use for each type of magic.

*Example:*

Suppose we have an adventurer with priest skill level 1 and shaman skill level 2. This adventurer can use 1st level holy magic and 1st-2nd level spirit magic. He cannot use any ancient magic, nor any holy magic of 2nd level or higher, nor any spirit magic of 3rd level or higher.

### 5.1.4 How to Use Magic

#### 5.1.4.1 Magic Power

Now let's explain how to actually use magic in combat, etc.
First, adventurers who use magic are given a score called magic power.

magic power = magic skill level + intelligence bonus

If you’ve acquired multiple magic skills, consider the magic power of each separately.

*Example:*

An adventurer with priest skill level 1, shaman skill level 2, and 13 intelligence  (+2 bonus) would have a magic power of 3 in holy magic and a magic power of 4 in spirit magic.

**Filling out the Character Sheet**

On the Character Sheet, there is a magic field. Characters with rune master skills must write their runes, skill level, and magic power in this field.
For the character in the above example, we’d write holy prayer and silent spirit in the runes column and 1 and 3 (holy prayer), and 2 and 4 (silent spirit) in the level and magic power columns to the right of them, respectively.

---

#### 5.1.4.2 Resisting

There are two kinds of magic. One is resistible. The other is non-resistible. Magic that is resistible can be rendered ineffective or less effective if the target chooses to resist. Non-resistible magic can be effective regardless of what the target does.
This distinction will be specified for each spell in the spell lists that follow.

#### 5.1.4.3 Procedure for Casting Non-resistible Magic

The game procedures for using resistible and non-resistible magic are quite different.
The procedure for an adventurer to use non-resistible magic is as follows:

Declare which magic you’re using, then roll 2D.

That's all. If the result is anything other than double ones, the magic takes effect. If it is double ones, then for some reason (the spell was inaccurate, etc.) the magic takes no effect at all.

#### 5.1.4.4 Procedure When an Adventurer Casts Resistible Magic on a Monster

When attempting to cast resistible magic on a monster, the caster must determine whether their opponent can resist it. You must make a success roll using caster’s magic power as the baseline score and the monster’s mental point resistance as the target score. If 2D roll + magic power is greater than or equal to the monster’s resistance score, the magic will be cast with its full effect. If the resistance score is not met, the magic will not work or will only have a weak effect. If the 2D roll comes up double ones, that means there was some mistake and the magic did not work. Even if the magic doesn’t normally lose its effect when resisted, in this case it is completely nullified.
If the 2D roll comes up double sixes, the magic automatically succeeds and will take its full effect, no matter how high the monster’s mental point resistance (except for when the magic itself has no effect on that monster. In this case, there is no need to roll the dice, and the magic does not take effect).

*Example:*

Consider an adventurer with shaman skill level 2 and a +2 intelligence bonus (magic power 4) who casts the Fire Bolt spell, which shoots a flaming bullet at the opponent, on a goblin (mental point resistance 9).
Fire Bolt is resistible magic, so if it’s resisted by the opponent, its effect will be reduced.
In this case, the adventurer (the player controlling them) must make a success roll. The baseline score is 4 (magic power) and the target score is 9 (goblin's mental point resistance). In other words, if the 2D roll is 5 or more, Fire Bolt will take its full effect.
Depending on the dice, there are 3 kinds of results:
Roll of 5 or greater: Fire Bolt takes its full effect.
Roll of 3-4: Fire Bolt takes an incomplete effect.
Roll of 2 (double ones): Fire Bolt does not work at all.

magic power + 2D ≥ monster's mental point resistance → magic takes full effect
magic power + 2D < monster's mental point resistance → magic takes an incomplete effect (some take no effect at all)
When the 2D roll is double sixes, the magic will automatically take its full effect
When the 2D roll is double ones, the magic unconditionally takes no effect at all

#### 5.1.4.5 Procedure When a Monster Casts Resistible Magic on an Adventurer

When a monster casts magic, the procedure is different from when an adventurer casts magic.
First, in the case of non-resistible magic, a monster can never fail. There is no need to roll the dice, as the magic will definitely take its effect. In the case of resistible magic, the issue is resolved from the perspective of whether the adventurer is able to resist the magic cast by the monster. This is determined by a success roll using the character's mental power resistance, that is, a mental power resistance roll. The target score is a score called the monster’s magic intensity.
If the adventurer's final score (mental power resistance + 2D) is equal to or greater than the monster's magic intensity, the monster's magic will only take an incomplete effect (some magic will take no effect at all). If the adventurer's final score is less than the monster's magic intensity, they will receive the full effect of the magic.
Also, since this is considered a success roll, it’ll be an automatic failure if the 2D roll is double ones. No matter how high the adventurer's mental power resistance or how low the monster's magic intensity, the magic will take its full effect. Conversely, double sixes is an automatic success, so in this case, regardless of the adventurer's mental power resistance or the monster's magic intensity, that’s a successful resist* and the magic will only take an incomplete effect.

*In this way, when making a resistible magic check, you can also call it a successful resist or failed resist from the perspective of the one being affected by the magic.

*Example:*

Ducard Ⅱ (mental power resistance 4) is attacked by a dark elf during an adventure. Dark elves have the ability to use spirit magic, with a magic intensity of 13. If the dark elf casts a Fire Bolt on Ducard Ⅱ, Ducard Ⅱ must make a mental power resistance roll (baseline score 4, target score 13).
Roll is 9 or higher: successful resist. Fire Bolt takes an incomplete effect.
Roll is 8 or less: failed resist. Fire Bolt takes its full effect.

mental power resistance + 2D ≥ monster's magic intensity → magic takes an incomplete effect (some take no effect at all)
mental power resistance + 2D < monster's magic intensity → magic takes full effect
When the 2D roll is double sixes, the resist automatically succeeds
When the 2D roll is double ones, the resist automatically fails

#### 5.1.4.6 Procedure When an Adventurer Casts Resistible Magic on an Adventurer

Just like player characters, even those whose data was created for a character may appear as an enemy. If such an enemy casts magic on an adventurer, or if an adventurer casts magic on such an enemy, both sides must roll dice to resolve the situation, just as in weapon combat between adventurers.
In other words, you compare the magic power + 2D score of the one casting the magic with the mental power resistance + 2D score of the one on whom the magic is being cast. If the score of the one casting the magic is greater, the magic will take full effect, but if there is a tie or if the score of the one on whom the magic is being cast is greater, the magic is resisted and will only take an incomplete effect.
The resolution of double ones and double sixes is also the same as weapon combat. In other words, if the resisting side rolls double sixes, the resist is an unconditional success, otherwise, if the side casting the magic rolls double sixes, the resist is a failure.
When the one casting the magic rolls double ones, the magic takes no effect at all.
When the resisting side rolls double ones, the resist fails, unless the side casting the magic also rolls double ones.

#### 5.1.4.7 Handling Resistible Magic and Multiple Targets

Some magic impacts a certain area and takes effect on all targets within that area.
This kind of magic can have multiple targets, and each target must make their own check to resist.

**When an Adventurer Casts Magic on Monsters**

The player must roll the dice and make a check for each monster.

*Example:*

Suppose an adventurer with sorcerer skill level 3 and a +2 intelligence bonus (magic power 5) casts the spell Lightning. Lightning is a spell that shoots a bolt of lightning, dealing damage to everything in its path. If there are multiple targets in the path of the lightning bolt, you must make a check for each one’s attempt to resist.
Suppose there are three goblins (each with a mental point resistance of 9) and one goblin lord (mental  point resistance 12) in this space. The player rolls the dice four times and for each one must determine whether the magic breaks through the resist and takes its full effect.
If the rolls against the three goblins were 6, 3, and 2 (double ones!), and the roll against the goblin lord was 6, then the result would look like this:
1st goblin: 5 (magic power) + 6 (roll) = 11 ≥ 9 (mental point resistance), full effect.
2nd goblin: 5 (magic power) + 3 (roll) = 8 < 9 (mental point resistance), incomplete effect.
3rd goblin: The result is double ones, so no effect
Goblin lord: 5 (magic power) + 6 (roll) = 11 < 12  (mental point resistance), incomplete effect.
When rolling the dice, you must clearly declare which target the dice are meant for before you actually roll.

**When a Monster Casts Magic on Adventurers**

Each player must make a resistance roll using monster's magic intensity as the target score.

*Example:*

A centaur and three adventurers get into a fight. This centaur has the ability to use ancient magic and unleashes Lightning. The game master declares that its magic intensity is 13.
Each of the three adventurers (the players in control) roll 2D to check if they resist, using their mental power resistance as the baseline score, and 13 (magic intensity) as the target score.

**When an Adventurer Casts Magic on Adventurers**

The following procedure is used to make a check when data created for a character casts magic on adventurers, and vice versa.
First, the side casting the magic rolls 2D. The final score (magic power + 2D) must then be determined this one time only. Each resisting side must then make a mental power resistance roll, using that determined final score as the target score.

*Example:*

The evil sorcerer Gorman (magic power 5) unleashes Lightning on the three adventurers. First, Gorman rolls 2D and adds his own magic power. If we assume his 2D roll is 8, each of the three adventurers must make a mental power resistance roll, with a target score of 5 (magic power) + 8 (roll) = 13. Suppose that the baseline score of all three of their mental power resistance rolls is 4. If their rolls are each 2 (double ones!), 6, and 9, then only the last one is able to resist the Lightning.

Handling double ones and double sixes is no different from when there’s only one target. If the side casting the magic rolls double ones, the magic has no effect on any targets. If the side casting the magic rolls double sixes, no one can resist, except for those who also roll double sixes.

**When an Adventurer Casts Magic on a Mix of Adventurers and Monsters**

Sometimes enemies written in the same data format as a character may also be accompanied by monsters. For example, a sorcerer who leads undead monsters to attack villages. Casting magic on such a group is handled the same way as when an adventurer casts magic on (a group of) adventurers, as described above. At this time, the monsters must also roll the dice to make resist checks. Please use the optional rules under 17.1: Changes to the Magic Procedure.

#### 5.1.4.8 If the Target Does Not Resist

Some magic can also have secondary effects. For example, the spirit magic Fanaticism increases attack power, but conversely decreases evasion speed. With such magic, the one on whom it is cast can choose whether or not to resist.
If the one on whom the magic is being cast chooses not to resist the magic, it will definitely take effect so long as the one casting the magic does not roll double ones. You’re also allowed to choose to not resist magic like Fire Bolt, which will definitely result in a loss if it's cast on you....... but that probably won't benefit you in any way.

### 5.1.5 Consuming Mental Power

Adventurers cannot use magic endlessly. When using magic, an adventurer must temporarily reduce their mental power.
Each magic has a score called base mental power cost. This base mental power cost, divided by the corresponding rune master skill level (rounded up), equals the actual mental power consumed by the character.

*Example:*

A character with shaman skill level 2 uses the Fire Bolt spell. The base mental power cost for Fire Bolt is 5. The character must consume 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5, rounding up to 3 points of mental power.

A character can reduce their mental power to 0, but they cannot make it negative. For example, a character with 5 points of mental power remaining can use magic that costs 5 points of mental power, but cannot use magic that costs 6 or more points of mental power.
If a character’s mental power reaches 0, they will lose consciousness. The character cannot regain consciousness unless they recover their mental power to 1 point or more in some way.

Even if magic fails, such as by rolling double ones or being resisted by the opponent, a character must still consume their mental power, same as if they had actually used the magic.

### 5.1.6 Recovering Mental Power

Mental power reduced due to using magic, etc. can be recovered via the following methods.

**Magical Recovery**

There's magic and magical items that can recover a character's mental power, such as the holy magic Transfer Mental Power. The amount recovered is explained under each magic and item.

**Recovery via Sleep**

If a character sleeps for at least 6 hours in a row, they will fully recover their mental power upon awakening. However, this method of recovery can only be done once a day. If you awaken within 24 hours of your previous awakening (when your mental power was last recovered), your mental power will not be recovered.

### 5.1.7 Magic Expansion

You can increase the effectiveness and certainty of magic by consuming extra mental power. This is called magic expansion.
Magic expansion is done by doubling or tripling...... the amount of mental power consumed. At this time, you do not double or triple the base mental power cost....... Instead, you divide the mental power by your skill level, rounded up, or in other words, the mental power you would consume when not expanding is doubled, tripled...... etc.

*Example:*

Suppose a sorcerer with sorcerer skill level 2 doubles the range of his Energy Bolt spell and casts it on two enemies. The base mental power cost of Energy Bolt is 5. For this reason, the mental power consumed without expansion is 5 ÷ 2, rounded up to 3. Therefore, for a doubled expansion, the amount of mental power consumed would be 3 x 2 = 6. You would not calculate (5 x 2) ÷ 2 = 5.

#### 5.1.7.1 Expansion Types

Magic expansion includes the following types.

**Targets Expansion**

If you expand a spell that affects one enemy (or ally), it can instead affect multiple at the same time.
If it's two or three targets...... the mental power consumed will also be doubled or tripled.......
In this case, you must roll the dice separately for each target to determine if they resist the magic, and other effects such as damage must also be determined separately.
For spells that can perform this expansion, targets is written in the expansion section of the magic details.

**Area Expansion**

If you expand a spell that affects a specific area, it can affect a larger area than originally intended.
If the area is doubled or tripled...... the mental power consumed will also be doubled or tripled....... You can also expand an area by a fraction (x1.5, x8/3, etc.), in which case the fraction is rounded up when determining the multiplier for mental power consumed. So for expansions such as x 1.5 and x 8/3, the cost would be double and triple, respectively.
Since it’s the area that gets expanded, the radius of a circle, a side of a square, etc., can only be increased by the square root of the expansion rate. Please see Table 5-1: Area and Size Expansion.
For spells that can perform this expansion, area is written in the expansion section of the magic details.

**Table 5-1: Area and Size Expansion**

| Expansion Rate | Area Expansion Length | Volume Expansion Length |
|---|---|---|
| x 2 | 1.4 | 1.3 |
| x 3 | 1.7 | 1.4 |
| x 4 | 2 | 1.6 |
| x 5 | 2.2 | 1.7 |
| x 6 | 2.4 | 1.8 |
| x 7 | 2.6 | 1.9 |
| x 8 | 2.8 | 2.0 |
| x 9 | 3.0 | 2.1 |
| x 10 | 3.2 | 2.2 |

Note) On the table, expansion rate is the multiplier for mental power consumed.

When you cast a spell by consuming that multiplied amount of mental power, multiply the number in area expansion length or volume expansion length by the number (radius, side) that represents the spell’s area of effect, and the result will be the spell’s area. For spells cast on a flat surface, use the number in the area expansion length column, and for spells cast into space, use the number in the volume expansion length column.
For example, when you cast the ancient magic Light, whose area is A space with a 10 meter radius, by consuming double the mental power, the resulting area will be a spherical space with a radius of 10 x 1.3 = 13 meters.

**Volume Expansion**

Some spells that affect a space or object can have their volume expanded. If the volume is expanded by two or three times...... the mental power consumed will also be doubled or tripled....... Rounding should be handled the same way you would for area expansion.
Since it’s the volume that gets expanded, the radius of a sphere, a side of a cube, etc., can only be increased by the cube root of the expansion rate. Please see Table 5-1: Area and Size Expansion.
For spells that can perform this expansion, target volume is written in the expansion section of the magic details.

**Duration Expansion**

For spells whose effects last a certain amount of time, you can expand that duration. If the duration is doubled or tripled...... the mental power consumed will also be doubled or tripled....... You can also increase it by a fraction, such as x1.5, in which case the fraction is rounded up when determining the multiplier for mental power consumed.
For spells that can perform this expansion, duration is written in the expansion section of the magic details.

**Distance Expansion**

Magic basically only works up to a certain distance. However, there are spells that can extend their reach. If reach distance is doubled or tripled...... the mental power consumed will also be doubled or tripled.......  You can also increase it by a fraction, such as x1.5, in which case the fraction is rounded up when determining the multiplier for mental power consumed.
For spells that can perform this expansion, distance is written in the expansion section of the magic details.

**Damage Expansion**

For spells that deal damage, expansion can increase the certainty of that damage. If you double or triple the amount of mental power you consume...... you can make a damage check (strike roll) twice or three times. The damage you will deal is equal to the greatest of all the strike rolls you made in this way (not the total).
For spells that can perform this expansion, damage certainty is written in the expansion section of the magic details.

**Final Score Increase**

There's some magic whose effects are negated or reduced if they're resisted by the target, but will also let you increase the magic's final score (resist target score) in order to prevent this. This increase in final score may also take effect if a nullifying spell is cast later.
If you double or triple the amount of mental power you consume...... you can add a +1 or +2 bonus to your final score.
For spells that can perform this expansion, final score is written in the expansion section of the magic details.
This expansion requires a deep proficiency in magic. Therefore, it cannot be done infinitely. Final score can only be increased up to a maximum of caster's rune master skill level ÷ 2 (rounded up). That’s up to +1 (double) for 1st~2nd level, +2 (triple) for 3rd~4th level, and +5 (sextuple) for 9th~10th level.

**Effect Expansion**

Some expansions don’t fall into any of the above types. For spells that can perform these special expansions, effect is written in the expansion section of the magic details, and an explanation is given under each spell.

#### 5.1.7.2 Combined Expansion

Some magic allows for multiple expansion types. In these cases, you can perform multiple expansions at the same time. However, at this time, the amount of mental power consumed will become extremely large. By multiplying the rates determined for each expansion, you can determine the total multiplied rate of mental power consumed.

*Example:*

Suppose a sorcerer casts an Energy Bolt on three enemies (x 3 expansion) with a +1 to his final score (x 2 expansion) and he makes 4 damage checks for each (x 4 expansion). In this case, the multiplied rate of mental power consumed is 3 x 2 x 4 = 24!

### 5.1.8 Magic Contraction

Magic can also be contracted as opposed to expanded. However, this does not reduce the amount of mental power consumed.

#### 5.1.8.1 Contraction Types

Magic contractions include the following:

**Area Contraction**

You can make the area of effect narrower than originally intended. The shape (usually a circle) cannot be changed. You also cannot cast it in such a way that it hits the enemy directly in the eyes.

**Volume Contraction**

Similar to area contraction, you can also reduce the volume of the targeted object or space. Again, the shape cannot be changed, and you cannot directly aim it at the enemy's eyes.

**Duration Contraction**

Magic with a duration can be optionally shortened. However, you cannot set a time that causes the effect to wear off in the middle of a round. You may only set it to wear off at the end of a round. You must also decide how long you want the magic to last before you cast it. Magic that’s already been cast cannot be canceled at will.

**Magic Power Decrease**

Magic power can be optionally reduced. This will reduce the final score and, if it's damage magic, also reduce bonus damage (it’s not possible to only reduce the final score or only reduce bonus damage). You must declare how many points you’ll reduce before you roll the dice. If used on multiple targets, you must reduce it by the same amount for all of them. Also, if the side casting the magic rolls double sixes, the resisting side will also need double sixes, no matter how much magic power was declared to be decreased (if it's a monster, it cannot resist).

**Effect Contraction**

All magic that can expand their effects can also contract their effects.

#### 5.1.8.2 Combined Expansion and Contraction

Although it's not often done, it's possible to perform magic expansion and contraction at the same time. For example, you could extend the area of effect while reducing magic power. The mental power required at this time is decided by expansion. It cannot be reduced by contraction. For example, if you double the area and reduce magic power by -4, the mental power consumed will still be double.

### 5.1.9 Magic Types

There’s several kinds of magic that share the same characteristics.
These characteristics are presented here as magic types.

**Damage**

A spell that deals damage to the target's life force or mental power. Spells of the damage type always have a clearly defined strike power. If you manage to break through the target's resist, use the spell's strike power to determine damage by making a strike roll using the Rating Table, same as if you had hit with a weapon in weapon combat. The key number is, of course, the indicated strike power. If you roll 10 or greater during the strike roll, a critical will occur and deal even more damage, same as in weapon combat. Conversely, If you roll double ones, it does not deal any damage at all.
Most magic of the damage type can still deal damage even if it's resisted by the target, only with a reduced effect. In this case, you must make the strike roll after subtracting 10 from strike power. Also, at this time, no matter how high you roll during your strike roll, a critical will not occur.
In either case, the caster's magic power can be added as bonus damage to the result of the strike roll. Also, the target's adventurer level (or the monster's monster level) is subtracted from the damage. However, there’s an exception if you roll double ones during your strike roll. In this case, no matter how much magic power your character (monster) has, they cannot deal damage to the target at all.
Also, even magic of the damage type may in rare cases take no effect if resisted. At this time, if the target successfully resists, you cannot make a strike roll.

**Target Score**

Some magic can nullify the effects of other magic. For example, the ancient magic Dispel Magic nullifies the effects of most magic.
Most magic is automatically nullified if a nullifying spell is used, but there are some that are not. This is magic that has a target score.
When casting any magic, no matter what kind of magic it is, you must roll 2D to check whether an automatic failure (double ones) will occur or not. If you're trying to nullify magic that has a target score with another spell, you must use that other spell's 2D roll to determine your final score. This final score equals magic power + 2D roll (similar to a final score for other success rolls).
If this final score is greater than or equal to the target score originally set for that first magic that was cast, then that first magic can be nullified.
This target score equals the final score of the first magic that was cast (in other words, the magic you're about to nullify). Therefore, when an adventurer uses magic that has a target score, that player should note down that score and remember it. However, if you feel this is cumbersome, or if you forget to make a note, then when it becomes necessary (nullifying magic has been used), you can roll 2D on the spot to determine a final score = target score.
When you use magic that nullifies other magic, if your roll is double sixes, the magic will automatically be nullified, no matter what the target score is.
Conversely, if the dice roll double sixes when determining the target score (the success score of the first magic), you must roll another double sixes in order to nullify that magic.

**Counters (Negates):**

When magic is cast against other magic, their effects may completely cancel each other out. For example, there are two kinds of ancient magic, Decrease Weight (which makes an object lighter) and Increase Weight (which makes an object heavier), but when both of these are cast on the same object at the same time, they will cancel each other out and disappear without taking effect.
Such magic, that is, magic that negates the effect of other magic, but at the same time is negated by that other magic without taking its own effect, is called counters (negates): type.
When casting counters (negates): magic to cancel the effect of certain magic, if the magic that was cast prior has a target score, then according to the definition of magic with a target score, the magic that's cast after cannot cancel the prior magic unless its final score is greater than or equal to the determined target score.

**Counters (Overpowers):**

When certain magic is cast against other magic, it can cancel the effect of the prior magic but still take its own effect afterwards. This is magic of the counters (overpowers): type.
For example, the ancient magic Darkness can nullify the effects of the spirit magic Will-O-Wisp and still close the surrounding area in darkness, which means Darkness itself is more effective.
However, this is often not automatic.
One, is when the target resists. In the case of magic that does not take effect if resisted, if the target does resist it, the magic will not take its effect, and the effect of the prior magic will continue.
The other, is when the prior magic has a target score. In this case, according to the definition of magic with a target score, the magic cast after will not take effect unless its final score is greater than or equal to the determined target score.
If the magic cast prior has a target score and the magic cast after is resistible, please follow the procedure below to make a check.

① First, confirm the target score (= final score) of the magic cast prior (if you forgot, determine it now).
② Next, the one casting the magic must roll 2D to find their final score (by adding their magic power).
③ Compare target score in ① with final score in ②. If the final score does not reach the target score, the magic will not be cast. This ends here.
④ If final score in ② is greater than or equal to target score in ①, the target may attempt to resist. The resist target score equals the final score in ②.
⑤ If the target successfully resisted in ④, the new magic cast will not take effect. The effect of the prior magic will continue. If the target failed to resist, the new magic cast will take effect and the effect of the prior magic will be negated.

Counters (overpowers): magic does not mean that certain magic is definitely stronger than other magic. It's possible for magic A to counter (overpower) magic B, and at the same time for magic B to counter (overpower) magic A.
Also, all magic counters (overpowers) itself. Therefore, if certain magic takes effect, and you cast that same magic on the same target multiple times, the effect will not be cumulative.

**Mind**

This is magic that affects the target's mental state. This kind of magic can only take effect on one target at a time. If magic of the mind type is cast on a target, and other magic also of the mind type is cast on that target, the new magic will take effect, and the prior magic will be nullified.
In other words, all mind magic counters (overpowers) other mind magic. Note that many are resistible magic, and some have target scores.

**Physical Ability**

This is magic that affects the target’s physical abilities (strength, etc.). Magic of this type can only take effect on humans, fae, and youma.
Also, with magic of the physical ability type, two or more cannot affect the same target at the same time. Please resolve them as counters (overpowers) magic.
It’s possible for both mind and physical ability magic to each affect the same target separately.

**Weapon Enchancement**

Magic that targets a weapon and enhances it. Two or more of this kind of magic cannot affect the same weapon at the same time. Please resolve them as counters (overpowers) magic.
Weapons that have been given power by ancient magic, silver weapons, or mithril silver weapons are assumed to already have non-removable (see below) weapon enhancement magic cast on them. Therefore, that power cannot be nullified, so even if you cast new weapon enhancement magic, it’ll be useless.

**Non-removable**

Some magic, such as Dispel Magic (ancient magic) and Remove Curse (holy magic), can be used as counter magic that removes other magic. However, magic of the non-removable type cannot be removed by other magic.
Although, the ancient magic Perfect Cancellation is able to remove non-removable magic. Also, sometimes there is counter magic that counters certain magic, which if specified, will be removed by that counter magic. It's also possible to remove non-removable magic by directly using the power of a god (wicked god) with the holy/dark magic Call God.

**Curse**

Dispel Magic (ancient magic) can be used to remove most magic, but for magic of the curse type, this is not possible. However, Remove Curse (holy magic) can be used to remove it.

**Detection**

A spell that detects objects, people, magic power, etc. These can be targeted by the ancient magic Counter Sense.
These spells also ignore the protection of the ancient magic Magic Protection.

**Focus**

Magic has a fixed duration. Magic of the focus type is that in which the magic's effect lasts as long as the caster (the one who casts the magic) is focused. While focusing, the caster cannot take the following actions:

Use other magic
Attack another with a weapon (or barehanded)
Move more than 3 meters in one round
Speak words
Any other voluntary action

Even while focusing, if another attacks you with a weapon, etc. you can still attempt to evade it. If magic is cast on you, it's also possible to resist it.
Also, when the target is no longer within the magic's area of effect (out of reach, out of sight, etc.), the magic will lose its effect, even if you continue to focus.
If the caster suffers damage to their life force or mental power, their focus will be interrupted and the magic's effect will be lost.
If the caster wishes to voluntarily release their focus (in order to take another action), they must declare it at the beginning of the round, when no one else has made their action announcements yet. If your focus is interrupted due to damage, etc. your focus will be interrupted at the timing in which the damage is dealt.

**Illusion**

A spell that deceives the five senses of the target. These spells deceive the opponent’s sensory organs and give them false sensations. There are three types of senses through which monsters perceive their surroundings: five senses, pseudo senses, and magic senses. Magic of the illusion type is effective on those that perceive their surroundings with five senses or pseudo senses. Humans and fae perceive their surroundings through five senses, so they can all be targeted by illusions.
If the target does not have the senses to deceive, this spell will have no effect at all. It is completely ineffective against monsters who perceive their surroundings through magic senses, and illusions that deceive vision are useless against monsters who lack vision.
The effects of spells of the illusion type can be detected by a resistance roll. However, in order to do this, you must first be suspicious of the illusion. The game master should decide in which cases it seems suspicious. In the case of player characters (adventurers), this may be when the players themselves suspect an illusion. For NPCs and monsters, the game master should decide based on their intelligence.
For example, suppose a fake floor is created above a pit using a Create Image (ancient magic) spell. No matter how smart you are, you’re not likely to notice this. But what if whoever was walking at the front of the line had the misfortune to step on top of it and fall?
No matter how foolish you are, you should suspect that the floor is an illusion.
Or, what if you were in a hallway with a thick layer of dust, but only one part of it was free of dust?
It might be unreasonable for a monster with low intelligence, such as a goblin, but a human of average intelligence would find it suspicious.
In the event of such a suspicion, the target of the illusion can make a mental power resistance roll. The target score is the final score from when the illusion spell was cast (if you forgot, roll the dice and determine it again). If you’re successful, you’ll be able to see through the illusion and know the correct situation. However, you can only make this resistance roll once. Once you fail, no matter how suspicious it may be, you won't be able to detect its true form (of course, you can walk around suspicious floors).

**Ritual**

Magic of the ritual type can increase its effect (final score) due to carefully preparing and performing a ritual.
With these spells, you can add +1 to your final score for each day you perform the ritual. If additional casters who can use the same spell also participate in the ritual, you will gain +1 to your final score for each one. Even humans who cannot use spells can participate in the ritual. In this case, you can gain +1 to your final score for every 10 people (rounded down). However, the ritual procedures are very complicated, so those who have not been taught the procedure in advance will not be able to participate in the ritual. If it's ancient magic, the caster must have been taught at a school such as an academy of sages, etc. and if it's holy (dark) magic, they must be a believer.
These bonuses are stacked in the form of days x participants. For example, suppose a priest performs a Remove Curse ritual. If the ritual takes two days (+2), one extra priest who can also use Remove Curse participates (+1), and 30 believers participate (+3), the final score bonus will be 2 x (1 + 3) = +8. Fractions of the number of days must be rounded down, so if you can't spend a full day, the final score bonus will be 0, regardless of how many participants there are.
Unlike magic expansion, there is no limit to the increase in final score through rituals.
A ritual must be performed without sleep or rest, and is usually limited to 2 days. If someone drops out during the ritual, their number will not be considered at all. In the previous example, if 10 believers drop out, even if it's right after the ritual begins, just before the ritual ends, or or at any timing in between, the final score bonus will be 2 x (1 + 2) = +6.
The person who makes the success roll is the one who takes the center role in performing the ritual.
The game master may also use a ritual to perform other expansions (targets, area, volume, etc.) that the magic would allow.

**Lost**

Among ancient magic, there are some spells known only to a limited number of sorcerers, and spells that were lost with the fall of the ancient kingdom. These are called lost spells.
The game master gets to determine which spells are considered lost spells. It's acceptable to have all spells on the magic list be lost, or conversely, to have all magic be known and no spells be lost.
If a spell says lost under its magic details, it may be easier to maintain balance when enjoying the game if you consider it a lost spell. Unless the game master has a particular reason, please consider these to be lost spells.

### 5.1.10 How to Read Magic Details

Magic details are each written in the following format:

#### Sample Magic Details

- **Base Mental Power Cost:** 0
- **Distance:** Caster
- **Area:** Caster
- **Duration:** Until you forget the contents
- **Effect:** Understand how to read magic details
- **Type:** Non-removable
- **Expansion:** ー
- **Resist:** None

This spell allows the caster to understand how to read magic details.

**Base Mental Power Cost**

The base mental power cost represents how much mental power is consumed when using that magic. As previously explained, this base mental power cost divided by the corresponding rune master skill level equals the actual mental power consumed by the caster.

**Distance**

This represents the distance the magic can reach. Basically, magic works on things that the caster can see and are within this distance. Any exceptions will always be noted in the explanation that follows. If the distance is touch, the caster must touch the target with their hand or (for ancient magic) with their magical catalyst (see below). To cast these spells in combat, you must be engaged with the target. At this time, there is no need to roll dice to determine whether you've touched your opponent, like with an attack hit check (you can touch them automatically).

**Area**

This represents the target that the magic affects.
If it says caster, the magic works only on the caster who cast the spell.
When it says one, it works on one enemy or ally. It is also possible for casters to cast it on themselves.
If it works on a specific space or area, then everything within that area is targeted. For example, a Fireball explosion deals explosion damage to everything within a 3 meter radius. It makes no difference whether it's the caster's enemies, allies, or the caster.
There are many other ways to write this, but it should be clear if you read the description.

**Duration**

This represents how long the magic will remain in effect. This includes instant, 18 rounds, 1 day, permanent, until focus ends or until certain conditions are met. For magic whose duration is instant, the magic itself disappears in an instant, but its effects remain. Wounds caused by a Fireball explosion will remain, and if you receive healing by Cure Wounds, those wounds will be recovered instantly.

**Effect**

This is a brief description of the magic's effect. Once you've read the detailed description, you'll be able to recall the general effect of the magic just by looking at this.

**Type**

Represents the type of magic. Please see 5.1.9: Magic Types. Counters (negates): and counters (overpowers): describe magic that can be negated by this magic, while countered (negated) by: and countered (overpowered) by: describe magic that can negate this magic. However, Dispel Magic, Counter Sense, Suppress Elemental, Dispel Order, Rune Shield, Perfect Cancellation, Neutralize Elemental, and Remove Curse omit this. Magic of the mind, physical ability, and weapon enhancement types also omit counters and countered by, since they are sufficiently described.

**Expansion**

Represents which expansion types can be performed. Please see 5.1.7.1: Expansion Types.

**Resist**

This represents whether the magic can be resisted, and what will happen if it is resisted.
None: No resist can be performed. Spells always take their effect.
Negated Effect: If resisted, the spell does not take its effect.
Reduced Effect: If resisted, the spell only takes an incomplete effect. Spells of the damage type will have their strike power reduced by -10 and can no longer critical.
After these details are briefly written, a detailed description of each spell is written. Be sure to read it over before attempting to use something you've never used before.

### 5.1.11 Ancient Magic and the Sorcerer Skill

High ancient are the great runes that were developed and studied during the time of the ancient kingdom. The power of these runes releases magic forces and brings about various effects. Many of the spells and runes of this system were lost with the fall of the ancient kingdom, but a small amount of them have been passed down to modern Forcelia, and are now referred to as ancient magic. The skill for using ancient magic is called the sorcerer skill, and those who have this skill are called sorcerers.

#### 5.1.11.1 Restrictions on Using Ancient Magic

Ancient magic has powerful effects because it draws its source of power (which is sometimes called mana) directly from matter, but it is subject to the following two restrictions in order to cast the spells correctly:

1. Must have a staff (mage's staff) as a magical catalyst
2. Due to complex gestures required, armor is limited to cloth or soft leather

If you don't obey these two things, you won't be able to use ancient magic. Other rules follow the basic rules of magic.

#### 5.1.11.2 Basic Abilities of the Sorcerer Skill

The sorcerer skill includes the following basic abilities in addition to spells:

**High Ancient**

Sorcerers can speak, read, and write in high ancient. It seems that these runes were used in official texts and conversation in the ancient kingdom, but now much of it has been lost, except for the few that remain in spellbooks.

**Low Ancient**

This was an everyday language of the ancient kingdom, called low ancient, and was the language used by citizens and slaves. Sorcerers can speak, read and write in this language. Low ancient was used in informal texts in the ancient kingdom.

#### 5.1.11.3 Characteristics of Sorcerers and Ancient Magic

Sorcerers and the ancient magic they handle have the following characteristics:

**Spellbook**

A sorcerer must somehow preserve the ancient magic spells they've learned. Otherwise, even those with the greatest minds cannot possibly memorize all the words, actions, etc. needed to cast spells.
For this reason, it is customary for sorcerers to write these spells down in a book called a spellbook. A spellbook is, so to speak, just a handwritten notebook, and the spellbook itself has no magical power of its own. However, because it's a very important thing, it would be a disaster if it were lost or stolen, so some sorcerers may keep several copies in reserve.
When you go on an adventure, or you're in a situation where you'd need to use magic, you'd usually bring your spellbook with you. However, no sorcerer looks at their spellbook every time they try to cast a spell. This is because the words and actions of a spell are usually sharply memorized in your head. A spellbook is used to review them, always confirming and ensuring your memory.
There are no special restrictions on spellbooks according to the rules. Nevertheless, keep in mind that sorcerers in Sword World always keep their spellbooks with them, and remember to take very good care of them.

**Magical Catalyst**

Sorcerers require a special medium to cast spells. This is a magical catalyst, and it is through this that a sorcerer exercises the power of a spell. Usually, a specially-made mage's staff is used as a magical catalyst, made from a wooden staff with a Create Device spell cast on it. This is why you must have a mage's staff as a restriction when using ancient magic. However, some sorcerers substitute other things besides staves. For example, Laverna, the witch of Orphan, uses an amethyst ring she wears on her right middle finger as her magical catalyst, and Cyanos, leader of the magic knights of Ramliearth, carries a small twig wand in his left hand as a magical catalyst.

### 5.1.13 Spirit Magic and the Shaman Skill

Silent spirit are runes used to communicate with spirits and draw out the power controlled by those spirits. The skill for using spirit magic is called the shaman skill, and those who have this skill are called shamans. The shaman skill allows you to use all basic abilities and spirit magic up to the caster's skill level.

#### 5.1.13.1 Restrictions on Using Spirit Magic

The basic rules for casting magic are the same, but the following conditions are required in order to use the shaman skill.

1. Must not wear metal armor
2. Both hands must not be full
3. The spirit power corresponding to the spell must be working

#1 is due to the spirits’ characteristic disdain for metal. However, only silver and mithril silver are excluded from this subject. This is because silver is a metal that also exists in the spirit world and has inherently magical power. In particular, mithril silver is a metal that barely exists in the material world and has strong magical powers. This is why silver weapons can be used against monsters that are only harmed by magic weapons, but metals that are not silver or mithril silver will still inhibit spirit magic, even if they are endowed with magical power.
#2 is because the procedure for casting spells requires the use of one hand. Therefore, you must not wear clothing or armor that severely restricts movement of your arms, and your fingers must be completely free. You can use either your left or right arm to cast a spell. You may hold a two-handed weapon in only one hand while using the remaining hand to use spirit magic, but if you're armed with a weapon in one hand and a shield in the other, then unless you drop the weapon or shield on the ground or put one away, you cannot use spirit magic.
#3 is ultimately decided by the game master, but below are some guidelines for where spirit power can be found at work.

⬤Wind Spirits
It must be in a place where the wind blows in its natural state. The spirit power of wind does not work inside buildings, deep caves, or even underground passages.
⬤Earth Spirits
This is a power that’s at work in places where the ground is exposed or underground. Particularly strong spirit power is at work in the mountains. Since the power of water spirits is strong on the ocean floor and the bottoms of lakes, the spirit power of earth does not work there. Likewise, this spirit power does not work on the floors of buildings (whether they're stone or wood).
⬤Water Spirits
The spirit power of water is at work wherever there is water, such as the ocean or a lake. Even if you carry water in a water bottle, etc., if you open the lid, the spirit power will work.
⬤Fire Spirits
This spirit power is at work wherever there’s fire burning. You can also use fire from a torch you're carrying with you.
⬤Ice Spirits
This spirit power is at work wherever there is snow or ice.
⬤Mind Spirits
This spirit power is at work wherever there are living things with functioning minds. Of course if the caster him or herself is like that, there's no problem. Some of these types of spirits include those that control the nature of the mind, and those that control fluctuation of emotions. It's said that if one emotion has too much of an outburst, it can cause phenomena such as alienation or complete loss of functions of other spirits, which can lead to mental disorders.
⬤Plant Spirits
Very strong power is at work in forests, etc. but in places where plants and trees grow, they're only at work in small amounts.
⬤Other Spirits
These are the spirits that control light, darkness, and life. Explanations of where these are at work can be found under each individual spell.

#### 5.1.13.2 Basic Abilities of the Shaman Skill

Shamans have the following basic abilities, which they can use without any restrictions.

**Infravision**

Salamander and frau control fire and ice, as well as heat and cold. Infravision is the ability to see things dimly even in the dark and find places where the temperature is unusually different compared to the surrounding area, by sensing the workings of these spirits. However, you cannot see colors at all.

**Sense Aura**

This ability is used to know what kind of spirit power is at work in your surroundings. You cannot use spells that belong to a specific spirit in places where that spirit power is not at work. When a player declares that they will use this ability, the game master must tell the player what spirit power is at work in that location.
Since there are spirits of life and spirits of undeath (which control negative life force), the sense aura ability can be used to distinguish between living things and undead. Ordinary corpses and monsters created by magic (magical creatures) such as gargoyles do not emit either aura.
Sense aura is a subtle sensation, more like what we can smell than see. Even if you know that spirit power is at work, you won't be able to tell where that spirit power is coming from unless you get very close. Also, if there's extremely strong spirit power in your surroundings, you may not be able to sense weak spirit power at work.

**Silent Spirit**

These are runes used to communicate with spirits. Those who can use these runes can communicate with any spirit. Most spirits speak directly to the mind without using words, but when a caster gives commands to a spirit, they must speak these runes out loud. Also, some fae are well versed in silent spirit.

### 5.1.15 Holy Magic and the Priest Skill

Holy prayers are runes used to contact the great beings, the gods, and use that power as a source to perform various miracles. There are many gods in Sword World, each drawing worship from humans and other intelligent races.
In order to use holy magic, you need to have enough faith to be able to hear the voices of the gods. The gods' thought form is completely different from humans, therefore humans cannot fully understand it. This is because their words enter the mind as highly complex symbols. Runes called holy prayers were created during the ancient kingdom period as a means to give words to this and cause miracles.
The caster uses these holy prayers to pray to the gods and activate the power of the spells. In addition, each holy magic has special spells that can only be used by believers of a specific god. Those spells cannot be used by members of other sects.
The skill for using holy magic is called the priest skill, and those who have the priest skill are called priests.
Among the many gods that exist in Forcelia, there are also those called wicked gods. The teachings of these wicked gods are extremely inconvenient for protecting human society, and many of them threaten the lives and property of others. Because of their anti-social nature, the followers of these wicked gods tend to be abhorred, and the magic used by the followers of these wicked gods is called dark magic and is distinguished from holy magic. The priests of the wicked gods are not called priests, but dark priests.
Game masters should not allow characters to become priests of wicked gods. If you role-play according to the wicked gods' teachings, there is a high possibility that it will soon lead to the destruction of not only that PC, but also the party.

#### 5.1.15.1 Restrictions on Using Holy Magic

There are no restrictions on armor or weapons to use the priest skill, and priests can cast magic simply by chanting holy prayers. However, this action must be in accordance with the will of the god you believe in. If you try to use magic for a purpose that is far removed from the god's will, it may not work at all. Also, a priest who loses faith immediately loses their priest skill.
Also, one character cannot worship multiple gods. Those with the priest skill must choose only one god to worship.

#### 5.1.15.2 The Six Major Gods

Among the gods of Alecrast, the six most powerful gods (and those with the greatest number of believers) are called the six major gods. These six major gods almost always have temples in most towns.
These six major gods are Pharis (the supreme god), the guardian of order and law, Phalaris (the dark god), who rules over destruction and chaos, Myrii (the god of war), the guardian of war and victory, Rahda (the god of knowledge), who rules over wisdom and knowledge, Cha Za (the god of trade), who is the god of luck and commerce, and Marfa (the earth mother), the goddess of nature and agriculture, and the protector of marriage.
Next, we will briefly illustrate the teachings of the six major gods, so game masters should take this as a hint and use them as a guideline for a priest's actions.

**Pharis**

Pharis's teachings are for people to act in an orderly manner and to achieve unchanging justice in the world. It’s up to the game master to decide what is fair, but assault, murder, and for that matter, theft for fun, etc. cannot be called good acts. Also, he especially dislikes undead, monsters created by magic, or summoned from other planes as they’re impure (this is a characteristic common to most gods except wicked gods). His ardent believers include members of the ruling class such as kings, aristocrats, etc.

**Myrii**

He denies any acts of cowardice or cowardly behavior. He only supports righteous conflict, so he will not lend his strength to fights that are just for the sake of stealing or simply killing one another. His believers are knights and mercenaries.

**Rahda**

He doesn't have many restrictions, but he tends to dislike barbaric behavior. He teaches an intelligent way of life, and his doctrine is that if all things are wise, the world will be well. It seems that he has many believers among sages and sorcerers.

**Cha Za**

He prohibits causing misfortune to others. He also denies closed-minded thinking. He teaches that happiness can be achieved through conversation and interaction between people, such as through trade. Although he prohibits stealing, it's okay to claim something that doesn't have an obvious owner (such as lost property or treasure in ruins) as your own. His believers are the citizens living in towns.

**Marfa**

Her doctrine is expressed in the words be natural. This is different from the concept of nature-worship, and her ideal is for humans to be in their natural state. She approves of fighting for self-defense, but denies any other kind of fighting. She particularly encourages farming and hunting, which are basic activities indispensable for human survival. It seems that she has many believers among the poor, farmers, and hunters.

**Phalaris (Wicked God)**

Phalaris is a wicked god. Game masters should not allow player characters to worship this god.
Followers of Phalaris recognize all desires inherent in the individual and affirm any means necessary to enhance one's own existence. He teaches that it is okay to do something, even if it means destruction or murder, as long as it is for your own benefit. What his followers aim for is complete freedom, and they dislike succumbing to the constraints of law, order, and morality. It is clear that those who follow these doctrines must eventually become wicked. Although there is no shortage of believers, it seems they often remain hidden in order to protect their own position.
Temples of Phalaris often exist in large towns, but because he is considered a wicked god, they are confined to basements and secluded forests. However, the kingdom of chaos Phandria, located in the central plains region, has Phalaris as its state religion, and the largest Phalaris temple in Alecrast is built there.

These teachings are not formal, but express inner thinking. Therefore, players and game masters should use common sense when making judgments, rather than forcefully twisting the doctrine or interpreting it too rigidly. The gods see everything, but they also see from a vast perspective at the same time, so unless it's a very extreme act, they will not abandon a priest. However, this does not mean that everything is forgivable, so game masters should only confiscate the priest skill when the character repeatedly commits unjust acts or shows no remorse. Also, if this happens, don't forget to warn them first.

#### 5.1.15.3 Other Gods

In addition to the six major gods, the following gods are also worshiped in Alecrast. Among these are wicked gods whose teachings are to bring disaster to the world. Player characters cannot worship wicked gods.

**Fenes**

Fenes is the younger brother of the supreme god Pharis, and is said to govern things that repeat (cycles). During the war of the gods, it is said that he did not join the forces of darkness nor the forces of light led by his older brother Pharis, choosing instead to take the position of king of the neutral gods. Belief in him is not popular in Alecrast, and he has almost no temples. It is sometimes said that he is the true form of the Nameless God of Madness (a wicked god, explained later), and he is also said to be the god of beastmen such as werewolves.

**Wehner**

He is the god of the arts and also the god of bards. He is also believed to write history, and so is also characterized as a god of destiny. Although he does not have great power, he is widely believed in by the masses, and his temples can be seen from time to time. Belief in him is especially popular among artists.

**Ganeed**

Although he is said to be the god of thieves, he was originally the god of craftsmanship, and so has believers among craftsmen. He is also considered the god of gifts, as he brings unexpected wealth. Temples dedicated to Ganeed can sometimes be seen in large towns, but his power is by no means great.

**Braki**

He is known as the god of smiths.However, he was originally the god of volcanoes, and because he told people that when you heat a stone, it melts like water, he was worshiped as the god of smiths. He has many believers among those who work in the mines, and he even has the personality of a chief god, especially among the dwarves. As a result, most dwarven settlements have built shrines to Braki.
However, his temples are rarely seen in human cities.

**The Nameless God of Madness (Wicked God)**

He is the god of madness, and because of his madness, he is a deity whose name cannot be identified. It is said that he exists in the star world and causes people to go mad, and it is believed that those who come into contact with the light emitted by this mad god on a full moon's night will be afflicted with madness. His followers are few and he has no temples either. The priests of his cult teach that madness frees its believers from bondage and gives them freedom. It is said that he gives sudden inspiration that is not based in logic, and for this reason he is also considered the god of art and invention, so there are sometimes believers in this god among artists and sages.

**Kardis (Wicked God)**

She is feared as the goddess of destruction and the goddess of the undead, and is considered to be the goddess who controls the end. Believers consider destruction and slaughter to be their mission anyway, as they believe that without destruction there can be no new beginning. Due to the nature of her faith, she has no temples, etc. but her doctrines have been continually passed down throughout the past and present, so she has believers.

**Mirleaf (Wicked God)**

He is the god of the dead of the sea, and teaches that life is pain and sin, and that death is the only way to be saved from it. Mirleaf believers believe that new challenges and efforts to survive (as exemplified by voyages) create and prolong suffering, and that a prompt death is the only mercy for those who do such things.
He has almost no temples. Even if he did, they would never appear publicly.

Game masters can also create original gods and spells in addition to these. This is a good thing, but it would probably be safer not to delve into actual religious issues.

#### 5.1.15.4 Basic Abilities of the Priest Skill

Priests can use the following abilities.

**Divination**

This is the ability to pray and communicate with a god, asking whether or not one's actions are right. Occasionally a god will give you a revelation (given at the game master's discretion). However, a god's words can only be understood abstractly by humans, and you cannot ask detailed questions to the gods.
Revelations should not be used too conveniently for the characters (players). It must be done only when they absolutely need a word from their god to protect their faith.
There is no need for a god (the game master) to respond to nonsense such as, If I die in a trap here, I won't be able to believe properly, so let's ask if there is a trap at this door.

**Vow**

This is a voluntary vow. When a priest wants to do something, they can make a vow to their god. When a priest's actions contradict or move away from their vows, their god will give them some form of attention or assistance. If a priest of Marfa and his wife make a vow to never divorce at the time of their marriage, but then the relationship between the two turns sour, the two of them may end up having a child together. No vow can be made that violates your faith in your god. For example, a priest of Myrii can make a vow to defeat the traitor XX with my own hands, but a priest of Marfa cannot.

## 5.3 Spellsongs

A spellsong is a song with magical power that was created during the ancient kingdom period. Those with the bard skill can use magic, although weak, by singing these spellsongs.
However, bards are never called rune masters. This is because compared to the runes used by rune masters, spellsongs are only a superficial imitation. On the other hand, even grassrunners who have no magical background can use spellsongs, because they only need to prepare the superficial form.
To use a spellsong, you must play an instrument. This instrument must be unique to each bard, and the spellsong cannot be used when playing another instrument. Furthermore, you must sing the lyrics in high ancient at the same time. However, it doesn't matter if you don't know the meaning, as long as you know the lyrics by heart. Also, if you choose a flute as your instrument, you can use the spellsong without having to sing the lyrics.
Basically, the spellsong takes effect on everyone who hears the bard's performance. In other words, it affects both friends and enemies. However, it has no effect on those who are unable to hear. You can learn one spellsong per bard level. A bard chooses which spellsong they want to learn. Every time your level goes up, you have to learn a new spellsong.
When a bard uses the ability of a spellsong, you resolve the effect in the same way as magic. However, instead of magic power, the baseline score is bard skill level + mental power bonus (not intelligence bonus!).
Using spellsongs does not consume a bard's mental power. Also, the effect of a spellsong cannot be expanded.
A spellsong remains in effect as long as the bard sings it. However, once the song is interrupted, the effect disappears. As long as a bard is singing a spellsong, they cannot take any other actions and cannot move more than 3 meters in one round. However, unlike focus, it’s not interrupted by damage dealt to life force or mental power (unless, of course, you fall unconscious as a result of the damage). Resistance rolls against spellsongs are generally made only once at the beginning of the song (depending on the spellsong, they may be on the fourth round). Once you successfully resist, you do not need to make any further resistance rolls and will not suffer from the effect, even if the spellsong continues.

### 5.3.1 Spellsong List

The types and effects of spellsongs are shown below.
On the list, spellsongs with the note that says mind are spellsongs that affect the mind of the listener. These can be considered in the same way as magic that belongs to mind explained under 5.1.9: Magic Types. In other words, each one counters (overpowers): and cannot take an effect at the same time as magic or other spellsongs that also belong to mind.

**Early Bird (Mind)**

This spellsong has the effect of waking up sleeping creatures. At the same time, as long as this song continues, listening creatures will not be able to sleep.

**Curiosity (Mind)**

This spellsong has the effect of arousing the curiosity of those who hear it. The resistance roll is made on the fourth round after the song begins (count the beginning of the song as the first round, and make the roll when the bard's action order occurs on the fourth round). Those who fail to resist will be tempted to catch a glimpse of the performer. The result is that they'll come out of their hiding place and reveal themselves to the performer. **Summon Small Animals (Mind)**

If the summoned small animals are attacked, the effect of the spellsong will be broken and they will all run away.

**Sing (Mind)**

Anyone who hears this spellsong will start singing the same lyrics along with the bard's song. There’s no need to remember the lyrics. However, it has no effect on creatures that do not sing. Those affected will not be restricted in their actions, but as long as the spellsong continues, they will also be singing and will not be able to cast magic. Of course, they can't even converse, and they cannot use spellsongs or common runes.

**Dance (Mind)**

Those who hear this spellsong will begin to move their limbs and dance according to the melody on their own. Those affected suffer a -4 penalty to attack power and evasion speed. Ancient magic and spirit magic cannot be cast. Since you can't use your musical instrument, you can't use spellsongs either. You will also be unable to use most skills’ abilities.

This spellsong has the effect of charming those who hear it. Those who are, are not charmed by the bard him or herself, but rather charmed by their singing voice and performance. As long as the spellsong continues, those who are charmed will listen to the song in ecstasy. However, if you suffer physical or mental damage, you can make another resistance roll.
With this spellsong, a bard can make those they charm take specific actions. What you want them to do must be carefully woven into the lyrics. This weaving of lyrics must be in a language that is understandable to those who hear it (conversely, it does not have to be in high ancient). If forced to perform such an action, the charmed one can attempt a resistance roll again If the resist is successful, not only does this save you from having to take the action, but also breaks you free from the spellsong's charm effect completely.

**Peace (Mind)**

This spellsong has the opposite effect of Morale, causing those who hear it to lose their will to fight. Those affected suffer a -1 penalty to attack power (points).

**Healing**

Those who wish to take part in the effect of this spellsong must remain completely relaxed for the duration of the performance.

**March (Mind)**

**Morale (Mind)**

Those who hear this spell will be able to muster up the courage within their own hearts and fight bravely against the enemy. The resulting check score will be attack power (points) plus 1.

**Lullaby (Mind)**

# Chapter 11: NOTES ON MAGIC

> Source: Sword World.txt lines 8512–9356.

Along with weapon combat, magic is the star of Sword World RPG. This chapter provides supplementary explanations about magic and rune masters, and includes a list of dark magic and dragon roar magic that can only be used by NPCs.

## 11.1 Rune Masters' Status

In Alecrast, there aren't that many beings called rune masters. Rune masters are respected in a sense by the general public because they have special powers called magic. However, public sentiment towards magic is far from good. This is because, in the days of the ancient kingdom, citizenship was not recognized for anyone except rune masters, and all other people were used as slaves. Therefore, there is a great sense of crisis regarding the power of rune masters (especially sorcerers). Some believe that once their numbers become sufficient and they regain the power they had during the ancient kingdom, the age of darkness may come again.

Although rune masters are not openly persecuted due to the necessity of their presence, they are also viewed with fear and disgust by the general public. Countries that are negative towards magic sometimes have policies that exile rune masters from the country.

Of course, these people also rely on magical power when they themselves need it.

## 11.2 Dark Magic

As mentioned earlier, these runes, like holy magic, use the gods as its source of power. However, it is derived from the power of the gods of evil (wicked gods) such as Phalaris, who are believed to cause harm to us humans. For this reason, the rune masters of these runes are both hated and feared by the general public. A rune master who uses dark magic is called a dark priest. In a narrow sense, only the priests of Phalaris are sometimes called dark priests. This is because believers in wicked gods other than Phalaris are rarely seen.

Dark magic basically has the same origin as holy magic, and dark priests can use most of the basic holy magic. In addition, dark priests can use wicked magic that the gods of light do not allow (do not give power to). Furthermore, just as there is special holy magic that is unique to each god among holy magic, there is also special dark magic.

### 11.2.1 Basic Abilities of the Dark Priest Skill

Dark priests can use the following abilities:

**《 Divination 》**
Like the priest skill, this is the ability to pray and communicate with a god.

**《 Vow 》**
Similar to the priest skill, this is the ability to make a voluntary vow and have a god protect you. Wicked gods often impose harsh punishments on actions that violate their vows.

## 11.3 Dragon Roar Magic

Dragon roar magic are runes used by those who worship dragons as guardians rather than gods. In modern-day Alecrast, there is no custom of worshiping dragons in civilized countries, so no one uses this type of magic.
The skill for using this magic is the dragon priest skill, and its owners are called dragon priests.
It is said that there are users of dragon roar magic among uncivilized savage tribes and among the greater species of lizardmen, an intelligent race born from the scales of primordial titans such as dragons. However, even among the great sages, the actual situation is not well known.
Those who believe in dragons have only one goal, to be reborn as a dragon. For this reason, they train their bodies and minds and strive to be strong and free. Due to the nature of their faith, dragon priests do not form a cult or gather believers. Through repeated training, users of dragon roar magic can master the physical, mental, and magical transcendental abilities of dragons.
Since their lifestyle, such as eating only raw food, worshiping fire, regarding poison as sacred, etc. is not compatible with civilized humans, they are sometimes considered to be heretical and may be subject to persecution. It is a famous story that the evil dragon that destroyed the kingdom of Moraana, which had flourished in the central part of Alecrast, was the reincarnation of Krish, a user of dragon roar magic. However, the original cause of this incident was that the savage tribes on the shores of Lake Air, where Krish was born and raised, were all exterminated because they were evil believers who worshiped dragons.
Dragon priests will not join an adventurer's party due to their religious beliefs. It can be said that this skill is not suitable for a player character.
There are no restrictions when using dragon roar magic spells. If your consciousness is clear, you can receive the effects of a spell just by remembering it in your mind. Even if Silence or Mute spirit magic is cast on you, you can still use dragon roar magic!
No action is required.
Dragon priests worship dragons, so the dragon priest skill cannot be acquired at the same time as the priest skill (and dark priest skill).

### 11.3.1 Basic Abilities of the Dragon Priest Skill

Owners of the dragon priest skill gain the following basic abilities.

**[ Reptile Check [Intelligence] ]**
Dragon priests have knowledge of dragons, their subspecies such as wyrms, wyverns, etc. and reptiles. For these monsters only, they can identify them with a success roll using dragon priest skill + intelligence bonus as the baseline score, just like the sage skill's monster check.
For the summon magic described below, the dragon priest must know the creature they're trying to summon.

### 11.3.2 Dragon Roar Magic Spell List

Here is a list of dragon roar magic that dragon priests can use. Among the types of spells, there is one called summon. For this, please follow the summon rules below.

**《 Summon 》**
Spells of this type are spells that summon and employ the race specified by each spell. When using these spells, the caster must determine the final score of the spell. Then, compare this final score with the mental resistance score of the creature you're trying to summon. The result will determine when it will be summoned (Table 11-1: Summon).
Summoning takes place across space. Therefore, it's possible to instantly summon creatures no matter where they are. However, the closer the target creature is, the easier it will be to summon it. When using the Summon table, the game master can apply any modifiers as desired. If you can basically infer that they'll be in the surrounding 10km, please add +2. If you know for sure they're there, +4. The caster can also increase the final score through spell expansion or rituals.
However, no matter what modifiers are applied, if the 2D roll when calculating the final score is double ones, the summoned creature will immediately appear and attack the caster him or herself.
All summons are non-removable magic. Therefore, Dispel Magic has no effect. However, by using Pharis's special holy magic Banish, it's possible to drive the summoned creatures back to where they came from.

## 11.4 Acquiring Rune Master Skills and Increasing Levels

Here is a summary of how to acquire and increase the level of each rune master skill.

**《 Sorcerer Skill 》**
There are two ways to acquire the sorcerer skill. One is to join the sorcerers' guild. The other is to become an apprentice to a sorcerer with a sorcerer skill of 6th level or greater.
Then, you'll be given basic knowledge as a sorcerer, such as speaking and reading in high and low ancient, thus a sorcerer who can use 1st level spells is born. In this case, the rules require 2000 experience points (1500 points if you've acquired the sage skill). Also, although there are no specific restrictions in the rules, a training period of about 3 to 5 years is required. The sorcerer skill is increased by spending experience points. As your sorcerer skill increases, your magic power increases and at the same time you also immediately gain access to higher level spells. Please interpret this as because the sorcerer's spellbook has spells that they cannot handle written down in advance.

**《 Shaman Skill 》**
To acquire the shaman skill, you must be taught how to communicate with spirits by a shaman with a shaman skill of 1st level or higher. The art of communicating with spirits is not based on theory, but must be learned through experience, by watching and imitating other shamans. Although there are no specific restrictions in the rules, training for one year or more is usually required.
The shaman skill is increased by spending experience points. As your shaman skill increases, your magic power increases and at the same time you also immediately gain access to higher level spells. This is because your relationship with the spirits becomes stronger.

**《 Priest Skill 》**
To acquire the priest skill, it is necessary to awaken to faith. When you awaken to faith and recognize the existence of the gods, you can acquire the 1st level priest skill. Therefore, there may be no particular need for a master, teacher, or period of training. It is possible to suddenly feel Myrii's power while fighting and become a priest, or to feel heartbroken by the chaotic mayhem and, worried about what to do, become an apostle of Pharis.
However, the surest way to awaken to faith is to work in a temple, constantly see other priests, and receive their teachings. This is because it is the best way to experience the nature of faith firsthand.
The priest skill is increased by spending experience points. As your priest skill increases, your magic power increases and at the same time you also immediately gain access to higher level spells. This is because your relationship with your god becomes stronger.
In rare cases, if a character who had the priest skill believes that their previous teachings are wrong and awakens to the teachings of another god, they may lose any previous priest skill levels they had and acquire 1st level in a new god's priest skill level instead. From there, you will have to increase your priest skill again. If the game master wishes, on the basis that you rebelled greatly against your old faith, they may require only half the normal experience points to increase your level until you reach your previous priest skill level.

**《 Dark Priest Skill 》**
The method of acquiring and increasing the dark priest skill is almost the same as the priest skill. However, many of the dark gods that dark priests believe in have no temples, and most dark priests will be those who have awakened to faith by some occurrence. Many such awakenings could be things like a longtime believer of a god of light being betrayed by their god. There are also cases such as a friend who was a priest of Pharis who, in order to maintain order, turned in his own brother, who was plotting a rebellion, and after grief about his actions, awoke to the teachings of The Nameless God of Madness (though usually you'd just say they went mad about things like this...)

**《 Dragon Priest Skill 》**
Dragon priests are based on a unique belief in dragons. This is only possible if you're part of a group of people who believe in dragons (mostly considered savages).
The dragon priest skill is increased by spending experience points. By increasing their level, they can have deeper connections to dragons, and they can gain access to new powers (spells) immediately.

## 11.5 Asking NPCs to Use Magic

In some cases, adventurers may need to rely on NPCs to save them from situations that are beyond their control. You may want to have a Remove Curse cast on you when you're cursed, a Resurrection cast when someone dies, or have a magical item appraised using Analyze Enchantment.
Of course, these services are not always available, and when they are, they are not free.
First, the game master must decide whether there are enough rune masters to do this. High ranking (high level) rune masters are not easy to come by. Even in a large country, there may or may not be at least one rune master who has reached 10th level.
As a rough standard, here we've listed Table 11-2: NPC Presence by Level.

**Table 11-2: NPC Presence by Level**

| Level | Presence |
|--:|:--|
| 10th level | Royal capital level of a large country |
| 7th level | Large city in a large country, royal capital level of a small country |
| 5th level | Moderate city |
| 3rd level | Town of around 1000 people |
| 1st level | All but the most remote village |

Once you have found such a rune master, you must next determine whether or not your request will be granted. The higher-ranking rune masters are, the busier they are. They surely won't hear your requests too easily, and it's questionable whether they'd even allow you to meet them in the first place.
Basically, you can expect to meet any rune master up to 2 levels higher than the adventurers' adventurer level. If you want to meet someone at a higher level, you'll need some connections on the side. Priests are relatively easy to meet, but shamans and sorcerers are by no means easy to meet. All such decisions are made by the game master. Of course, it would be difficult to ask a single rune master to do all sorts of things again and again, and if the adventurers conclusively incur an NPC's displeasure, no amount of money will make them say yes.
And of course, a certain price is required in order to have the magic cast. The market price is NPC's rune master skill level × base mental power cost for magic × 20. If you have the magic expanded, the price will increase by double, triple...... etc. depending on the expansion rate. The same goes for having a ritual performed. If you want to increase the final score by +1, +2...... etc. through a ritual, you have to double, triple...... etc. the price you pay.
For such requests, you usually give 10% to 30% as advance payment. If the magic use fails, you do not have to pay the remainder.

**Example:**
Think about when you use Resurrection to bring back a friend who lost their life on an adventure. Let's say you have a corpse that's been dead for 5 days, but has been preserved via Preservation. At this time, the final score required for resurrection is 19.
You successfully find a 10th level priest and the negotiations are settled. If you want to cast Resurrection normally, the required money is 10 (priest's level) × 45 (base mental power cost for Resurrection) × 20 = 9000 (gamels). However, assuming the priest's intelligence bonus is +2, a 2D roll of 7 or greater is required for a successful resurrection.
Thinking that this is not enough, the adventurers ask the priest if they can expand the magic and increase the final score. The priest's mental power is 14, so it can be expanded by double (final score +1). The money required at this time is 18,000 gamels. If you roll 6 or greater on a success roll, it'll be a success.
To be even more certain, the adventurers request an additional +3 to their final score via ritual. If you do this, the resurrection will be successful unless you roll double ones. However, in order to do so, you must donate 9000 × 5 (because the eventual final score is +4) = 45,000 gamels to the priest (temple). In this case, the average advance payment is between 4,500 and 13,500 Gamels.

These are the going rates when an adventurer makes a request to an NPC. Adventurers themselves cannot go around charging these same rates. If you don't have clients, you won't get paid. Trying to force people to pay is, of course, a criminal act.

# Chapter 17: OPTIONAL RULES FOR MAGIC

> Source: Sword World.txt lines 17376–17473.

Along with weapon combat, the main focus in Sword World RPG is on magic. This chapter introduces optional rules regarding magic.

## 17.1 Changes to the Magic Procedure

You can unify the rules of magic, allowing for a more integrated approach to casting. However, in this case, the master's work becomes quite complicated.
The magic procedure is resolved the same way regardless of whether a monster or an adventurer is casting the spell. In other words, you roll 2D and add it to your magic power. Using that as the target score, everyone on the side on which it was cast must attempt a mental power resistance roll. If they fail, the magic will be cast with its full effect. If they succeed, the magic will not be cast or its effect will be reduced. However, if the caster rolls double ones, the magic will fail completely and have no effect.

**《 Monster Magic Power 》**
Monster magic power is predetermined for each monster.

**《 Monster Resistance 》**
The baseline score for a monster's mental power resistance roll is resistance points −7 (written in parentheses). However, a monster's resistance roll fails if there's a tie. The final score must exceed the final score from when the character cast the spell. This is an exception to the rule in order to maintain numerical balance. Please be careful.

**Example:**
A shaman is attacked by three skeletons. He decides to expand the spirit magic Fire Bolt by triple and cast it on each of them. His magic power is 4. His 2D roll is 8. The target score is 8 + 4 = 12, so the skeletons attempt to resist. A skeleton's resistance score due to their mental power is 9 (2), so the baseline score for the resistance roll is 2. A monster will fail to resist if the final scores are tied, so a skeleton needs 11 or greater to resist. If the game master rolls the dice three times and gets a 4, a 12, and an 8, then only the second skeleton will be able to reduce the effect of the Fire Bolt.

## 17.2 Lost Spells

Not all ancient magic spells are taught in the sorcerers' guild. Of course, no matter how high-level a sorcerer is, they don't know every spell. There are spells known only to a limited number of sorcerers, and also spells that were lost with the fall of the ancient kingdom. These are called lost spells.
Lost spells cannot be used simply by increasing your sorcerer skill level. You must know of a spell's existence and learn how to use it.
The learning methods are as follows.

**《 1. Obtain Spellbooks or Scrolls Containing Lost Spells 》**
Spellbooks and scrolls containing lost spells can be found as treasure in the ruins of the ancient kingdom, and by studying them, you can learn lost spells.

**《 2. Learn From a Sorcerer Who Knows Lost Spells 》**
There are some sorcerers who, as a result of years of hard work or through sheer luck, have acquired lost spells. You can learn lost spells by learning from these sorcerers.

However, even if you learn a lost spell, you cannot use it unless your sorcerer skill is of the spell's level or greater. If your skill level is insufficient, you must wait until your skill level increases before you can use it.
Which spells are considered lost is entirely up to the game master's choice. As a guideline, Table 17-1: Lost Spells is included, and the individual detail section of those spells also says lost under their type. However, if the game master wishes, spells not on the list may be designated as lost spells, and some or even all of the spells on the list may be designated as general spells.

## 17.3 Catalysts Other Than Staves

Basically, a mage's staff is used as a magic catalyst, but if the game master approves, you may use another object as a magic catalyst. Commonly used ones include mage's wands, mage's rings, mage's medals, etc. In any case, when chanting a spell, you must hold the magic catalyst in at least one hand, and cannot hold anything else in that hand at the same time.
Weapons other than staves (swords are preferred) can also be catalysts, and some sorcerers who call themselves magic warriors use them. However, rarely do people use shields or gauntlets as catalysts. This is because these are often ill-suited for the complex movements required by ancient magic spells. As a result of research, it is said that objects that do not inhibit finger movement at all, or single, homogeneous, rod-shaped objects are suitable for ancient magic.

## 17.4 Using Magic Without a Catalyst

To cast ancient magic, a sorcerer needs a magic catalyst to aid in focus. However, a character with the sorcerer skill might find it quite frustrating if they just dropped their staff and couldn't use their spells anymore. The game master may interpret this to mean that ancient magic can be used without a staff, but it cannot be handled effectively.
Following this interpretation, a sorcerer can cast spells without a catalyst. However, in this case, he or she will suffer a -2 penalty to the spell's final score, and furthermore, the spell will fail completely if the 2D roll made to determine the final score is not only double ones, but also 3 or 4.

## 17.5 Full Resistance

When a character anticipates danger or wants to resist magic cast by an opponent at all costs, they may be able to take the full resistance action during the round.
On any round you choose full resistance, your character gains a +4 bonus to resistance rolls. However, there are some restrictions to taking this action.
One is that the character cannot take any other actions (wielding a weapon, casting spells, etc.) during that round. Moreover, if you're hit by a physical attack, you'll suffer a -4 penalty to evasion. Also, the character's movement method can only be stand still. Character movement is limited to within 3 meters.
The other is that you must be aware of the direction in which the danger is coming from. You must be fully aware of the enemy, etc. that's trying to cast spells on you. At this time, it doesn't matter if you only vaguely define the direction (such as "The area around that trap door looks suspicious"). Also, if you predict danger from multiple locations, there's no problem so long as you're fully aware of each. However, if the game master feels that it is impossible to anticipate and keep an eye on dangers from so many different directions ("I think the statue in front of me, the mural on the right, behind the curtain on the left, as well as the ceiling, behind me, and under my feet are all dangerous, so I'll full resist anything that might come from those places!"), he or she can add restrictions or refuse the player's declaration. If you try to pay attention to what's behind you, which you can't see, you usually neglect what's in front of you.
When danger comes from an unexpected direction or location, you receive no bonus to resistance rolls and must make a check normally.
