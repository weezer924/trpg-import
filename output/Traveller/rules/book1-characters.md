## Introduction

> PDF p.5

Traveller deals with a common theme of science-fiction: the concept that an expanding technology will enable us to reach the stars and to populate the worlds which orbit them. The major problem, however, will be that communication — be it political, diplomatic, commercial, or private — will be reduced to the level of the 18th century, reduced to the speed of transportation. The result is a large (bordering on the infinite) universe ripe for the adventurer's bold travels.

Using this set of three books, players can play single scenarios or entire adventuring campaigns set in any science-fiction situation. These game rules are as complete as they could be and provide for solitaire and unsupervised play. But the main thrust of the game is the refereed or umpired situation. An independent referee allows a large degree of flexibility and continuity often not possible when players themselves control the game. A referee inserts some measure of uncertainty in the minds of the players as they travel through the universe. Finally, the referee deals with new situations that these rules may not cover; after all, no set of rules can totally define the universe and how it works.

Traveller is basically a conversation game. It requires pencil and paper for notes, lists, and computations. Beyond that, it calls for a handful of six-sided dice for the generation of random numbers useful in combat and commerce. Everything else is optional, and depends on who is playing and what they want to play.

Traveller is designed for mature people who have imagination and an interest in the worlds of science-fiction. Traditionally, a more experienced person serves as the game referee and controls the action of the scenario. A group will consist of two or more players and a referee; optimum group size is between three and ten players.

### Required Materials

**Necessary:**
- Traveller, Books 1, 2, and 3.
- **Dice.** Six-sided dice. Each player should have two; the referee should have more. Different colors are useful.
- **Paper.** Scratch, lined, and graph.
- **Writing Instruments.** Pencils, pens, erasers, and colored markers.

**Optional:**
- Other Traveller Books, Adventures, and Supplements.
- Miniature Figures (persons, aliens, beasts, vehicles, starships).
- Calculators.

### Playing the Game

> PDF p.5–6

There are three basic ways to play Traveller: solitaire, scenario, and campaign. Any of these may be unsupervised (players administer rules themselves) or refereed (recommended).

- **Solitaire.** One player undertakes a journey alone, handling the effects of the rules as play progresses. Ideal for players isolated by situation or geography, and well suited to generating characters, designing starships, and mapping subsectors for later group use.
- **Scenario.** Several players embark on a journey together with a stated goal (resembles a science-fiction novel). Usually a one-time affair. Example: players generate characters (Book 1), find a patron (Book 3), who sends them to Sirius after "diamonds as big as your fist."
- **Campaign.** Several players manipulate their characters across a series of continuing, linked adventures in a consistent universe. The referee generates the basic facts of the universe before play; as the campaign unfolds, players may range beyond the referee's original boundaries, temporarily halting the campaign while new data is prepared. Where the scenario is a novel, the campaign is a continuing series.

### Die Roll Conventions

> PDF p.6–7

Dice throws may be made by players for their characters or by the referee. Referee rolls may be kept secret when players would not actually know the exact result.

Generally, a dice throw involves two dice; exceptions using one or more dice are clearly stated.

- **Saving Throw (also called Throw):** the dice roll required to achieve a stated effect.
  - A bare number (e.g., `8`) must be rolled exactly.
  - `8+` = that number or greater.
  - `6-` = that number or less.
  - The sign **follows** the number.
- **Die Modifier (DM):** a number applied to a die roll before it is used. DMs are preceded by a sign: `+4` is added, `-2` is subtracted.

### The Referee

Crucial to the continuing campaign is the referee; he or she actually creates a universe and catalogs the creatures and societies which populate it. The referee creates a star map of a subsector and generates the specific details of the worlds within it. Initially only clues (sometimes false or misleading) about the universe are available to the players. The referee may indicate possible quests through rumors, barroom conversations, and so-called general knowledge.

The referee is responsible for maintaining master maps and charts, determining the effects of natural forces and chance, running non-player characters, and settling rule disputes (using imagination rather than strictly adhering to the letter of the rules).

### Getting Started

- **Book 1** contains rules for generating individual characters plus personal combat rules and weapon types. Best place for players to begin.
- **Book 2** covers starships: basic transportation, trade and commerce, ship design and construction.
- **Book 3** covers alien worlds and star systems. The referee generates worlds before the travellers arrive; players explore them afterward.

## Characters

> PDF p.8

Characters are the central focus of Traveller; they are the alter-egos of the players, and all activity is centered on them. The character is the Traveller personality; the player is the person engaged in playing the game. Once generated, a character continues to live and adventure until killed in action, or until too old and decrepit to keep up.

All characters begin the game the same way: untrained, inexperienced, and about **18 years of age**.

### Initial Character Generation

Characters are generated through a series of six two-dice rolls which determine the basic characteristics affecting abilities and reactions. Roll 2D for each of the six characteristics and record the results.

### Characteristics

| 英文               | 中文     | 说明                                                         |
|--------------------|----------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Strength (Str)     | 力量     | 角色身体能力的整体评估，以及可施加的具体力量。               |
| Dexterity (Dex)    | 敏捷     | 身体协调能力。                                               |
| Endurance (End)    | 耐力     | 身体决断力与体能储备。                                       |
| Intelligence (Int) | 智力     | 相当于 IQ。                                                   |
| Education (Edu)    | 教育     | 所受最高正规教育程度。                                       |
| Social Standing (Soc) | 社会地位 | 角色（及家族）所处的社会阶层。                               |

- 初始值范围：**2–12**（平均值 7）。
- 经过各种修正后，最终范围：**1–15**。
- 玩家角色的特征值上限为 15，下限为 1，除非遭遇严重伤害或年老衰退。

Obviously, it is possible for a player to generate a character with seemingly unsatisfactory values; nevertheless, each player should use the character as it is created. The experience procedures and acquired skills table offer a genuine opportunity to enhance values, given only time and luck. Should a player truly consider the character so poor as to be beyond help, the low survival rate of the Scout Service may make it the best career choice.

### Universal Personality Profile (UPP)

Characters in Traveller are precisely defined using the **universal personality profile (UPP)**, which expresses the basic characteristics in a specific sequence using hexadecimal (base 16) numbers. Digits 0–9 are the common Arabic numbers; digits 10–15 are letters A–F. The highest single digit in base 16 is 15 (F). Characteristics are listed as a string of six digits in the original order: **Str · Dex · End · Int · Edu · Soc**.

Examples:
- A totally average character: `777777`.
- Highly intelligent character (Int 11): `777B77` (the B in the 4th position).

While the use of the UPP is optional, it allows the referee (and players) to tell at a glance the characteristics of persons they encounter.

|---------:|---------:|----------------------------------|
| B        | 11       | Knight, Knightess, Dame          |
| C        | 12       | Baron, Baronet, Baroness         |
| D        | 13       | Marquis, Marquesa, Marchioness   |
| E        | 14       | Count, Countess                  |
| F        | 15       | Duke, Duchess                    |

### Naming, Titles, Rank

**Naming.** Once generated, the character should be named. Several schools of thought exist — using the player's own name (convenient, but loses campaign flavor), or pseudonyms / fanciful names (Alexander Lascelles Jamison; Timothy Fairweather; more extremely, Seeker or Starkiller).

**Titles.** Any character with Social Standing B (11) or greater may assume his or her family's hereditary title. Noble titles are commonly used even if the individual is not engaged in local government. At the referee's discretion, a noble may have ancestral lands or fiefs, and may actually have some ruling power.

- A knight (Soc B) is entitled to **Sir** (or **Dame**) before the name.
- Social Standing C entitles the individual to **Baron** or **Baroness**, or in lieu may use the prefix **von**, **haut**, or **hault** (denoting baronial nobility).

**Rank.** Characters receiving commissions or promotions (as explained below) may use their rank as part of their names, even after leaving the service or retiring.

## Acquiring Skills and Expertise

> PDF p.9–11

A newly generated character is singularly unequipped to deal with the adventuring universe, having neither the expertise nor the experience necessary for the active life. In order to acquire some experience, it is possible to enlist in a service.

### Enlistment & Draft

**Enlistment.** A character may choose one of the six services (**Navy, Marines, Army, Scouts, Merchants, Other**) and attempt to enlist. The **Prior Service Table** gives the enlistment throw required. Successfully throwing the number or higher on 2D allows enlistment. Most services allow DMs if the character has one or two stated characteristics at a given level or higher. If both stated characteristics are present at the required level, the DMs are **cumulative**.

Example: Navy enlistment throw is `8+`; DM +1 for Int 8+, DM +2 for Edu 9+. A character with Int 6 and Edu 10 gets DM +2 only. Rolling 3 (+2 = 5) is insufficient, so he is rejected.

Only **one** enlistment attempt is permitted per character. If rejected, he must submit to the draft. Enlistment or draft is not allowed after age 18.

**The Draft.** If enlistment fails, the character rolls 1D and enters the service with that draft number (see Prior Service Table). It is possible for a character to be drafted into the very service which rejected his enlistment.

Draftees are **not** eligible for commissions during their first term of service; they become eligible during the second and subsequent terms if they reenlist.

### Terms of Service & Survival

**Terms of Service.** Upon enlistment (or being drafted), a character embarks on a term of service lasting **four years** (adds 4 years to age). Each reenlistment is an additional 4-year term.

**Survival.** Each term involves some danger; the character must throw his service's **survival number** (with any DMs) to avoid death. Failure results in death and a new character must be generated.

- *Optional Rule:* If indicated prior to generation, a failed survival roll may be converted to **injury**. The character is not dead, but is injured and leaves the service (after recovery) having served only two years of the four-year term.

### Commissions and Promotions

Each service (except Scouts and Other) has a **commission number**. The character must throw that number to be commissioned as an officer (rank 1). DMs may apply. A character may attempt once per term until successful, with the exception that a **draftee may not attempt a commission in the first term**.

In the same term a commission is received, and in each subsequent term, a character may also attempt **promotion** (throwing the promotion number with any DMs). If successful, the character advances to the next higher rank. **One promotion per term** maximum.

Commissions and promotions are **not available** in the Scout Service or Other Service.

### Skills and Training

During each term of service, a character has the opportunity to acquire personal skills and expertise:

**BASIC SKILL ELIGIBILITY**

| 触发条件                         | 技能数 |
|----------------------------------|-------:|
| For the initial term of service  | 2      |
| Per subsequent term of service   | 1      |
| Upon receiving a commission      | 1      |
| Upon receiving a promotion       | 1      |

Skills are acquired by rolling on the Acquired Skills Table once for each skill allowed, using **one die**. There are **four** tables, each containing different general types of skills. One table is chosen **before** the die is rolled, and the single die roll indicates the specific skill.

- The first three tables may always be used by a character.
- The fourth (Advanced Education, Edu 8+) is available only to characters with Education 8 or greater.

During the first term, the character is eligible for 2 skills; each additional term grants 1. A commission grants +1; a promotion grants +1. Thus a Navy character who receives a commission and promotion in the initial term is eligible for **4 skills** that term.

Some skills automatically accrue to a character (without using eligibility) by virtue of rank or service. See **Rank and Service Skills** table.

**Scout Service exception.** Scouts have no rank or promotion, so they do not become eligible for extra skills that way. Instead, scouts receive **2 skills for each term of service, including the first**.

### Reenlistment & Retirement

**Reenlistment.** Each service has a reenlistment number. At the end of each term, a character must throw that number or greater (no DMs allowed) to undertake another term.

- Failure = the person must leave the service.
- Throw **12 exactly** = mandatory reenlistment; the needs of the service force another term regardless of personal desires.

The reenlistment throw is required during **each** term of service, even if the character does not intend to reenlist.

**Retirement.** A character may serve up to **seven terms** voluntarily, and may leave after any term (provided mandatory reenlistment did not occur). A person may **retire any time after the end of the fifth term**. Retirement grants an annual pension (see Retirement Pay table) in addition to any mustering-out benefits.

Service beyond the seventh term is normally impossible, and retirement is mandatory after the seventh. However, persons who throw mandatory reenlistment must serve that additional term — it is theoretically possible to be required to serve a ninth or tenth term under mandatory reenlistment.

## Mustering Out

> PDF p.11–12

When a character leaves the service (for any reason), he or she is eligible for mustering-out benefits. Two tables matrix benefits by service and a single die roll: the **Benefits Table** (travel, education, material) and the **Cash Table** (severance pay).

**MUSTERING OUT BENEFITS — rolls allowed**

| 条件                    | 额外次数 |
|-------------------------|---------:|
| Per term of service     | 1        |
| If rank 1 or 2          | +1       |
| If rank 3 or 4          | +2       |
| If rank 5 or 6          | +3       |

**Allowed Die Modifications**
- If rank 5 or 6: **DM +1** on the Benefits Table.
- If character has gambling skill: **DM +level** on the Cash Table.

One benefit roll is allowed for each term served. Additional rolls from rank are added on top. Characters may freely choose between the Benefits Table and Cash Table for each roll, **but no one may consult the Cash Table more than three times during the mustering-out process**. The player must designate the table **before** rolling.

Example: an uncommissioned character who served four terms is eligible for 4 rolls — she may split them as she wishes between the two tables, provided she uses the Cash Table no more than three times.

## Aging

> PDF p.12

Because each term of service is four years long, a character can potentially age twenty or more years before venturing into adventure. This aging may have a detrimental effect on Strength, Dexterity, Endurance, and even Intelligence.

When a character reaches **age 34** (end of the 4th term of service), aging begins. The Aging Table must be consulted then, and thereafter at **four-year intervals**. If a specific throw is failed, the reduction indicated is applied to the characteristic.

The use of **drugs** (to speed up or slow down body chemistry) or **low passage** (suspended animation) will make the character age faster or slower than a strict calendar. It is quite important that each player maintain careful records of physical age.

The negative number is the potential reduction in the characteristic **if the saving throw (on 2D; throw shown in parentheses) is not made**. Term of service refers to the end of that numbered term; age refers to the first day of the personal (physical, not chronological) year.

### Aging Crisis

If, as a result of aging, a characteristic is reduced to **zero**, the character is considered to have had an **aging crisis** and become quite ill.

- Basic saving throw of **8+** applies to avoid death (subject to a DM for the expertise of any attending medical skill).
- If survived, recovery is immediate (under **slow drug**, which speeds up body chemistry). The character ages immediately (1D = the number of months added in age under slow drug), but returns to play without delay.
- The characteristic reduced to zero automatically becomes **1**.
- This process occurs each time (and for each characteristic) a stat is reduced to zero.
- If slow drug is not available, the individual is incapacitated for the rolled number of months **if the 8+ throw succeeds** (otherwise, death).

## Non-Player Characters

> PDF p.13

Sometimes (often) player-characters will encounter people not actually manipulated by a player — thugs or assailants, potential hirelings or patrons. Their skills and abilities are determined by the referee using the character generation procedure.

Example: a starship captain looking for a crew would have the referee generate characters until one has the required skill (navigation, medical, etc.). The first appropriate character to be generated would present himself or herself for employment; if not accepted, a suitable delay occurs before another becomes available. Alternatively, the referee may simply generate a character and assign the required skill plus a few others for variety.

**Loyalty and Dedication.** In most cases, non-player characters will be dependable and loyal (assuming the absence of bad treatment). However, a seemingly loyal NPC may have foul play at heart. The referee should use the character reaction table (Book 3) to determine such potential. Continued loyalty depends on treatment and the level of skill of their employing player-characters.

**Character Generation.** Most players new to Traveller spend some time generating various character types. It is recommended that the referee save these for future use as NPCs, hirelings, and other types.

## Acquired Skills Tables

## Skills and Benefits — Categories

> PDF p.16

The skills and benefits acquired during a term of service are of greatly diverse types and values. There are a finite number of skills listed, and it is possible that one specific skill may be acquired more than once — in such cases, the second acquisition increases expertise in that skill. Upon first acquisition, the player writes the skill name followed by a dash and the number 1 (thus: `navigation-1`). Each additional acquisition increments the level: `navigation-2`, `navigation-3`, etc.

Benefits are of **three basic types**: characteristic alterations, weapon expertise, and basic skills.

### Characteristic Alterations

Expressed as an addition (or subtraction) to a characteristic: `+1 Strength`, `+1 Dexterity`, `+1 Endurance`, etc. Applied immediately and require no further attention.

### Weapon Expertise

Expressed as a general trait — **brawling, blade combat, gun combat, gunnery**. When acquired, the character notes the area and immediately selects the specific type of skill (Gun Combat → specific firearm; Blade Combat → specific blade or polearm). Gunnery and Brawling have no subdivisions.

All player-characters have an innate weapon expertise of **level-0** in all weapons. Acquiring a weapon skill boosts that weapon to **level-1**. Additional acquisitions in the same weapon increment the level by 1.

### Brawling

A general skill for hand-to-hand combat. Includes the use of hands, clubs, and bottles as weapons.

### Blade Combat

A specific skill in the use of blades and polearms. The character may select one blade or polearm each time Blade Combat is received. The **Blades and Polearms** table indicates the weapons available and shows:

- **+DM column** — minimum Strength required to receive strength bonuses for the weapon.
- **−DM column** — Strength level (or less) that causes mandatory penalties.
- **Wounds column** — degree of wounding ability (relative power).

A character may choose, for example, `cutlass-2` (two cutlass picks) and `dagger-1` if Blade Combat is received 3 times, or concentrate on one weapon (e.g., `foil-3`).

> Note: one of the possible selections is named **blade**. Players must designate the specific weapon selected; otherwise, the skill defaults to the edged weapon named "blade".

Blade combat is fully explained in the Personal Combat section (see `book1-combat.md`).

## Skill Descriptions

> PDF p.17–23. All skills not otherwise covered above. Referee notes are preserved.

### Administration (Admin)

The individual has had experience with bureaucratic agencies, and understands the requirements of dealing with them and managing them.

- **Contact with officials**: basic throw **7+** to successfully resolve normal interaction (police harassment avoidance, prompt license issuance, etc.). DMs: no expertise −3; per level +2.
- **Serving in a bureaucratic organization**: DM +1 per level for competency in superiors' eyes. Exact throw determined by referee.
- **Referee:** Admin expertise should also affect the quality of work and potential for success of an organization the character is controlling or managing.

### Air/Raft

Training and experience in the use and operation of the air/raft, floater, flier, and all types of grav vehicles.

- The air/raft is the major transportation vehicle on most worlds with high enough tech levels.
- Dangerous to operate at high speeds or in bad weather: basic throw **5+** to avoid an accident. DMs: per level +1; extreme weather / old craft / gunfire involved −1.
- **Referee:** roll once for a short chase, twice or three times for longer flights. Book 3 p.154 has full description.

### ATV

All Terrain Vehicle skill; ATV includes AFV (armored fighting vehicle). Used on frontier and airless worlds or where weather/atmosphere/intent precludes air/raft or aircraft.

- ATVs are reliable: throw **11+** per day for mechanical breakdowns.
- Susceptible to off-road problems (mud, sand, jungle). Driver DM +1 per level helps avoid difficulty.
- **Referee:** ATV skill also allows increased speed/maneuverability, diagnosis of malfunctions, repair, and preventive maintenance.

### Bribery

Experience bribing petty and not-so-petty officials to circumvent regulations. Does not guarantee success but minimizes bad effects if rebuffed.

- Throw: the world's **law level or less** (on 2D, plus a cash offer) for the official to do as asked. If refused, a second roll may be made with cash offer **doubled**.
- The character offering the bribe should first roll on the reaction table (Book 3) and should not offer to a negatively reacting official.
- DMs: no expertise +5; per level −1; if official is a strong friend on the reaction table (roll of 12) −2.
- **Referee:** Ensure both the cash offered and the act solicited are reasonable; apply appropriate DMs. If a bribe is not accepted, throw **3−** for the offer to be reported to higher authorities.

### Computer

Skilled in the programming and operation of electronic and fibre optic computers, both ground and shipboard.

- Characters with computer expertise can be hired (when needed) to operate and program computers.
- Starship programs (Book 2) may be written if the character has access to a suitable computer, knowledge of the skill being incorporated, and no other duties during each week of work.
- **Referee:** Throw **7 exactly** for a fatal error to be written into the program. If none, throw **5−** for a negative DM to appear when used; half chance the DM is −1 or −2. Flaws remain hidden until actually used in space combat.
- **Referee:** Follow the general procedure in Book 2 for writing programs.

### Electronics

Skill in the use, operation, and repair of electronic devices (including energy weapons).

- Expertise allows a DM on throws to understand, repair, assemble, or operate electronic items.
- Complex items may also require certain Education or high Intelligence; many devices also require some Dexterity.
- **Referee:** Throws depend on the specific situation; many are impossible without accumulated DMs from expertise, education, dexterity, intelligence, and availability of parts/tools.
- **Referee (throw construction):** To generate a specific throw, analyze the specific circumstances and select a number to be thrown (usually that number or greater to succeed). Allowed DMs: **level of electronics skill**, **+1 for Intelligence above ~10**, **+1 for Education above ~9**, and appropriate values for lack of tools (perhaps **−5**) or poor conditions (maybe **−3**). The throw is then made and success determined by the result. Restrict such throws to **one per specific time period** (an hour, four hours, a day, or a week, as appropriate).

### Engineering

Skilled in the operation and maintenance of starship maneuver drives, jump drives, and power plants.

- Engineer is essential for proper operation of any starship. Expertise qualifies an individual for such jobs; can be used to get working passage.
- **Referee:** Skill level is generally a DM on throws to remedy an engineering problem. DM may be assigned at **+2 per level**.

### Forgery

Skill at faking documents and papers with a view to deceiving officials, banks, patrons, etc.

- Encounters with officials who might inspect documents happen when the law level for a world or less is thrown (generally once or twice per day).
- Throw for **detection** of forged documents: **6+**. DMs: **−2 per level of expertise**.
- **Referee:** Forgery is useful, but control is required to prevent blatant misuse. Administration and Bribery skills may determine whether documents are actually examined.

### Forward Observer

Training (in military service) to call on and adjust artillery (projectile, missile, laser) fire from distant batteries and from ships in orbit.

- First shots invariably miss the target. On each subsequent turn (2-minute turns), basic throw **11+** to hit.
- DMs: per level +4; per turn of adjustment +1; if adjusting person has no expertise −4.

### Gambling

Well informed on games of chance and wise in their play. Should not be confused with general risk-taking.

- **Organized games** (casinos): bets up to Cr5000, throw **9+** to win.
- **Private games**: bets Cr50–Cr5000, throw **8+** to win.
- Gambling skill DM: +1 per level. **The house always wins on a throw of 2 exactly.**
- Games may be **crooked** (throw 10+ to be dishonest); referee stacks odds against players.
- **Gambling-3 or better** usually detects crooked games (throw 7+).
- **Gambling-4 or better** may be suspected of cheating and ejected (throw **9+** to be suspected; DM −1 per level over 4). Characters may use a lower expertise level to avoid detection.
- **Referee:** Don't divulge players' die rolls when gambling; just inform them of wins and losses, to conceal any manipulation.

### Gunnery

(See above under *Skills and Benefits — Categories*.)

### Jack of All Trades

The well-rounded individual (the renaissance man), uncommon but naturally proficient.

- General ability applicable to nearly any endeavor at the referee's discretion.
- Confers **skill level-0** in every other skill (but never level-1).
- Can attempt activities not normally possible due to absence of skills. Example: no medical expert present at an aging crisis — Jack-o-T can substitute, with the referee considering appropriate characteristics (Int, Edu), equipment (drugs, instruments), and other factors.
- Never sufficient to achieve standing in another skill: using Jack-o-T in a medical situation does not imply medical skill; piloting a ship in an emergency does not imply Pilot skill.

### Leader

Has led troops in battle (or on adventures); possesses knowledge and self-assurance for emergent or appointed leadership.

- Required to control a group of more than **6** non-player hirelings or soldiers — they will obey the character with the highest Leader expertise. Reaction throws are necessary when leader and group first meet; DM **+1 per level of Leader** when consulting the reaction table (Book 3).
- **Leader-3 or better**: soldiers obey orders without hesitation.
- **Leader-4 or better**: positive DM when recruiting soldiers/hirelings for adventures. Exact throws depend on the situation.

### Mechanical

Skill in the use, operation, and repair of mechanical devices (excludes engineering; includes non-energy weapon repair).

- Allows easy operation of mechanical devices and quick/efficient repair.
- Harder tasks (fabricating a main drive bearing as a ship plunges into a sun) are harder than easy ones (repairing a broken air lock hatch in port). Affected by tool availability, Strength, Dexterity, Education, and situation.

### Medical

Training and skill in the medical arts and sciences. Medical services are in great demand. Levels represent increasingly better ability:

- **Medical-1**: qualifies as medic on a starship crew.
- **Medical-2 or better**: DM +1 when reviving low passengers (each normally throws **5+** to revive; otherwise the passenger dies).
- **Medical-3**: sufficient to be called **doctor** — assumes a license to practice medicine, write prescriptions, handle most ailments, deal with other doctors professionally. Requires **Dex 8+** to also be a **surgeon**.

### Xeno-Medicine

Medical expertise normally applies to humans (and to a limited extent animals on human worlds). Applying to non-human aliens imposes a **−2** reduction in effective skill level. Example: a human doctor (medical-3) treating an unfamiliar alien operates at medical-1. The referee should consider strange environments and unfamiliar chemistry.

### Navigation

Training and expertise in the art and science of interplanetary and interstellar navigation.

- Qualifies for **navigator** position on a starship or interplanetary vessel.
- **Referee:** On exploratory missions or in unexplored territory, can assist with speedy computation and determination of courses, and position when lost or strayed.
- Also assists with land or sea navigation: DM **+1 per level** on throws to determine direction/location — requires the night sky to be visible from the planetary surface.

### Pilot

Training and experience in the operation of starships and large interplanetary ships. Encompasses both interplanetary and interstellar aspects of large ship operation.

- Qualifies for **pilot** position on a starship or interplanetary vessel **over 100 tons**.
- **Referee:** Used as a DM for handling of starships. Pilot refers primarily to interstellar ships; much ordinary operation is similar to interplanetary craft and Pilot also applies to **large interplanetary craft (100 tons and up)**.
- **Small interplanetary craft (under 100 tons)** handle differently: **Pilot − 1** = Ship's Boat expertise for small interplanetary vessels. Thus Pilot-3 = Ship's Boat-2 for small craft. (The reverse is **not** true.)

### Ship's Boat

Familiar with the function and operation of small interplanetary craft (5–100 tons), including shuttles, lifeboats, launches, ship's boats, and fighters. **Pilot − 1** may be used as equivalent Ship's Boat expertise.

- **Referee:** Used as a DM on throws during small-craft operations. Example: pinnace under attack, flown by a character with Ship's Boat-2:
  - Throw **10+** to escape on contact (DM +2 from skill).
  - Throw **8+** to avoid being hit by enemy fire if escape fails (DM +2).
  - Alternate until escape or hit. If hit: throw **5+** for the pinnace to be crippled and boarded; throw **4−** to be destroyed; no DMs apply to damage throw.
- Landing in bad weather: throw **9+**, DM **+2 per level of expertise above 1**.

### Steward

Experienced in the care and feeding of passengers (ship's steward duties). Anyone can be hired as steward, but this skill represents training and is an advantage when seeking such a job.

- **Referee:** Steward represents general awareness of cooking, personal care and attention, and other duties that keep passengers and crew happy with their conditions of passage.

### Streetwise

Acquainted with the ways of local subcultures (which tend to be similar everywhere in human society). **Not** the same as alien contact experience.

- Close-knit subcultures (lower classes, trade groups, underworld) generally reject strangers. Streetwise allows contact to obtain information, hire persons, purchase/sell contraband or stolen goods, and other shady activities.
- **Referee:** After establishing throws for activities (e.g., name of a cooperative licensing official: **5+**; location of quality guns at low prices: **9+**), allow Streetwise as a DM. If no Streetwise is used, impose **DM −5**.

### Tactics

Training in small-unit tactics (up to 1000 troops or individual spaceships). **Not** strategy (which is the realm of players, not characters).

- Provides advantage in gaining victory or reducing defeat when small units encounter hostile forces.
- **Referee:** Intangible — the exact result is left to the referee to implement. May influence information available in hidden-movement miniature resolution, or be applied as a DM in crucial situations. Should be considered when the character is applying for employment where such skill is useful.

### Vehicle

Skilled in the type vehicle commonly available in society. The individual immediately selects **one vehicle group** and gains one level in that specific type. Groups available:

- **Aircraft** — select Helicopter, Propeller-driven Fixed Wing, or Jet-driven Fixed Wing
- **Grav Vehicle**
- **Tracked Vehicle**
- **Wheeled Vehicle**
- **Watercraft** — select Small Watercraft, Large Watercraft, Hovercraft, or Submersible

Individual vehicles within a class may be found in Book 3 p.20+. In the case of **Aircraft and Watercraft**, other similar vehicles within the group may be operated by the individual at **skill level − 1**. Vehicle skill is used for operation, as a DM against accident, and may be used for repair/maintenance assistance.

### Vacc Suit

Trained and experienced in standard vacuum suits (space suits), including armored battle dress and suits for exotic/corrosive/insidious atmospheres.

- Basic throw **10+** to avoid a dangerous situation whenever any non-ordinary maneuver is attempted while wearing a vacc suit (running, jumping, hiding, jumping untethered ship-to-ship, etc.). DM **+4 per level**.
- When such an incident occurs: remedy throw **7+** by any character with Vacc Suit skill (including the endangered one). DMs: per level +2; no expertise −4.
- **Battle dress** and **combat armor** are special forms of armored vacc suit and require at least **Vacc Suit-1** to wear at all.

## Other Skills

The above list is not exhaustive. Additional skills may be encountered.

- **Creating New Skills.** The experience rules of Book 2 indicate methods for characters to learn additional skills after beginning active adventuring, and cover requirements for creating a new skill not otherwise detailed (e.g., a newly-developed laser pistol would need a new skill).
- **Default Skills.** Often characters have no skill appropriate to a situation (e.g., crossing a vacuum plain with no one trained in vacc suit). The referee may indicate all unskilled individuals have the relevant skill at **level-0**. Level-0 indicates orientation (ordinary activities OK, dangerous or fancy actions not). **Level-0 is not a stepping stone to level-1**.
- **Maximum Skills.** As a general rule of thumb, a character may have **no more skills (or total of levels of skills) than the sum of his or her Intelligence and Education**. For example, a character with UPP `77894A` would be restricted to a total of **13** combined skills and levels of skills (Int 9 + Edu 4 = 13). **This restriction does not apply to level-0 skills.**

**Travellers' Aid.** The Travellers' Aid Society (TAS) is a private organization maintaining hostels and facilities at all class A and B starports in human space. Available (at reasonable cost) to members and guests.

- Membership may be acquired upon mustering out (Benefits Table result `Travellers'`). Further receipt of this benefit has no effect — membership may be achieved only **once per character**.

- Membership is for the life of the character and is **not transferrable**.

### Material Objects

**Weapons.** The Benefits Table shows `Blade` or `Gun` — the character chooses any weapon in the category. If the same benefit is received again, the character may:

1. Take another example of the same weapon;
2. Select a different weapon;
3. Take the benefit as **+1 skill** in the weapon previously received (only in a weapon received as a benefit).

Example: benefit `Blade` → character chooses cutlass. Next benefit `Blade` → may take another cutlass, pick a different blade (e.g., foil), or take cutlass-1 expertise.

**Starships.** Two types are available:

- **Type A Free Trader** — 200-ton cargo vessel (freight + passengers). Fully described in Book 2. Receipt confers possession of the ship but also liability for monthly payments (**~Cr 150,000 / month**) for the next **40 years**. Fuel, crew, other expenses handled by the character.
  - If received again, each additional receipt represents actual possession for a 10-year period — the ship is 10 years older and total payment term is reduced by 10 years.
  - Rolling the ship benefit **5 times** total (once to obtain + 4× to pay off four 10-year sequences) gives the character a ship **free and clear** — and 40 years old.
- **Type S Scout/Courier** — 100-ton dispatch vessel. Scout service policy makes surplus scout ships available to selected individuals on a **reserve basis**. Only one Scout Ship may be acquired; further receipt has no effect.
  - Possession is at the pleasure of the scout service; the ship **cannot be sold**.
  - Fuel is **free** at scout bases. Maintenance is **free** at scout bases at class B starports.
  - Character responsible for upkeep and crew costs. Ship and pilot are available for recall to duty when needed.

### Characteristic Alterations (Benefits)

The Benefits Table also provides characteristic alterations — typically increases to Education or Intelligence, applied to the character immediately.

## Character Generation Example

> PDF p.25–26. Actual die throws in brackets.

## Character Generation Checklist

> PDF p.26

1. Generate the six personal characteristics (all 2D throws).
   - A. Strength
   - B. Dexterity
   - C. Endurance
   - D. Intelligence
   - E. Education
   - F. Social Standing
2. Select a branch of service and apply for enlistment.
   - A. If accepted, go to step 3.
   - B. If rejected, submit to the draft.
3. Determine survival.
4. Officer Rank.
   - A. If uncommissioned, apply for commission.
   - B. If commissioned, apply for promotion.
5. Determine skills received this term.
   - A. For service.
   - B. For commission received.
   - C. For promotion received.
   - D. Automatic skills.
6. If completing the 4th or later term of service, check for aging effects.
7. Roll for permission to reenlist.
   - A. For voluntary (or mandatory) reenlistment, go to step 3.
   - B. If disallowed, go to step 8.
8. Mustering Out.
   - A. Roll for cash and benefits.
