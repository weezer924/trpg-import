## Basic Combat Concept

> PDF p.30

As adventurers journey through the cities and the wilds of the worlds they visit, they will encounter both men and beasts. At times, the only way such encounters can be dealt with is by force.

This combat system covers situations where a party of adventurers encounters a party of people or beasts, and violence is offered by either side. The actual circumstances of each encounter are governed by the referee, in accordance with the encounter provisions of Book 3.

Combat is based on **successive attacks** by each character involved — blows (brawling), swings (blade weapons), and shots (firearms). A **basic throw of 8+** is required in every case to hit, subject to Die Modifiers (DMs) for:

- Range between parties
- Attacker and defender expertise in their weapons
- Types of weapons used
- Surprise
- Other factors

If a hit is obtained, **wounds are inflicted based on the type of weapon** which made the hit.

**Each combat round lasts 15 seconds.** Combat continues until one party is vanquished, flees, dies, or surrenders.

## Combat Procedure

> PDF p.30

Step 1 sets up the encounter. Step 2 repeats as necessary. Step 3 resolves final details and ends the encounter.

1. **Determine facts of the encounter.**
   - A. Which party has surprise?
   - B. Initial encounter range?
   - C. Escape or avoidance?
2. **Begin combat round.**
   - A. Individual movement status.
   - B. Individual targets and attacks.
     1. Attacker's DMs.
     2. Defender's DMs.
   - C. If attack succeeds, determine wounds inflicted at end of the round.
   - D. Roll for morale if unit has taken 25% casualties.
   - E. Begin new round (go to 2).
3. **When combat ends**, attend to the wounded and regroup forces.

## Surprise

> PDF p.30–31

Surprise is possible for either party, and the element of surprise gives an advantage both in attacking and in avoiding the enemy.

**Resolution.** Each party rolls **1D**. If one party's die roll is **3 or more greater** than the other, the higher-rolling party has achieved surprise. DMs are allowed for each party depending on expertise and situation (see Surprise DMs). Only one party can achieve surprise, and it is possible that neither party will. If not achieved, both parties are aware of each other at encounter range.

A party with the element of surprise may:

- Elect to **avoid contact** with the other party (see Escape and Avoidance); or
- **Attack with surprise swings, blows, and shots** until surprise is lost.

**Surprise blows** are defined in the Endurance rule (see *Effects of Characteristics*). Surprise is lost when a member of the other party gives the alarm in some manner:

- All **unsilenced** shots alert the enemy. Silenced pistols, laser weapons, and all guns in vacuum make **no noise** when fired.
- Any character who is hit but not rendered unconscious makes sufficient noise (probably a scream) to raise the alarm.
- If no alarm is raised this way, there is a chance (**throw 9+**) that a comrade in the defending party will see the person fall and give the alarm.

Surprise continues until lost, and may thus continue indefinitely. Once lost, normal combat begins.

Because all attacks are **simultaneous**, all members of the attacking party may each make one attack as a surprise if the party has surprise. If surprise is not lost, each member may make another surprise attack. This continues until surprise is lost. Everyone completes their surprise attacks even if one of them results in the loss of surprise.

### Escape and Avoidance

> PDF p.31–32

Encountering parties may attempt to avoid contact with, or escape from, an encounter.

- A party which has achieved surprise may **always** avoid an encounter by so stating.
- NPC parties that have surprise **and are outnumbered** will avoid an encounter on a throw of **7+** (no DMs).
- If two parties encounter **without surprise**, either may attempt to escape immediately (before any combat or contact occurs). Throw **9+** to escape. DM based on range: **−1 if close or short range**, +1 if medium, +2 if long, +3 if very long.
- An NPC party will attempt to escape at the referee's option based on the situation. Animals operate under different rules (Book 3).

Once contact or combat begins, a party may leave the field of battle **only through movement**.

## Movement

> PDF p.32–33

For movement, distances are measured in **range bands**, each representing approximately **25 meters**.

### Range Bands

| Range       | Bands       |
|-------------|-------------|
| Close, Short| 0 (same band, markers touching for Close) |
| Medium      | 1 to 2      |
| Long        | 3 to 10     |
| Very Long   | 11 to 20    |

A character 4 range bands from another is at **long range** with respect to the other.

It is suggested that ranges be mapped on a **line grid** (ordinary lined paper works). Each line band = one range band. At encounter start, place markers in bands separated by the encounter distance. In subsequent rounds, characters may move to close or open the range.

- To indicate two characters at **close range**, place their markers touching.
- All other characters in the same range band are at **short range**.
- Characters may move **one band per combat round walking**, or **two if running**.
- Animals may move faster (see Book 3).

The referee may expand to a square or hex grid for more detailed maneuver.

### Movement Statuses

Before each combat round, each character must state a movement status. Four possibilities:

- **Evade.** At any range. The person may **not attack** (no swings, blows, or shots) and may not use his weapon to parry or block. He receives an advantageous **defensive DM** based on range from the attacker:
  - −1 at short or close range
  - −2 at medium range
  - −4 at long or very long range
- **Close Range.** Move closer to the enemy. Walk 1 band, or run ~double speed. **Running counts as a combat blow** (reducing endurance total, and prohibiting an attack that round — see Endurance). Moving from short to close counts as moving 1 range band.
- **Open Range.** Move away. Same rules as closing — however, he may move from close range to one band away in one move without running.
- **Stand.** Do not move this round.

All movement is performed **simultaneously**. Each member decides his own status. Any character who moves **more than 20 bands away** from the nearest enemy is out of range and has escaped.

## Combat Resolution

> PDF p.33–34

Combat is resolved in a series of attacks represented by dice throws. A combat round is a series in which each participating individual makes one throw. Rounds continue until the battle is resolved by rout, death, or surrender.

### Basic Required Throw

During each combat round, each combatant selects a target from the opposing party and makes a **basic throw of 8+** to hit. If successful, wounds are determined.

All DMs to the basic throw are **cumulative** — added together and then applied. Adding negative DMs is subtracting (`3 + 1 = 4`; `3 + −1 = 2`; `−3 + −1 = −4`).

The basic throw is subject to a variety of DMs:

- **Armor/weapon relation** — see [Weapons Matrix](#weapons-matrix-weapon--armor)
- **Range** — see [Range Matrix](#range-matrix)
- **Strength of attack** — combat or weakened blows (see Weapons Table)
- **Movement status** — evade DMs, running penalties
- **Attacker and defender expertise**
- **Other factors** — surprise, terrain, accessories, etc.

Roll 2D, modify by accumulated DMs. If the modified result is **8 or greater**, the attack hits. Consult the wound column on the Range Matrix for damage.

### Wounding and Death

The wound column indicates damage inflicted by a hit, depending on the **type of weapon** (not the strength of character or blow). `D` = die/dice; `3D` = 3 six-sided dice rolled for damage.

Wound points are applied to the target's **Strength, Dexterity, and Endurance** on a **temporary** basis. Each die rolled (e.g., each of the two dice in a `2D` result) is taken as a single wound or group of hits, and must be applied to a single characteristic.

**First blood.** The **first wound** received by any character may stun or daze him, and is handled differently:

> The first wound is applied to one of the three physical characteristics (Str, Dex, or End) determined **randomly**. First blood may immediately incapacitate or even kill.

**Unconsciousness and death.**

| Characteristics at 0 | Status                 |
|:--------------------:|------------------------|
| **1**                | Unconscious            |
| **2**                | Seriously wounded (also unconscious) |
| **3**                | Dead                   |

Once a characteristic is reduced to zero, **further points may not be applied to it** — they must be applied to other (non-zero) characteristics.

**Recovery.**

- Unconscious (1 stat at 0): regain consciousness after **10 minutes (40 combat rounds)**. All characteristics temporarily at the value **halfway between full strength and the wounded level**. Example: Str 8 → wounded to Str 4 → regains consciousness at Str 6, and remains so until recovered. Round fractions **against** the character. Return to full strength requires medical attention (**medical kit + Medical-1**) OR **three days of rest**.
- Seriously wounded (2 stats at 0): regain consciousness after **3 hours**. Characteristics remain at the wounded level (or 1, whichever is higher). Recovery requires **medical facility + Medical-3**. Recovery without medical attention is **not possible**.

## The Effects of Characteristics

> PDF p.34–35

Personal characteristics affect combat as sources of DMs based on weapons and battle duration.

### Strength

The efficiency of various **blows and swings** (but not shots) depends on personal strength. The Weapons Table indicates both a **required** and an **advantageous** strength level for all blade and brawling weapons.

- Below required: apply the negative DM from the table.
- At or above advantageous: apply the positive DM from the table.

Example: character with Str 5 using a cutlass (required 7) → DM **−2**. Character with Str 11+ using a cutlass (advantageous 11) → DM **+2**.

### Dexterity

The accuracy of **shots** depends on the firer's dexterity. The Weapons Table lists required and advantageous dexterity levels for guns, with positive and negative DMs.

### Endurance

The **number of blows and swings** (and their impact) depends on endurance. All blows and swings are classed into four types:

- **Surprise** — completely unrestricted as long as surprise is retained (but still only one per combat round).
- **Combat** — the ordinary attacks after surprise is lost. A character may make a number of combat blows/swings **not exceeding his endurance** (End 9 = 9 combat blows). Once used up, no more combat blows until he has **rested at least 30 minutes**.
- **Weakened** — attacks after combat allowance is exhausted. Each weakened blow/swing is subject to the negative DM in the Weapons Table. Unlimited number allowed. A character may **elect** to make any blow weakened (to conserve combat allowance).
- **Special** — allowed in situations where strength is not a factor (e.g., against an unconscious opponent). Not weakened, no endurance cost.

**Only one blow or swing per combat round.** The endurance allowance is set at the **beginning of the encounter** — wounds suffered during the encounter do not reduce it, but pre-encounter wounds that reduced endurance do. The total allowance applies to all such attacks regardless of whether the character shifts from brawling to blade combat and back.

**Gun combat is NOT affected by endurance.**

## Expertise

> PDF p.35

A character's expertise or skill in a weapon can affect both attack and defense.

### Attacking

Expertise level in a specific weapon = **positive DM on the basic throw to hit**. If the character changes weapons, the new weapon's expertise is used.

### Parrying

A character may use his brawling or blade weapon expertise as a **negative DM on the enemy's throw** when engaged in brawling or blade combat (both using such weapons). The character is considered to be blocking or parrying the enemy's blow.

- Only **blows or swings** may be blocked, not shots.
- A **long gun** (rifle or carbine, but **not pistol**) may be used to parry — treated as a brawling weapon (cudgel).
- A weapon may be used **both to attack and parry** in the same round.

### Untrained Weapon Usage

Any character using a weapon in which he has **no training** (i.e., not even level-0) is subject to:

- Attacking: **DM −5**
- Defending: **DM +3** (against, effectively negative to attacker)

All **player-characters automatically have expertise of 0** in all weapons shown in this book. This zero value is sufficient to avoid the no-expertise penalty, but does not provide a positive DM.

## Weight

> PDF p.36

Characters are restricted in total weight carried; encumbrance imposes negative DMs.

| 装载类型        | 最大重量（kg）     | 代价                                                  |
|-----------------|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| **Normal Load** | Str (kg)           | No penalty.                                           |
| **Double Load** | 2× Str (kg)        | Str, Dex, End **each −1** until at least some shed.   |
| **Triple Load** | 3× Str (kg)        | Str, Dex, End **each −2**. Only military unit members can carry triple load. |

Clothing, personal armor, holsters, scabbards, and belts **are not counted**. Tools, communicators, instruments, rations, calculators (Book 3) count toward load.

Example: character UPP `788953` carrying 12 kg of load is treated as `677953` until at least 5 kg have been shed.

### Different Gravity

A world of size 8 has normal gravity. The formula:

$$\text{additional carry \% allowed} = (8 - \text{World Size}) \times 12.5\%$$

- World size 4: (8 − 4) × 12.5% = **+50%** additional load.
- World size 10: (8 − 10) × 12.5% = **−25%** allowable load.

World sizes are explained in Book 3. Different densities are possible via a density constant **K** (referee-set, Book 2 notes). When K is in use, multiply world size by K to get true gravity factor for load calculation.

## Morale

> PDF p.36

A party which sustains casualties will ultimately break or rout if not victorious.

When **25% of a party** is unconscious or killed, the party must begin making **morale throws**. For an average party, **7+** to stand (not break and run). Valiant parties may have a lower throw.

| DM 条件                            | DM  |
|------------------------------------|----:|
| Party is a military unit           | +1  |
| Leader present (leader skill)      | +1  |
| Leader has any tactical skill      | +1  |
| Leader killed (2+ rounds, until new leader) | −2 |
| Casualties exceed 50%              | −2  |

### Guns

Law levels (Book 3) may restrict possession, use, or importation of some or all guns. **Pistols** are effective at all but the longest ranges; **other guns** are effective at all ranges.

#### Body Pistol (300 g loaded; Cr 520; TL 7)

Small, non-metallic semi-automatic, designed to evade weapon detectors. Fires 5 g projectiles at 500–600 m/s. 6-round magazine in the handle. **Ammunition not interchangeable** with any other gun. Can be fitted with a silencer.

- Length: 100 mm
- Unloaded: 250 g (loaded magazine: 50 g)
- Base price: Cr 500 (loaded magazine: Cr 20)

#### Automatic Pistol (1000 g loaded; Cr 210; TL 5)

Basic repeating handgun. Fires 9 mm, 10 g bullets at 400–500 m/s. 15-round magazine in handle. **Ammunition interchangeable with SMG** (magazines are not). Can be fitted with silencer and detachable shoulder stock.

- Length: 170 mm
- Unloaded: 750 g (loaded magazine: 250 g)
- Base price: Cr 200 (loaded magazine: Cr 10)

#### Revolver (1000 g loaded; Cr 155; TL 4)

Older-variety handgun. Fires 9 mm bullets, characteristics similar to auto pistol (**not interchangeable**). No magazine — 6 cartridges loaded individually. **Reload: 2 combat rounds (1 round if not evading).** Can fit a detachable shoulder stock, but **not a silencer**.

- Length: 200 mm (some shorter/longer)
- Unloaded: 900 g (6 cartridges: 100 g)
- Base price: Cr 150 (6 cartridges: Cr 5)

#### Carbine (3125 g loaded; Cr 210; TL 5)

Short rifle firing 6 mm, 5 g bullets at 900 m/s. 10-round magazine under the barrel ahead of the trigger guard. **Ammunition not interchangeable** with any other type. Sling, telescopic or electronic sights, folding stock all possible.

- Length: 750 mm
- Unloaded: 3000 g (loaded magazine: 125 g)
- Base price: Cr 200 (loaded magazine: Cr 10)

#### Rifle (4500 g loaded; Cr 220; TL 5)

Standard military arm. Fires 7 mm, 10 g bullets at ~900 m/s. Standard provisions for bayonet + telescopic sights + shoulder sling. 20-round magazine at front of trigger guard. **Ammunition interchangeable with auto rifle**; magazines interchangeable and same weight. Telescopic/electronic sights, folding stock possible.

- Length: 1000 mm
- Unloaded: 4000 g (loaded magazine: 500 g)
- Base price: Cr 200 (loaded magazine: Cr 20)

#### Automatic Rifle (5500 g loaded; Cr 1020; TL 6)

Highly refined version of the rifle; full auto + semi-auto. Normally fires **bursts of 4 bullets per pull**. May switch to semi-auto at end of a combat round after all firing — treated as a rifle until switched back. Ammunition and magazines identical to rifle. Equipped with sling, bipod, muzzle brake.

- Belt-fed versions (100-round belts) exist — **not usable in rifles**. Belt cost = 6 loaded magazines; belt weight = 2.5 kg. **Belt reload = 3 combat rounds.**
- Length: 1000 mm
- Unloaded: 5000 g (loaded magazine: 500 g)
- Base price: Cr 1000 (loaded magazine: Cr 20; 100-round belt: Cr 120)

#### Shotgun (4.5 kg loaded; Cr 160; TL 4)

Basic weapon for maximum shock effect without regard to accuracy. 18 mm barrel. Fires shells containing either six 7 mm bullets, or 130 × 3 mm pellets (total 30 g) at ~350 m/s. Cylindrical 10-shell magazine inserted under and parallel to the barrel. **Reload = 2 combat rounds** (replacing the cylindrical magazine). Magazines are ~350 × 20 mm and clumsy to carry. Sling + optional folding stock.

- Length: 1000 mm
- Unloaded: 3750 g (loaded magazine: 750 g)
- Base price: Cr 150 (loaded magazine: Cr 10)

#### Submachinegun (3000 g loaded; Cr 520; TL 5)

Small automatic firing pistol ammo. 30-round magazines forward of trigger guard or in pistol grip (depending on design). Fires **4 rounds per pull**. **Ammunition interchangeable with auto pistol** (magazines are not). Most have slings.

- Length: 450 mm
- Unloaded: 2500 g (loaded magazine: 500 g)
- Base price: Cr 500 (loaded magazine: Cr 20)

#### Laser Carbine (8000 g including power pack; Cr 3500; TL 8)

Lightweight laser weapon firing 2 mm energy beams via integrated optic sights, powered by a **backpack battery**. **50 shots per power pack**; recharge requires **8+ hours at a high-energy source**. Heavy-duty cable connects carbine and pack.

- Length: 800 mm
- Carbine weight: 5000 g; power pack: 3000 g
- Base price: Cr 2500 (extra power pack: Cr 1000)
- Commercial recharge cost: Cr 200

#### Laser Rifle (10 kg including power pack; Cr 5000; TL 9)

Standard high-energy weapon. Heavier than carbine, capable of longer sustained action, sturdier. **100 shots per power pack**. **Power packs are NOT interchangeable** between laser carbine and laser rifle.

- Length: 1000 mm
- Rifle weight: 6000 g; power pack: 4000 g
- Base price: Cr 3500 (extra power pack: Cr 1500)
- Commercial recharge cost: Cr 300

### Armor and Protection

Personal armor weight is assumed to be part of the character's **clothing load** and is **not affected by the weight rule**. Only **one form of personal armor may be worn**, *except* that **Reflec may always be worn under other clothing or armor**.

## Special Considerations

> PDF p.42

### Drawing

Weapons are usually **holstered or slung** unless stated otherwise. Using a holstered or slung weapon in a combat round = **DM −3** when drawing.

When two or more people **draw against each other** (assuming surprise is not a factor), each rolls **2D + Dexterity**; highest modified throw achieves surprise for the first shot.

### Minor Accessories

Holsters, magazine carriers, belts, scabbards, cleaning kits, etc., are **10–20%** of the base weapon price. Effectively no weight (part of personal clothing). **Shoulder holsters** conceal pistols in public; otherwise pistols are carried in **hip holsters**.

### Throwing Blades

Daggers, blades, and bayonets may be thrown at a target at **short range**.

- Throw **18+** to hit.
- DM: +Dexterity, +Blade skill, −target's evasion DM if evading.
- Wound: **2D** on hit.
- Retrieval = 1 combat round at close range with the target.

**Polearms** (spears, pikes, halberds) may be thrown by the same procedure, but the thrower must have **Strength ≥ 3 × weapon weight in kg**. Throwing a blade or polearm **counts as a combat blow or swing** (Endurance cost).

### Weapon Length Effects

**Polearms** (spear, halberd, pike) and similar long weapons use the **Short range modifier only on the first combat round** at short range. **Thereafter, use the Close range modifier** (even if the actual range remains Short). This reflects the difficulty of wielding a long weapon when the opponent has closed the distance.

### Reloading

Technically, guns reload themselves after each shot. However, when the magazine capacity is exhausted, the shooter must reload with a fully loaded magazine.

- **Default:** Reloading with a full magazine takes **1 combat round**, during which the shooter is treated as **evading**.
- **Revolvers** (no magazine): **2 combat rounds** to reload, or **1 round** if not simultaneously evading.
- Empty magazines are reusable. Ammunition may be purchased at **~half the price** of a full magazine. The tedium of reloading empty magazines means it must be done at leisure, not in combat — several minutes per magazine.
- **Laser carbines and laser rifles** do not use cartridges; their power packs must be **recharged** when exhausted. Replace the power pack to return the weapon to service. Recharging a spent pack requires **~1 hour at a high-energy power source**. Commercial recharge: **Cr 200** (laser carbine) or **Cr 300** (laser rifle). Generally, power packs can be recharged at a ship's power plant at no cost.

### Armor Layering

With the exception of **Reflec**, no armor may be worn with another type of armor. **Reflec may be worn in conjunction with another armor type** — if the wearer is attacked, **the better type of armor provides the DM**.

### Darkness and Night

Poor lighting restricts the ability of an individual to see and attack.

- **Total darkness** restricts engagements to **close and short** range. Gun attacks at **greater than short range**: DM **−9**.
- **Partial darkness** (moonlit night, distant illumination, weak light sources): visibility reduced to **medium range**. Gun attacks: DM **−6**.
- **Electronic sights eliminate** negative DMs due to darkness and poor lighting.

### Cover and Concealment

- **Cover** = any solid object between attacker and defender capable of protecting the defender from a weapon attack (wall, rock, heavy bulkhead). An individual **under cover cannot be attacked**.
- **Concealment** = any object that prevents viewing or sighting the defender. **Cover may also be concealment, but concealment is not necessarily cover.** An individual in concealment cannot be attacked unless the attacker has some reason to shoot into the area. Targets may be partially concealed by walls, objects, atmospheric conditions, or darkness. If **fully concealed**, a target cannot be attacked.

**Attacking from cover/concealment:**

- From **cover**: attacker becomes visible and may themselves be attacked; retains partial cover for defending **DM −4**.
- From **concealment**: attacker provides reason to believe they are present and may be attacked; retains partial concealment for defending **DM −1**.

### Zero Gravity

Virtually all weapons have recoil (**except** laser carbines and laser rifles). In a zero-G environment, this recoil can disorient or render helpless individuals not trained to compensate for it.

When fighting in zero-G, any individual has a chance of **losing control** of their movement/position each combat round. **Throw 10+ to avoid losing control**.

**Zero Gravity DMs:**

| 条件                          | DM  |
|-------------------------------|----:|
| If firing a weapon            | −4  |
| If using a handhold           | +5  |
| If performing a swing or blow | −6  |
| If Dexterity 9+               | +2  |
| If Dexterity 11+              | +2  |

(Dex 9+ and Dex 11+ DMs are cumulative — a character with Dex 11+ gets **+4** total.)

### Full Automatic Fire

Submachineguns and automatic rifles fire **four-round bursts** instead of single shots. The higher ammunition usage produces the hit probabilities shown on the table (**auto rifle uses the rifle row when firing single shots**). Automatic fire allows:

- **Rolling to hit twice** against the same target; and
- The **group hit rule** against companions (see below).

### Group Hits by Automatic Fire

Regardless of the designated target, **non-evading individuals adjacent** to the target are also attacked by the burst.

- **Max 2 adjacent targets.**
- Each attacked with a to-hit roll at **DM −3** (plus all other appropriate DMs).

### Group Hits by Shotguns

Each shotgun shot may attack **up to 3 individuals adjacent to the original target**, provided they are in a group (herd, pack, band) and are each human-sized or smaller. Additionally, firing at **flying targets** (winged animals, flying vehicles) within range allows a **DM +2**.

### Coup De Grace

Any gun or blade may be used to administer a coup de grace and kill an unconscious or unstruggling individual at **close range in one combat round** if stated.

- Ammunition is expended.
- **No die rolls necessary.**
- With hands or brawling weapons (using special blows), **die rolls ARE required**.

## Quick Reference

> PDF p.48. Condensed tables for use in play.

### Combat Procedure (short)

1. Determine if either party has the element of surprise.
2. Determine the initial range which separates the parties.
3. Determine if escape or avoidance for either party is possible or warranted.
4. Resolve actual combat using a series of combat throws.
   - A. Each character indicates his or her movement status.
   - B. Each character indicates his or her attack and target.

### Weight Limitations (summary)

- **Normal Load:** may not exceed personal Strength in kg.
- **Double Load:** encumbered; twice personal Strength in kg; Str, Dex, End **−1** each.
- **Triple Load:** encumbered; triple personal Strength in kg; Str, Dex, End **−2** each. Military unit members only.
